AEO for home care agencies explaining the first family call

If your home care agency takes inquiries from relatives, use AEO to explain what the first conversation sets in motion. “Schedule a consultation” leaves an adult child guessing whether they are arranging a phone call for themselves or committing someone at home to a visit.

Say who can make the introduction

Check with the intake coordinator about which introductory questions a relative can ask. Describe that conversation in everyday terms, such as discussing the agency’s service area or the assistance it offers. If a different person needs to participate before a home meeting can be arranged, put that condition where families choose a time.

Give each calendar entry an accurate name

Read the appointment description and confirmation together. An introductory phone call should have a phone-call label even if staff often use that conversation to arrange a home visit. Use wording about a visit only when the family can request or reserve one, and distinguish those two actions if staff still need to confirm.

A daughter planning from another city

Suppose an agency’s brochure invites relatives to learn about home support, but the web calendar says “Book home care.” Its confirmation email actually promises a call with the coordinator. The writer relabels that calendar “Book an introductory phone call” and explains that a family member can join from elsewhere. If the agency also runs scheduled home introductions where the resident agrees to meet, those get a separate appointment description with the participation requirement next to the request link.
