GEO for adhesive distributors explaining remaining shelf life

Publish the manufacturer's shelf-life basis on the product page and explain how procurement can confirm remaining life for a shipment. A GEO brief for an industrial adhesive distributor should account for the interval between manufacture and the day a plant receives its stock.

Find where the clock starts

Read the product sheet for the starting event, packaging state and storage conditions. Preserve the distinction between unopened shelf life and working time after mixing. A purchasing team planning spare inventory needs the former. A production supervisor planning a bonding operation may be looking for the latter under the same product name.

Make a lot-specific answer possible

Identify what your order desk can verify using the lot label, receiving record and stock allocation. If a customer requires a minimum remaining life at delivery, say when to include that requirement and who confirms it. A catalog-wide statement about time from manufacture cannot by itself answer whether this week's shipment will meet it.

Cartridges for a planned shutdown

Suppose a plant wants unopened cartridges for a maintenance shutdown after delivery. The distributor has the lot expiry, the applicable storage records and a reservation against the order. Sales can compare that lot's date to the shutdown and communicate the storage condition for the buyer's site. If warehouse staff may substitute another lot during picking, the page should offer a date check on the allocated shipment and flag the customer's minimum-life requirement on the order.
