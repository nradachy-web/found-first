AEO for hardware retailers explaining door lever handing

For a hardware retailer, AEO can help shoppers answer the question behind a left-hand or right-hand selector. Use the specific lever maker's diagram to establish where the shopper should stand, what they should observe and which option matches that view.

Put the viewing position before the option name

A person looking from the hall sees hinges on the opposite side from someone looking from inside the room. If the diagram distinguishes door swing as well as hinge position, preserve both observations in the answer. Place the diagram and its text beside the handed options so a shopper can use them while making the selection.

Check what the selector actually changes

A single product family may include fixed-handed and reversible mechanisms. Confirm which mechanism ships under the selected code. Give a shopper choosing a reversible set any manufacturer-stated limits that affect selection, such as a trim configuration supplied in only one orientation.

A photograph taken from the hallway

Suppose a retailer has a supplier diagram that defines the view from the corridor for a particular inward-swinging lever set. A shopper's corridor photo matches the hinge position and swing in the diagram's left-hand row. The listing can direct them to that row and its matching order code. If their photo was taken from inside the room, ask for the corridor view before naming a hand. If they choose the documented reversible version, direct them to its single order code.
