Connection is not a complete description of the product

A buyer exploring integration software may need to move data, coordinate processes or connect applications in a particular way. Those needs can overlap without being identical. A website that presents every connector as proof of complete support can leave the actual product role difficult to evaluate.

For a vendor strongest in a defined kind of integration, the public explanation should make that distinction visible. A broader platform still needs a coherent account of how its capabilities relate. Found First helps turn approved product scope into useful buyer information rather than imply that one integration label covers every technical requirement.

Keep connector names attached to their actual capabilities

The presence of an application name does not establish every action, object or workflow a buyer might expect. Current connector scope can be decisive in a real evaluation. The public story should give a prospective customer a clear route to the information needed to confirm its requirements.

We help preserve those boundaries while making the commercial value understandable. Marketing can explain why a connection is relevant without publishing a detailed implementation recipe. The important distinction is between a supported relationship and an unsupported assumption that every possible use of the connected systems is available.

Before you decide.

Can we promote every application listed in our connector catalog?+

Current supported connections can be represented, but their actual scope should remain clear. An application name does not prove every workflow is available. Found First helps explain the commercial relevance without expanding the claim beyond the functionality your team has verified.

Is this the same as marketing a data warehouse?+

Not necessarily. The product's actual role needs to be explicit. Data storage, movement, orchestration and application integration can involve different buying decisions. The service should make your platform understandable for the role it truly plays rather than borrow adjacent category language indiscriminately.

Do we need to publish implementation recipes?+

No. Public content can explain the problem, supported scope and product relationship without exposing a complete technical procedure. The goal is to help a suitable buyer recognize fit and reach the documentation or conversation appropriate to a deeper evaluation.
