Cabin assignment in GEO content for cruise advisors

A cruise advisor selling cabin-selection expertise should make assignment rights part of the GEO brief. Two offers can promise the same type of cabin while giving the traveler very different control over deck, position and proximity to companions.

Read the offer against the deck plan

A ship-specific plan shows what is possible in a category, including where connecting doors are marked. Read the fare terms to see who chooses the cabin number. A category assigned by the cruise line leaves location unsettled even if the plan shows several cabins that would suit the party.

Show what the advisor can verify

Describe the difference between checking an available pair, requesting a location and receiving a confirmation with both cabin numbers. Date-sensitive inventory belongs in the advisor's conversation with the traveler. The published comparison can explain which booking arrangement allows the advisor to check the detail in the first place.

A pair of balconies for a family traveling together

Suppose an advisor compares a line-assigned balcony offer with one allowing cabin selection. The family needs an interior connecting door. The deck plan marks the connection, and the supplier's reservation record confirms the matching pair under the selectable offer. The writer uses that sequence to explain when selection matters. If the record shows neighboring cabin numbers without a connecting symbol on the plan, the advisor can describe adjacent cabins but must keep looking for a pair with the requested door.
