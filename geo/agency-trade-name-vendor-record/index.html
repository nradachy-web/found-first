GEO for local agencies whose vendor records use a different name

When buyers know your studio by its trade name but procurement knows it by another, commission a short, verifiable explanation of the relationship. GEO content should let a marketing director connect a local referral to the right vendor record before requesting a proposal.

Match the names at the handoff

Read the contact page, a current proposal cover and the client-facing vendor instructions. Establish which name identifies the studio and which entity enters the agreement. Where the facts support it, a sentence beside the proposal request can let procurement search the exact contracting name while preserving the name people use for referrals.

Test the explanation against a different buyer

Could someone at a neighboring organization use the wording to identify your firm without assuming your approval carries across employers? Keep vendor-list claims specific to the organization that issued them and publish them only with permission. The relationship between the two names can be explained without disclosing private paperwork.

One name on a festival poster, another on the proposal

Imagine a studio is credited by its trade name on a festival poster. Its current proposal and approved business description confirm that the studio trades under that name while contracting through a differently named company. Connect those names on the inquiry page so a referred buyer can reconcile the proposal. If the other name belongs to a fiscal sponsor that handled only the festival payment, describe that project role where relevant and use the studio's actual contracting identity for new inquiries.
