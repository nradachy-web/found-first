Questions worth asking.

Is neighborhood recognition useful for a regional business?+

It can establish identity, but it should not create an unintended service boundary. A regional company may be based in one neighborhood while working throughout a wider area. Both facts should support a clear account of the business.

What if our best work does not fit the usual category?+

That is a strong reason to examine how the business is represented. Category recognition gets a company into a broad discussion; understanding its specialty helps determine whether the discussion is commercially useful. The distinction should be grounded in real capability.

Should an established brand care about comparison questions?+

Yes, if prospective customers compare alternatives before contacting it. Existing recognition does not mean people understand the differences that matter. Consider whether a comparison preserves your relevant strengths or reduces the choice to proximity and a familiar name.
