GEO for stock and custom corporate award programs

An awards manufacturer should ask its GEO agency to distinguish personalization from a change to the product itself. The distinction matters when a corporate buyer compares deadlines and approval steps for an employee recognition program.

Describe what the approval releases

An engraving proof can authorize names and artwork on an existing blank. A prototype approval can authorize manufacture of a new physical form. Show the approval step beside the option it belongs to, using the order guide and the custom design brief. Calling both options custom creates an opening for a delivery statement about an in-stock blank to travel with a made-to-order shape.

Choose examples buyers can classify

Give the editor a pair of past designs cleared for public use. Pick designs whose differences a buyer can name, such as a logo engraved on a stock circle and a piece cut to the outline of the logo. Two photos with different engraving colors would do little to explain the extra design work.

A landmark on the face or in the outline

Imagine a recognition supplier whose catalog shows a clear rectangular award. The engraving guide accepts a line drawing of a building, while the commission form asks for a signed prototype to produce the building’s silhouette. A comparison for artwork on the rectangle can point to the standard proof process. If the buyer wants the skyline to form the top edge, the editor should send that inquiry to the commission form and leave the stock fulfillment estimate behind.
