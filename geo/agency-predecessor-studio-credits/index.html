Which predecessor studio credits belong in a local agency’s GEO content?

Credit older campaigns to the business and people who did the work. If your agency occupies a familiar local studio, a GEO project should help a buyer distinguish the address's history from your team's experience and the business they can commission today.

Look beyond the studio door

Compare the original project credits with the current team's roles and any approved account of the studio transition. An old production program can document who directed a shoot. A photograph of the same editing room establishes where it happened. Each supports a different claim about the current agency.

Decide where the credit belongs

When the only verified connection is an individual's work, use a precise role on that person's bio, subject to permission to publish. A current agency case study needs evidence that the firm can claim the engagement and accurately describe the scope. A recognizable predecessor name alone leaves that question open.

A festival film shot at the same address

Suppose a new agency rents a former film studio. A festival program credits the old studio as producer and the new agency's founder as editor. The agency can describe the founder's editing credit on her bio and introduce its present services on the studio page. If the records instead document a trading-name change by the same agency, and its portfolio permission still covers the film, keep the project in the agency portfolio with its original production credit and a brief name history.
