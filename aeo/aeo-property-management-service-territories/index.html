AEO for leasing and full management in different territories

Give each owner service its own geographic answer when your property management firm will place a tenant farther afield than it will manage a building. A single territory map can make a landlord assume the team that handles leasing will also oversee repairs after move-in.

Identify the responsibility that changes with distance

Compare the leasing agreement with the ongoing management agreement. Find the duties that depend on field coverage, such as arranging access for an inspection or meeting a maintenance vendor. An owner can then see why two properties might qualify for the same tenant placement service but receive different answers about ongoing management.

Make the owner choose an inquiry they can use

On the outer-area page, identify which agreement an owner can ask about before the proposal form. A landlord looking for someone to take over an occupied building gains little from a leasing consultation if the firm will have no responsibility after tenant placement.

One town appears on only the leasing map

Picture a firm whose leasing coordinator can show homes across the county. Its maintenance roster stops at the northern town line. An owner north of that line asks for rent collection and repair coordination on an occupied house. The writer should explain the management boundary and avoid funneling that owner into a tenant placement proposal. If the same owner has a vacant house and wants to handle repairs personally after move-in, the leasing agreement and showing calendar support a placement inquiry for that exact address.
