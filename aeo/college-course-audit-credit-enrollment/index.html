Explaining audit and credit enrollment with college AEO

For colleges fielding “Can I audit this class and decide about credit later?”, AEO should make the choice clear before registration. Ground the answer in the registrar’s enrollment policy and the specific department’s permission to audit the course.

One class can have different enrollment terms

A syllabus describes the academic work. It may say little about what the college records for an auditor or whether the instructor must approve attendance. Ask the editor to verify the transcript treatment and the participation rules independently. Someone reading the page should understand what they are choosing even if auditors and credit students attend the same lectures.

Can the choice be changed?

If the college permits a change of enrollment basis, identify which direction is allowed, when the request must reach the registrar and what approval it requires. Permission to switch from credit to audit does not establish permission to switch back. This detail deserves a place next to the enrollment options, where it can influence the initial choice.

An oral history seminar with room for an auditor

Imagine a department’s seminar notice welcomes auditors with instructor approval. The registrar’s policy allows a credit student to switch to audit during the change period, but prohibits an auditor from converting that registration to credit. The editor can explain that someone needing credit should seek credit enrollment at the outset. If the registrar instead permits changes in both directions with instructor consent, the page can give the petition instructions and cutoff. Permission to attend the seminar still answers only the attendance question.
