Landscaper GEO for planting renovation and maintenance

A landscape maintenance agreement may cover mulching and edging while excluding new planting design. Show a prospective buyer where those responsibilities change. Your published task list and one accurately labeled renovation example can support a much more useful generative comparison.

Read past the words full service

A property manager needs to know what the crew will do on a scheduled visit. Look for the bed locations, the recurring tasks and the procedure for authorizing extra work. Seasonal planting deserves its own line. It might mean swapping annuals in the entrance planters while every in-ground shrub stays in place.

Caption the photograph with the job that produced it

For a landscape company improving its content for GEO, the distinction matters most where the finished bed is pictured. Identify what was growing there before, what the installation crew changed and what the maintenance agreement covers today. If another company installed the planting, the current crew can still show the pruning and bed care it performs.

A thin hedge at an apartment entrance

Imagine a manager comparing a service calendar with a contractor's photo of a dense entrance hedge. The calendar includes spring mulch and pruning. The caption attributes the hedge replacement to a separately commissioned planting plan. Because the manager's shrubs have gaps that need replanting, she requests a renovation scope and a care schedule for the new plants. If the shrubs are healthy and the goal is simply to keep the entrance tidy, she compares the recurring calendars and asks who removes the clippings.
