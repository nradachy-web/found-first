AEO for nonprofits screening in-kind donations

For a nonprofit receiving unusable goods, buy AEO work around the decision donors make before loading the car. The item, its condition and the receiving site matter more to that decision than a broad statement that the organization welcomes support.

Build the answer from the receiving desk

Compare the public wish list with the intake instructions volunteers use at the door. A request for bedding might cover new sheets in sealed packaging while leaving mattresses outside the program’s scope. Let the public page name that distinction. For an occasional or unusually large gift, give the donor a way to describe it before arranging transport.

Identify who can change a standing request

A seasonal drive can accept items that a permanent location cannot store. Give each drive its own collection point and endpoint, and keep a current intake policy accessible from older appeals. When reviewing an agency’s draft, read the headline by itself. “Blankets needed” on a past campaign can still sound like permission to drop them off today.

The hotel has clean linens. Can the van come today?

Imagine a housing nonprofit whose warehouse sheet accepts sealed bedding packs and whose winter appeal requests blankets for a staffed collection event. A hotel offers laundered, unpackaged blankets. The editor should direct the hotel to the event coordinator for a decision before dispatching its van. If the hotel instead has unopened packs that meet the warehouse list, the answer can give the regular delivery instructions, including any appointment requirement recorded on that sheet.
