Using seating plans in event venue GEO comparisons

For a venue selling both dinners and presentations, buy GEO content that compares the rooms in the arrangement a planner intends to use. A pair of annotated seating plans can answer a shortlist question that a ballroom portrait leaves open.

Specify what occupies the floor

Name the seating style and show the stage, screen, control desk and service routes included in that drawing. Keep any guest count with the configuration from which the venue derived it. A standing reception figure tells a planner little about a plated dinner with a presentation between courses.

Test the seat at the edge

A floor plan can establish that a column lies between a table and a screen. A labeled photograph from that table gives the planner a better basis for judging the interruption. Where staff have only reviewed the center of the room, leave the view from the edge open until someone checks it.

Shortlisting rooms for an awards dinner

Consider a venue with a ballroom and a long gallery. The banquet captain supplies dinner plans for both, along with photographs from the rear tables. Both layouts fit the guest list, but a column masks the gallery's presentation screen from an entire row. The comparison favors the ballroom for an awards dinner with projected names. If the organizer drops the projection and asks for one continuous dining table, the gallery's verified long-table drawing becomes the relevant exhibit.
