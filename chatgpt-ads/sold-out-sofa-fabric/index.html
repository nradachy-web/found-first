Where should ChatGPT ads lead when a sofa fabric sells out?

Keep a sofa ad running with its original fabric only if the shopper can still place the kind of order the ad offers. When a ready-to-ship velvet sells out, pointing the same velvet photograph at a grid of available linens leaves the retailer’s main promise unresolved.

Identify what made this sofa the offer

Read the proposed ad with the swatch name covered. If the appeal still depends on the sheen, color or texture in the photograph, the fabric is part of the offer even when the headline names only the frame. A change of destination alone cannot make a linen sofa deliver that visual promise.

Check the order path for the exact upholstery

Follow the fabric selection through to the fulfillment message. A sample request, a restock alert and an accepted made-to-order purchase represent different decisions. The frame’s general availability tells you little about which action is possible for the photographed fabric.

A moss velvet becomes an order fabric

Suppose a furniture merchant’s stock ledger shows the last stocked moss velvet sofa has sold. Its upholstery book still lists the fabric, and the order screen accepts a custom build with a displayed production window. The merchant keeps the photograph for a made-to-order ad, removes ready-to-ship wording and opens the moss configuration. If the mill discontinues the velvet, the merchant pauses that offer. A stocked linen version can have its own photograph and delivery message once the order screen confirms what can ship.
