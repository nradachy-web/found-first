When a GEO report shows different domains for the same claim

Before paying for work to recover a citation, follow both links and check what each lets a buyer verify. A change in the cited domain can leave the buying decision intact, improve it or send the buyer to terms that apply to a different sale.

Identify whose promise the buyer is reading

A manufacturer can establish what it includes with a machine. A dealer may be the authority on installation for orders it sells. Mark the exact sentence in each saved answer, then open the cited page and look for the party making that promise. A familiar logo on both pages can conceal different obligations.

Set the threshold for a correction

Ask for editorial work when the answer extends a claim beyond the orders it covers or the replacement source leaves the buyer unable to check a material condition. A report that records only the domain change gives you too little evidence to approve that work.

A roaster sold through a dealer

Suppose a coffee equipment maker's order guide includes commissioning with direct purchases. A later answer to the same buyer question cites a dealer. If the dealer's quote sheet includes commissioning and the answer specifies dealer orders, record the new source and close the review. If the sheet lists commissioning as an optional line item and the answer calls it standard everywhere, approve a clarification distinguishing direct purchases from dealer quotes.
