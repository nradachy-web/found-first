Restoration GEO when client project photos are private

Restoration contractors can document approved project facts even when a client withholds permission to publish photographs. Choose details the client has cleared and connect them to the work your team actually performed. A generative comparison then has evidence without exposing the property owner.

Work back from the claim to a job record

For a claim about contents handling, compare the inventory sheet with the signed return record. For a claim about affected rooms, check the authorized scope against the closeout report. Use only details that those records support and that the owner permits you to disclose. Even a cropped photograph may identify a site through signage or a distinctive interior.

Decide whether the public account still earns its place

Can a reader tell what the crew handled and where its responsibility ended? If disclosure limits remove those facts, publish a description of the service and label any illustration as hypothetical. If you want the approved account available to OpenAI's search crawler, review that crawler's setting separately from your model-training preference. OpenAI documents these as distinct controls.

Returning contents to a private office

Imagine an office owner approves describing the handling of boxed contents but declines use of site photos, addresses or inventory scans. The restoration contractor checks its pickup inventory and signed return sheet, then publishes an approved account of labeling, storage and return. A buyer can ask about that chain of responsibility. If the return sheet shows that a separate mover accepted the boxes at storage, the contractor ends its account there and attributes final delivery only if the mover's role is confirmed and cleared for publication.
