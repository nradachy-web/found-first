Who gets credit when SEO and AEO edits overlap during a pilot?

When two teams publish the same purchasing fact during a pilot, record the improvement and leave individual credit open unless the evidence distinguishes their contributions. You can still decide which work is worth funding again.

Trace the fact through the releases

Gather the before-and-after page copies, publication history and saved answer. Identify what each team contributed. One team may have obtained the missing policy from operations while another added it to a page buyers already visit. Documenting those contributions is possible even when nobody can establish why an answer service selected a source.

Use the link for the claim it supports

If the answer links to the SEO team's page, inspect whether that page actually contains the relevant fact. The link shows a reader a route to evidence. On its own it cannot establish which team's edit caused the answer to change. For the next purchase, ask whether you still need help obtaining facts, publishing them, or interpreting the resulting answers.

One deposit rule lands on two pages

Suppose a pallet supplier's SEO team adds its return deposit terms to the ordering page while the AEO writer publishes the same terms in a buyer FAQ. A later answer describes the deposit correctly and cites the ordering page. Credit the shared improvement in public information and the observed citation. If dated copies show the FAQ alone explained an exception for damaged pallets and the answer links to that passage for the exception, give the writer specific credit for supplying that evidence.
