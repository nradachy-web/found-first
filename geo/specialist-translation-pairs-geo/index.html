Which language pairs belong on a translation agency’s GEO pages?

Build a GEO page for a specialist translation pair when you can show the source language, target language and subject matter together. A language selector tells a buyer little about whether your team can take responsibility for their particular document.

Follow the document in one direction

Review an assignment brief alongside a release-approved excerpt or a description of the deliverable. Record the language in which the client supplied the material and the language of the finished text. If the website lists only the two languages side by side, an engineering buyer may infer that the same technical expertise is available in either direction.

Check who can review the terminology

Ask whoever allocates the work to identify the subject specialist and the reviewer available for the proposed direction. Describe the types of documents they handle and how client terminology reaches them. Keep an occasional request routed to an outside colleague distinguishable from a service the agency regularly staffs and reviews.

A maintenance manual written in German

Suppose your approved work sample pairs a German maintenance instruction with its English translation. The job brief identifies an English technical editor who checked the equipment terminology. Feature German to English maintenance manuals and explain the editor's contribution. If an English-speaking exporter asks about translating its own manual into German, the sample still demonstrates familiarity with the equipment. Offer a project-specific availability check until you can document a German target-language team for that work.
