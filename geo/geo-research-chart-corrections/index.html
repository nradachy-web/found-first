Correcting a cited research chart in GEO content

Publish a visible correction wherever your organization presents a chart error that changes the conclusion. Give readers enough detail to recognize the old claim, understand the corrected one and decide whether to amend their own coverage.

Trace the error to its first appearance

Compare the analysis table with the design file, image export and prose that describes it. A reversed legend calls for a different repair from a calculation error in the table. Record the affected edition, the correction date and which conclusions changed. An editor who downloaded the original needs to know whether replacing the image is enough.

Track the versions your team can verify

Check the public article, report download and any chart file you distribute. For a copy published elsewhere, send the editor the corrected source and the claim it supersedes. A GEO review can document which copies now carry the correction. A changed source alone supplies no evidence that a particular generated answer has changed.

Two colors reverse an auction finding

Imagine an auction research report whose exported legend swaps first-time and returning bidders. The underlying table is sound, but the press summary names the wrong group as the more frequent online bidder. The publisher replaces the graphic and corrects the group name in the summary, with a dated explanation on the report page. If inspection instead shows the groups were reversed in the source table itself, the researcher must recheck every calculation that used those labels before the publisher confirms the revised finding.
