GEO for a rental yard that moved pickup but kept its repair shop

When only part of your rental operation moves, commission a GEO update that assigns each public address a job. A blanket address change could send a contractor with a trailer to a repair bay or send a mechanic's parts delivery to the new yard.

Map the trip described by each source

Read the collection email, return ticket and repair intake form as if you had the keys to the truck. Which gate do they send you to, for what purpose, and under whose instructions? Where the directions conflict, have the relevant desk confirm its receiving point before changing the public wording.

Keep the old address where it still earns a visit

A former yard may still take service appointments while its public rental counter has closed. Put that distinction on the old location page and in directions linked from equipment pages. An old address attached to a valid service is worth explaining, even when a simpler location list would look tidier.

The trailer fits through only one gate

Imagine the rental desk moves to a freight park while the old town shop continues repairing compactors by appointment. A pickup email names the freight gate, but a downloadable fleet guide still sends all traffic to town. Replace the guide's directions and describe the shop's repair intake separately. If returns for long-term hires still go to town under their rental agreements, preserve that exception in the return instructions and tell renters which document identifies their drop point.
