Catalog readiness is a business responsibility

A feed is only as useful as the product facts behind it. If merchandising, customer service and the website disagree about what a product includes, another destination can spread the disagreement. The first decision is which approved information represents the offer and who has authority to change it.

For a retailer that sells a product in several configurations, the distinction between a product family and a purchasable variant matters. A prospect should not discover one configuration and arrive at a page that quietly describes another. Found First can help assess whether the current public catalog is clear enough to support an additional discovery surface.

Separate product data from the purchase itself

OpenAI's commerce guidance describes feeds as structured catalog information and distinguishes them from browser actions and purchase confirmation. The practical implication is straightforward: a feed can help explain the offer, while the live purchasing experience must still establish what the customer can actually buy.

That boundary matters when stock, price or conditions change. A catalog project should not be sold as an automatic checkout integration or a guarantee of completed orders. Found First evaluates the marketing and customer-experience fit before proposing any implementation scope. If a required capability is not confirmed, the recommendation should remain an assessment rather than a deployment promise.

Choose an assortment your team can stand behind

Extending every product into a new channel is not always the right starting decision. Some items may need substantial explanation, specific eligibility or a sales conversation. Others may have straightforward descriptions and a reliable store destination. The right scope follows your commercial model and the information you can maintain.

Consider a supplier with both stocked items and custom configurations. Treating them as identical buying experiences could create false expectations. Found First can help distinguish product discovery from quote requests and identify where website content must carry more context. Your team decides what can be sold and how; the marketing work should make that decision visible.

Coordinate the people who own changes

The person responsible for advertising may not own stock, catalog naming or product policies. A feed engagement needs a business owner who can resolve those questions. Otherwise, a technical completion can conceal an ongoing information problem that no one has accepted responsibility for.

Found First brings the discussion back to the buyer journey: which products should be discoverable, what must remain accurate, and where should the customer go next? We can assess readiness alongside organic AI visibility and supporting website work. The result of that conversation is a clearer scope and ownership decision, not an assumption that every catalog requires the same integration.

Before you decide.

Does a product feed replace our product pages?+

No. Your product pages and purchase experience still explain and confirm the offer. A feed is an additional way to provide structured information. If the underlying pages are unclear or inconsistent, the catalog problem remains.

Is an Ads Manager feed the same as an organic commerce feed?+

Do not assume they have the same purpose. OpenAI's current product-ad guidance limits that beta feed use to ads. A readiness discussion should identify the exact surface and intended use before claiming broader distribution.

Can Found First manage every commerce integration?+

We can assess your catalog, buyer journey and visibility needs. Any managed integration depends on the available platform capabilities and agreed technical scope. We will distinguish confirmed implementation work from a recommendation to investigate a particular route.
