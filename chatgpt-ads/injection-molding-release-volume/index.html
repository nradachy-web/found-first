Quoting molding volumes through ChatGPT ads

An injection molder buying ChatGPT advertising should choose whether the offer covers a production release, a volume study or both. Annual demand alone gives an estimator too little to price the first order. Show purchasing how to describe the runs it expects to buy.

Separate the forecast from the release schedule

A single run and a series of small releases can add up to the same annual quantity. They create different setup, storage and delivery obligations. Invite buyers to identify the initial release, likely reorder pattern and whether they expect the molder to hold finished stock. Mark a forecast as an assumption in any estimate based on it.

Let tooling status determine the scope

A buyer transferring an existing mold can supply tool records for review. A new part may require a tooling estimate alongside the piece price. Before promoting an offer for either job, have the estimator show marketing a sample quote that makes the tooling responsibility and production assumptions visible.

One enclosure, two purchasing plans

Imagine an enclosure buyer sends a part drawing and an annual forecast for twelve thousand housings. Its draft order calls for a thousand at a time. The molder offers pricing for those releases and asks who would carry stock under a larger run. If purchasing instead commits to taking the full run in one delivery, estimating can price that schedule separately. The ad can credibly offer a comparison because sales has terms for both arrangements.
