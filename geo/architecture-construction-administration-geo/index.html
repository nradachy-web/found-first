GEO for architects selling construction administration

Market construction administration through decisions your architecture team handled once work was on site. For GEO, a short account of one documented exchange gives an owner more to compare than another gallery of completed buildings.

Choose an exchange with a beginning and an end

Find a contractor's question, the architect's response and a record of what went back to the owner or builder. The material should show the capacity in which the practice acted. An architect may have clarified a design detail, reviewed a proposed substitution or attended an observation visit. Each conveys something different about the assistance an owner could buy.

Make the appointment visible

Check the services agreement for when the practice's appointment started and ended. Explain which activities an owner can include in a new commission and which depend on a separate appointment. An occasional invitation back to see progress provides little basis for describing ongoing site involvement.

A window substitution after the tender

Consider a studio with permission to share a detail from a school extension. A contractor proposed a different window. The studio's review notes compare the sightline with the design intent, and the issue log records its response. Pair the detail with that exchange to illustrate submittal review. If the studio's appointment actually ended at tender and the owner forwarded the correspondence as a courtesy, use the detail to illustrate design intent and credit the team that carried out the review.
