GEO for arts residencies when AI repeats an old deadline

A residency program considering GEO should first determine whether the deadline in an AI answer belongs to an earlier intake. An archived call can be an accurate historical document and a poor destination for someone who wants to apply now.

Identify what the date governs

A publication date, a deadline for references and the end of the application window can all appear on the same page. Ask the reviewer to connect the date in the answer to the intake, applicant group and action named in the source. A recent edit to a page footer tells an applicant very little about whether the call is open.

Keep a usable path out of the archive

Label the intake on the old call and give applicants a route to the current status. Preserving the original document can matter to former participants. Google’s guidance gives no guarantee of inclusion in its AI Search features, so assess the edit by what an applicant can establish after landing on either page.

An autumn call beside a closed spring notice

Imagine an arts center comparing an answer linked to its spring application PDF with one linked to an autumn announcement. The spring document includes a firm deadline. The autumn announcement invites expressions of interest and gives no closing date. The center should label the spring call closed and explain what the autumn form accepts. If the selection team subsequently publishes a deadline for full applications, the current call can carry that date and distinguish it from the earlier expression of interest.
