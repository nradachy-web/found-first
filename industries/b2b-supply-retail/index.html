The buyer may be choosing a supplier relationship, not one item

A business purchaser can need more than a product that looks suitable today. The relationship may involve repeat orders, several users or specific purchasing requirements. If the website reads entirely like a consumer storefront, the reasons to consider your company as a business supplier can remain hidden.

For a retailer with an approved trade offering, the public story should explain that relationship at a useful level. It should not imply that every customer receives the same account terms or service arrangement. Found First helps make the business offering recognizable while preserving the commercial details that require an actual account discussion.

Specifications need to remain connected to the exact product

A broad product category may contain items with different dimensions, materials or intended uses. A workplace buyer needs a reliable way to distinguish them. Category-level copy can help orient the purchase, but it should not make a general suitability claim that overrides the information for an individual item.

We help connect approved product facts to a coherent assortment story. The objective is to make the supplier easier to evaluate without offering technical compliance assurances or claiming that every item meets an unverified requirement. A useful public explanation directs the buyer toward the details needed to confirm the actual business purchase.

Before you decide.

Is this for a wholesaler or a retail supplier?+

The service can support either where the business model is accurately described. This page focuses on retailers serving business purchasing needs. The website should explain the actual ordering and account relationship rather than assume wholesale terms from the presence of business customers.

Do we need to publish negotiated account terms?+

No. Public content can make the business purchasing relationship understandable while keeping specific commercial arrangements private. The important distinction is what kind of offering exists and how a suitable buyer can discuss it, without inventing universal terms for every account.

Can this help buyers find a specialist supply category?+

Yes, when the category is a real strength and supported by useful product information. The work should make its relevance to business purchasing clear. A narrow, accurate specialty can be more commercially useful than a generic claim to supply everything to every organization.
