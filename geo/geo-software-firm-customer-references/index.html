Choosing customer references for a software firm’s GEO content

Choose a customer reference for the decision it can help a prospective buyer make. For a software development firm investing in GEO, a reference who observed a difficult release can be more informative than a well-known customer whose engagement ended at design.

Match the witness to the question

Review the statement of work and delivery record before approaching a reference. Who worked with your team when a release decision had to be made? A sponsor can speak to how options were presented. A client engineering lead who ran the cutover can address rehearsal, recovery planning and the transfer of responsibility.

Offer a reference on agreed terms

Separate material approved for publication from topics the customer has agreed to discuss privately. An anonymized case can establish the type of engagement without implying that a named executive has agreed to take calls. Give procurement enough detail to decide whether to request the conversation.

A launch from scratch or a live cutover

Suppose your portfolio includes a polished launch for a familiar brand and a less visible migration for a distributor. The prospect needs to keep incoming orders flowing during a system change. The distributor’s signed project scope and rehearsal notes establish that its operations lead saw that exact decision. With permission, offer that reference. If the prospect instead needs evidence of stakeholder alignment on an entirely new product, the brand’s design sponsor may be the better witness.
