GEO for document scanning with custom indexing

When your records bureau sells scanning and indexing, ask a GEO provider to show buyers what they can retrieve from each deliverable. Finding a word inside a PDF and filtering an archive by a verified contract renewal date require different evidence on the service page.

Let a sample answer the retrieval question

An anonymized PDF can demonstrate text search. A sample export with labeled columns shows the structure of an index. To describe human verification, the editor also needs a public account of which entries are checked and how an unreadable value appears. A clean-looking spreadsheet gives a buyer little basis for inferring a review service.

Identify where the field came from

Consider a scanning job that imports folder names supplied by the customer. Those names can make a delivery easier to navigate, but they establish nothing about whether someone inspected the contracts inside. In a comparison, attach each claim about the index to its source, whether that is a folder label, a customer spreadsheet or a field read from the document.

Finding leases due for review

Imagine a bureau’s standard sample contains searchable lease PDFs named for each property. It also publishes an optional index specification that defines renewal date and flags ambiguous amendments for review. A facilities team searching for the phrase “renewal notice” could evaluate the standard sample. If the team needs a sortable list of renewal dates, the comparison should point to the indexing scope and sample exception row before describing that workflow.
