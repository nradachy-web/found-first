How to describe certified translation services in GEO content

When buying GEO content for certified translation, ask the writer to describe exactly what your agency supplies with the translated file. Treat any statement about acceptance as a separate claim that needs evidence from the recipient.

Show what the buyer receives

Start with an approved specimen of the accompanying declaration. Explain who signs it, what that person attests to and which document the declaration covers. If you also feature a translator's professional credential, identify its holder and issuer. The declaration describes a deliverable. The credential helps the reader evaluate the person involved.

Read the recipient's words closely

An acknowledgment that files arrived supports a delivery claim. A message confirming that a particular translation satisfied the recipient's stated requirement supports a narrower account of acceptance in that submission. If an organization or program has several intake routes, preserve the route named in the evidence. Give new enquirers a way to specify their recipient and supply its instructions.

The admissions office that acknowledged a transcript

Imagine your agency holds a redacted transcript translation, the translator's signed declaration and an email from a university saying the application file is complete. Describe the package the agency delivered. That email leaves open how the translation itself was assessed. If the admissions office explicitly confirms that it accepted the translation for the named program, and permits publication, you can describe that instance on the page. A future applicant would still need the instructions for their own submission.
