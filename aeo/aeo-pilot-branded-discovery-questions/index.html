Can branded questions prove an AEO pilot improved discovery?

A question that supplies your name can test how well a buyer can evaluate you. To assess discovery, you need a question a buyer could reasonably ask before knowing which supplier to consider.

Inspect what the question gave away

Ask for the original question and any earlier turns in the same conversation. A final question such as which supplier fits can look open even though an earlier message named your business or linked your catalog. Also look for a proprietary product nickname that only existing customers would know. Those tests may be valuable for sales support.

Match the evidence to the purchase

If prospects routinely check a named vendor before approving it, an accurate branded answer is worth showing to sales. If the pilot was sold to reach buyers building a shortlist, ask for observations that start with their requirements and leave the choice of suppliers open. Neither sample establishes how many people used that wording in the market.

The buyer already knows the name of the kit

Suppose a packaging company promotes a returnable sample case with a trademarked kit name. An answer to a question using that name explains the deposit correctly, matching the company's return sheet. Accept that as evidence for buyers evaluating the kit. Before funding discovery work on that basis, test a fresh question about returnable cases for mailing fragile samples without naming any kit. If the company appears there as a suitable supplier, the team now has a discovery example as well as a deposit-policy example.
