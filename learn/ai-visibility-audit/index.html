An AEO audit of the answers buyers see

A clearer view of the business buyers encounter, and the decisions that follow.

A Found First AI visibility audit examines how your business is represented when buyers ask AI about their needs and options. It helps owners and marketing teams distinguish useful visibility from an incomplete or inaccurate picture, so the next investment starts with a clearer business question.

Does the explanation match the business you run?

Your company might be known for an older service, described too broadly to communicate its specialty, or confused with another business. It might appear for one customer need and barely register for another.

An audit asks whether a prospective buyer can recognize your actual fit: what you do, who you serve and why someone should investigate further. That matters when you are launching an offer, entering a market or evaluating an agency.

Three signals that mean different things

A visibility total can hide the distinction that matters to the sale. The surrounding answer gives the signal its meaning.

Three signals that mean different things
SignalWhat it tells youWhat still needs interpretation
Your business is namedThe answer includes the company.It could be a suitable option, a passing reference, a caution or a mistaken identity.
Your website is citedA page is used as a supporting source.The answer may use information from your site without presenting the business as a provider to choose.
Your business is recommendedThe answer presents the company as a possible choice.Its description, customer fit and practical accuracy still matter. A recommendation is not a booked customer.

Use the findings to make a better business decision

A useful assessment should help you decide what deserves attention. An inaccurate service description raises a different issue from an absent company name. A strong presence for low-value work may matter less than weak recognition for the specialty your business is built around. The commercial significance comes from your customers and offer.

Found First combines Nick Radachy's marketing judgment with Jeffrey Davis's website, software and AI engineering. We can connect questions about representation to the business priorities behind them, then discuss whether organic visibility work is appropriate.

The audit also has boundaries. An observed answer is evidence of that answer, not a permanent ranking or a record of every customer's experience. Google itself explains that its AI search features can produce different responses and links. A useful assessment makes uncertainty understandable rather than hiding it inside a single score.

Questions about an AI visibility audit

Is an audit useful if AI already names our business?+

It can be. Being named does not establish whether the description is accurate, whether the company fits the customer's need or whether that appearance is representative. An assessment should address the uncertainty relevant to your business, rather than simply confirm that a mention exists.

Will the audit itself improve our visibility?+

An audit is diagnostic. It informs a decision about what work may be useful; it does not itself change the business's position. Any subsequent website, content or marketing engagement has its own agreed scope.

Can an audit show every answer our customers will see?+

No. It describes the evidence examined, not every possible question, person or future answer. A credible interpretation explains what the observations support and where they leave uncertainty.

Does this measure whether our ChatGPT ads are working?+

Organic AI visibility and paid campaign performance are different questions. ChatGPT ads are separate from the answers. If your priority is advertising results, we can discuss campaign measurement rather than treating an organic mention as an ad outcome.

How is the investment decided?+

We discuss the business question and proposed assessment directly. The scope should be useful for the decision you are making, with commercial terms agreed before the engagement starts.
