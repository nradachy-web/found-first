AEO for fundraising software that helps donors find employer matches

Fundraising software marketers should use AEO to answer a development director’s practical question. After a donor finds an employer in the matching gift tool, who still has work to do before the nonprofit can record a matching gift as received?

Follow the donor out of the lookup

Use the donor-facing result screen, the employer instructions and the fundraiser’s view of the gift record. Describe what the product contributes at each point. Showing an employer’s instructions helps a donor find a request route. It tells a nonprofit buyer little about whether the product transmits that request, checks its status or reconciles a payment.

Give a buyer a way to test “matched”

Ask the content team what evidence sits behind that word in a product description. A donor reporting a submission, an employer approving it and a nonprofit recording receipt are useful but distinct events. Name the event a status represents and who supplies it. If staff must verify a payment against their own records, that is part of evaluating the product’s fit.

The donor has found their employer. Has the request been filed?

Consider a hypothetical vendor whose lookup displays an employer form and whose activity log records the donor opening it. The editor should describe a referral to the employer and tell the development director that staff cannot infer submission from that log. If the vendor can show an acknowledgment returned by the employer for a submitted request, the copy can describe that additional confirmation. The nonprofit would still need evidence of payment before treating the match as received.
