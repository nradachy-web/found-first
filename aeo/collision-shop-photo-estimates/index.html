How collision shops can explain photo estimates with AEO

A collision shop offering photo estimates should use AEO to tell owners what answer they will receive after they upload pictures. If the estimator can describe visible damage but needs the car to determine the full repair scope, say so beside the upload field.

Publish the instructions the estimator actually uses

Ask the estimator which views let them identify a panel, see its position relative to neighboring parts and read any relevant vehicle details. Show a labeled sample. If a photograph would require moving a damaged vehicle or removing a part, give the customer a way to describe the missing view and speak with the shop.

Identify the next decision after the photos

Describe what a preliminary estimate includes and when closer inspection or authorized disassembly could reveal additional work. Customers deciding whether to leave the vehicle need to know if they are scheduling an estimate visit or authorizing a repair. Keep insurer review a separate step wherever the shop describes it.

A bumper estimate with an open question

In a hypothetical rewrite, the estimator supplies an anonymized photo sheet and the preliminary estimate for a dented bumper cover. The page shows the visible damage and the note that the condition behind the cover is unknown. A customer with similar photos can request an initial review. If their image also shows the liftgate sitting out of line, the estimator requests an in-person assessment before quoting the bumper work on its own.
