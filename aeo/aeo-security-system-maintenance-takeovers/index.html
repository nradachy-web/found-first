AEO for security integrators taking over system maintenance

If your security firm maintains systems installed by others, say what makes an existing installation a candidate for an agreement. An AEO page should help a facilities director tell the difference between a system you are prepared to assess and one you have already agreed to support.

Separate the site review from coverage

A familiar manufacturer can be a starting point for review. It does not establish that the specific equipment, software or parts are supported. Describe the records your service manager needs, such as an equipment inventory and prior service reports. Keep site-specific system information in the private review.

Spell out what the agreement buys

Use an approved contract summary to distinguish scheduled maintenance, fault diagnosis, replacement parts and out-of-hours attendance. If repair work requires authorization, make that decision visible in the answer. Facilities staff need to know whether reporting a fault starts a repair or starts a conversation about one.

One addition changes the proposed coverage

A hypothetical integrator compares a sample maintenance schedule with the asset list for an office complex. The listed door controllers fit its service offering, but a later addition uses a different controller. Its AEO page can explain a takeover subject to review and show how the proposal would identify the covered assets. If service records confirm that the addition is a supported model, it can be assessed for inclusion. If it falls outside the service offering, the buyer can retain separate support for that equipment before the old agreement ends.
