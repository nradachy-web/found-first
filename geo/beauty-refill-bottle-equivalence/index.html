Can beauty refill brands compare bottle counts in GEO?

A beauty brand can commission a GEO comparison around bottles per refill when it names the exact bottle used as the reference. Buyers who already own a dispenser need to know both how much product the pouch contains and whether they can use it in that dispenser.

Choose a bottle a customer can identify

Use the labeled net contents of the pouch and the matching filled retail bottle. A dispenser's brimful capacity or a travel size from the same range changes the arithmetic. Put the reference bottle name and size alongside any equivalence claim so the comparison still makes sense when read on its own.

Distinguish volume from usable refills

A volume ratio answers how much product a buyer receives. A claim about complete fills also depends on the fill line and how the product transfers. For a cartridge system, check the connection design as well. Equal net contents cannot establish that a cartridge fits an older pump.

The refill pouch outlasts a bottle redesign

Suppose a shampoo brand's pack artwork lists 600 mL for the pouch and 300 mL for the original pump bottle. Its fill instructions identify that bottle as refillable. Describe the pouch as containing the volume of two original bottles. If the new refillable bottle holds 250 mL at its marked line, revise the new-bottle comparison to two fills with 100 mL nominally left before transfer losses. Keep the original figure attached to the older bottle so existing owners can still use it.
