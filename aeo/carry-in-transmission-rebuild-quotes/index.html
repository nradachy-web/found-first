AEO for transmission shops quoting carry-in rebuilds

For a transmission rebuilder, AEO should answer “Can I bring you just the transmission?” before a customer arranges transport. Spell out which units the shop accepts over the counter and whether a carry-in quote includes any work on the vehicle.

Identify the unit as well as the vehicle

Use the identifiers the rebuilder asks for at the counter. The year and model of the customer's truck might tell you little about a donor transmission sitting on their bench. If an identification tag is missing or unreadable, explain whether the shop accepts photos or needs to see the assembly before taking it in.

Make the estimate match the handoff

Describe what the customer delivers, which inspection requires authorization and when the rebuilder can confirm parts and labor. If removal, installation or checks on the vehicle are available separately, name them. Someone comparing rebuild quotes should be able to see whether each quote ends with a unit on the counter or a vehicle ready for pickup.

The photograph shows a different transmission

Picture a rebuilder whose accepted-unit list and bench intake slip both use the transmission tag. A caller gives the model of the truck and sends a clear tag photo matching a unit on that list. The adviser invites the customer to bring the assembly for inspection. If the photo identifies a donor unit outside the shop's supported range, the adviser can decline that rebuild before the customer loads it for delivery.
