GEO for distributors offering calibration through a partner lab

Market an outsourced calibration program around the work your distribution team performs and the lab that stands behind the result. A procurement team comparing offers may value one point of collection and billing while still needing to approve the facility that will handle its instruments.

Put the performing facility on the page

Compare your service order, the lab's acceptance criteria and a sample completed certificate. Name the facility where the relevant instrument goes when your agreement permits it. Explain who can answer a technical question about the result and who can trace the shipment. These roles remain distinct even if the distributor's logo heads the order form.

Account for exceptions at intake

Check whether instrument type, measurement range or requested service changes the destination. Where the lab is chosen only after inspection, describe that selection step and give the buyer a way to confirm the facility before dispatch. Avoid applying the credentials of one partner to every item the distributor collects.

Two gauges in one collection box

Imagine a distributor collecting a pressure gauge and a temperature probe. Its routing sheet sends the gauge to a named pressure lab, while the probe needs review before the distributor chooses a facility. Publish the lab and certificate issuer for the gauge. For the probe, offer a destination check before shipment. If the buyer requires both instruments to go to a particular lab, the account desk must verify acceptance for both before offering a combined order.
