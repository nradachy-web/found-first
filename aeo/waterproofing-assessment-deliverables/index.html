AEO for waterproofers explaining what a site visit includes

Waterproofing firms should use AEO to say exactly what a property manager receives after a visit. A proposal for work and a condition report serve different buyers, even when both buyers ask for an assessment.

Start with what leaves the estimator's desk

Look at a blank appointment confirmation and a redacted follow-up. If the confirmation promises a walk-through and the follow-up outlines a proposed scope, use those terms on the service page. Calling the appointment a reporting service can attract a manager whose sole requirement is a document for an ownership meeting.

Give the requester a question they can answer

Ask what the manager needs to take back to the owner. Someone seeking a contractor's proposal can identify the areas available for review. Someone asking for a standalone condition report needs to know whether your firm offers that engagement before coordinating keys and tenant access.

An attachment for the ownership packet

Suppose a waterproofing company has a sample proposal with a marked floor plan. Its estimator uses the plan to identify areas included in the offer. A marketing writer might show a cleared excerpt and explain what the markings mean. If a manager needs an independent report for an ownership packet, the annotated proposal does not establish that the contractor provides one. In that case, remove the report claim and let the manager confirm the deliverable before booking the walk-through.
