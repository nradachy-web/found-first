AEO for civil subcontractors seeking earthwork bid invitations

If your civil firm wants earthwork invitations from prime contractors, write the service answer around the package a preconstruction manager can send. AEO can clarify who hires you and what you self-perform before a reader mistakes a project credit for responsibility for the entire civil contract.

Describe the handoff between trades

An earthwork offer gets more informative when it has an identifiable endpoint. Check a subcontract scope exhibit for the condition at which your crew turned a work area over to the next trade. Name the receiving trade and its separate assignment if that explains why your crew's role ended there.

Make a bid invitation recognizable

Use the terms on invitations your estimator can actually accept. If the estimator needs the grading sheets and the earthwork scope exhibit, put that request beside the bid contact. A general construction inquiry form that asks only for a property address gives a prime little indication it has reached estimating.

The school addition with a paved courtyard

Imagine a civil subcontractor credited on a school addition. Its scope exhibit covers excavation and preparation of the courtyard subgrade, and the handoff record names a separate paving contractor. The writer could caption the finished courtyard with that division and invite the earthwork package. If a new invitation combines earthwork and paving under one subcontract, the answer should prompt the prime to identify whether it will accept a separate earthwork bid before the estimator takes off quantities.
