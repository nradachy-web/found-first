Should a moving company publish a drive-time limit in its AEO copy?

Publish a drive-time limit only when dispatch can explain where the clock starts, which leg it measures and how the company handles exceptions. If a map estimate is just a preliminary screen, the AEO answer should tell a prospect which addresses will settle the question.

Find the trip your estimator measures

A crew’s trip from the yard to the pickup address can be much longer than the customer’s move across town. Read the estimate worksheet to see which journey determines eligibility. Keep that journey distinct from time spent loading and driving between the two homes.

Decide what belongs on the map

A shaded circle is easy to misread where a river, mountain pass or limited crossing changes the drive. Name the places routinely served and describe how dispatch checks the edge. A map can still help readers orient themselves if its caption explains that nearby streets may have different access routes.

The closest yard is across the river

Consider a mover whose worksheet checks crew travel from its dispatch yard to the first stop. A riverside neighborhood looks close on the coverage map, but its access road starts at a distant bridge. The writer can use the yard map and estimator instructions to explain why dispatch asks for the pickup street before offering a local move estimate. If the company begins dispatching crews from a yard on that bank, reassess the neighborhood using the new starting point. Moving the office mailing address alone would have no bearing on that decision.
