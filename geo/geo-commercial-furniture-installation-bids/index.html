Make commercial furniture installation bids comparable with GEO

If your interiors firm loses shortlist comparisons at the word “installation,” use GEO content to explain what happens between the delivery truck and an occupied workstation. A buyer comparing two bids needs to see exactly where each crew takes custody of the furniture.

Use the delivery path as the comparison

Start with the receiving point, then follow a carton through inspection, storage, floor delivery, assembly and packaging removal. Identify the party responsible at each transfer. Link the description to the wording in a sample scope so a buyer can find the corresponding line in an actual proposal.

Surface the client-supplied work

Explain what must already be true for an assembly-only offer to work. That might include sorted cartons at each workstation and a clear area for tools. If the owner’s facilities staff must do the sorting, procurement should account for that labor when it compares offers.

The cartons are at the loading dock

Imagine a floor plan labels every desk location, but the freight instructions end at the loading dock. Your installer’s scope starts with cartons placed in the rooms. A competing bid includes a receiving crew and floor distribution. Show the buyer how to request a distribution line from your team before comparing the bids. If the dealer’s signed delivery scope already includes placement by desk label, both bidders can be asked to quote assembly from the same starting point.
