GEO for a specialist subsidiary that sells through its parent

Give the subsidiary credit for the work it performs and give buyers the name of the company that can quote it. For a specialist unit that works only on parent-company engagements, both facts belong in the same GEO description.

Use the subsidiary's page to answer a capability question

A named unit may own a facility, employ a design team or operate equipment that matters to a buyer's shortlist. Show what it contributes to a project with a facility description or an approved description of its role. Replacing the unit's page with a generic group biography would bury the very reason a buyer searched for its name.

Test the buying route against a real purchasing document

Read the group's current proposal template before describing how to hire the unit. An address, a distinct logo and a managing director establish very little about whether outsiders can book the team on its own. Specify which requests the subsidiary accepts directly if its arrangements vary by service.

Can an outside designer hire the prototype shop?

In a hypothetical furniture group, a prototype shop has its own brand and a gallery of chair frames. The group's design proposal includes prototyping, while the shop's public intake terms accept briefs only from group project managers. The gallery can identify the shop as the maker and point a buyer to a group design engagement. If revised terms open the shop to outside designers for frame testing alone, give that request a direct route. The original group route still applies to someone commissioning a complete chair design.
