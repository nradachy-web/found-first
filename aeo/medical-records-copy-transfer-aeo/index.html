AEO for medical groups handling records requests

Give your AEO writer two questions to test on the medical group’s records page. Can someone find how to get a copy for themselves, and can someone switching practices find out how to arrange a transfer? The intended recipient is a better starting point than an internal department name.

Use the recipient to label each choice

Compare the titles on the records office’s current instructions with the labels in the site menu. If the public link says “Documents,” a visitor could reasonably expect anything from new-patient forms to a visit summary. Label the route by what it lets the visitor request. When both requests begin at the same office, one contact with two short explanations can serve both readers.

Keep the instructions on the public page

Write enough on the website for someone to recognize the right route before signing in or opening a form. The public answer can identify the office responsible and the next step without exposing any records. For Google’s AI features, ordinary search requirements apply to this explanatory page.

One portal link cannot explain two delivery routes

Suppose the group’s portal guide describes downloading available documents, while its records office handout directs transfers to a separate request service. The editor adds “Get a copy for yourself” beside the portal guide and “Send records to another practice” beside the transfer instructions. If the records office also handles personal copies missing from the portal, the personal-copy answer adds that fallback. Visitors can then choose by destination even if they have never used the portal.
