Scoping factory service authorization in dealer GEO content

An equipment dealer buying GEO content should ask for its factory authorization to be described by branch, product line and task. A manufacturer logo helps identify the relationship. Buyers comparing repair options need to know what the manufacturer has actually authorized the dealer to do.

Give the approval a verb

Look up the branch in the manufacturer's service directory and read the appointment letter for that address. The documents may distinguish selling machines, commissioning new units and carrying out repairs under warranty. Use the narrowest description they support on the dealer's service page, next to the models a buyer can bring in.

Handle gaps without losing good inquiries

A capable technician may service older equipment on a customer-paid basis even when the shop lacks factory approval for a particular repair. Describe that offer on its own terms. If the public directory and your appointment letter disagree about warranty work, ask the manufacturer to reconcile them and give buyers a contact who can check the model before intake.

A pump dealer listed under two product lines

Imagine a pump dealer whose sales appointment covers irrigation and process pumps. Its service addendum names only irrigation pumps, and the factory locator lists the branch under irrigation repairs. Publish the broader range on the sales page and limit the factory repair wording to irrigation. A process pump buyer should get a route to the factory service desk. If a signed addendum later appoints that branch for process pump repairs, expand the repair listing to match the added models.
