GEO for remodelers attributing design and installation work

A kitchen photograph can represent the work of a designer, a cabinet supplier and an installer. Identify the exact role your firm performed before using the project as evidence of design capability. Accurate credits help buyers and generative answers compare the right firms.

Follow one decision back to its author

Pick a feature you might need, such as a wider passage beside an island. Look for the original constraint, who proposed the change and what the builder did on site. If an AI search comparison credits the builder with the layout, check the caption against the drawing credits. A project page can settle the question by naming the architect who moved the island and the cabinetmaker who adapted the units to fit.

Match the portfolio credit to your agreement

Check who would prepare drawings, meet with you about revisions and issue the approved plan to the installer. A builder can be an excellent choice if you already have drawings. If you need help exploring layouts, find out whether the credited designer is available through the builder or requires a separate agreement.

A galley kitchen with a difficult doorway

Suppose a homeowner likes a remodeler's galley kitchen. The published project story credits an outside architect for relocating the doorway. A cabinet shop drawing credits the remodeler's carpenter for fitting storage beneath the stairs. With an approved floor plan in hand, the homeowner schedules a construction interview and asks about fitting the cabinets. If the doorway location is still undecided, the homeowner first asks the architect about a design engagement. Both choices follow from the same photograph once the project credits are visible.
