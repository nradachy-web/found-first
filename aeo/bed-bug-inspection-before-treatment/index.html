Pest control AEO for inspection-only bookings

A customer may be ready to pay for a bed bug inspection without committing to treatment. If your company offers that appointment, state what the inspection includes and what the customer receives afterward. That gives an answer engine a defensible basis for directing an inspection inquiry to your business.

Give an uncertain customer a way to book

Someone who has seen an unfamiliar insect beside the bed may hesitate at a button labeled “Schedule treatment.” Put the company's answer to “Can you identify this first?” above that button. Explain whether the office reviews insect photos, offers an on-site inspection or refers identification requests elsewhere. A description of marks on skin gives a pest dispatcher too little information to identify an insect or select a treatment.

Identify the deliverable the owner needs

For a homeowner seeking peace of mind, a discussion of the inspection findings may meet the immediate need. A renter whose property manager requested written findings needs to know whether the company supplies a report and whose permission it needs for the visit. Explain these options before asking someone to commit to preparation for treatment.

An insect photo accompanies the request

In a hypothetical inquiry, the customer sends a blurry photo of a speck on a headboard. The office uses its sample inspection report to explain what will be documented and books the inspection the customer wants. If the customer instead supplies a recent professional report identifying bed bugs in the same bedroom and asks about treatment, the estimator reviews its findings, confirms what still needs inspection and prepares the scope for a treatment proposal.
