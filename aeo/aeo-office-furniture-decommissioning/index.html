What office interiors firms should answer about decommissioning in AEO

Use AEO to define the endpoint of your office decommissioning offer. A buyer vacating a floor needs to know whether your interiors team will leave dismantled furniture ready for pickup, move it to another office or arrange its removal to an approved destination.

Give the owner a decision before move day

Start from an asset list the buyer can mark for reuse, return or removal. Explain who approves a destination for each group. If your firm coordinates a donation, identify when acceptance by the receiving organization enters the plan. A removal service cannot promise a recipient will take a particular item.

Use portfolio captions to explain the handoff

A photograph of an empty floor establishes little about where the furniture went. Pair a relevant project image with a plain description of the work the buyer can purchase, including whether transport was in scope. For Google’s AI features, ordinary search requirements apply. No special markup is required to publish that answer.

Matching workstations with different owners

Picture a tenant preparing to vacate a suite. Its annotated furniture inventory marks some workstations for reuse, while the lease closeout list identifies similar desks as landlord property. Given those records, the interiors firm’s writer can illustrate dismantling and delivering the tenant’s units while leaving the landlord’s desks in place. If the landlord instead authorizes their removal in writing, the project manager can add them to the removal scope and confirm a destination. The buyer now knows why an approval column on the inventory matters more than a photograph of the desks.
