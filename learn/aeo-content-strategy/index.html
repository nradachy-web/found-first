AEO content strategy

An AEO content strategy is a short list of the questions buyers ask before they choose, each matched to one page that answers it with evidence. Take the questions from your sales conversations and from what AI answers already say about your market, give every question a single home, and re-read those pages whenever the facts change. More pages is not the goal: a page earns its place by settling a decision.

[ definition ]

An AEO content strategy is the plan that decides which customer questions a business will answer, which page is the home for each one, what evidence that page needs and when it is next reviewed.

This page is about what to write. What the service involves and what it costs is on Answer engine optimization services.

How to choose the questions worth answering

Start from what people already ask you. A keyword tool can tell you a phrase exists. It cannot tell you which question decides the sale.

  1. Collect the questions you are already asked

    Go through sales calls, estimate requests, emails, support threads and reviews. Add the queries Search Console shows for your site. Write each question down in the buyer's words, not yours.

  2. Put the same questions to the assistants

    Ask ChatGPT and Google's AI features, save every answer, and note where you are named, where a detail is wrong and where the answer is thin. An AI visibility audit does this at full scale.

  3. Fold phrasings into one question

    Ten wordings of the same worry are one question. Group them, and split only where the right answer would differ.

  4. Rank by the work you want and the gap you found

    Favor questions tied to the jobs or accounts you most want, where current answers are weak or wrong, and where you hold first-hand knowledge others lack.

  5. Give each question one home

    Look for an existing page that should answer it and improve that page first. Create a new one only when nothing suitable exists.

  6. Write the evidence list before the draft

    For each page, note what a skeptical reader would want to check and where it will come from. If you cannot supply it, the page is not ready to write.

What the answers say about you is the brief

Reading the answers, not only counting them, shows what to write next. In our first published study one business appeared in three different roles, and some answers mixed it up with another company. Each of those observations points to a page: a service page for the shortlist question, a guide worth quoting, a plain statement of availability, and identity details that leave no room for confusion. None of them proves what a new page will change.

What every answer page needs

This is our editorial standard, not a platform requirement. Google says its AI features need no special optimization, markup or file.

  • One question, in the buyer's wordsThe title and main heading state the question or its plain topic. A page answering three unrelated questions is three weak pages.
  • The answer firstTwo or three sentences near the top that could be quoted without the rest of the page. Detail and qualifications follow.
  • Evidence a stranger can checkSpecifications, process steps, photos, named people, documents, outside reviews. Anything stronger than your own adjectives.
  • Who it is for, and who it is notSay where your product or service is the wrong choice. It is the detail a comparison question most needs.
  • Dated factsPrices, availability and anything a platform or regulator can change carry the date they were last checked.
  • A named sourceThe business and, where it matters, the person whose experience the page rests on.
  • Words, not only picturesGoogle lists important content being available in textual form among its fundamentals. A price sheet that exists only as an image fails this.
  • Links to the next questionEach page points to the question a reader is likely to ask next, and to the page that takes an inquiry.

Google's statements are from Search Central: AI features and your website, checked November 11, 2026.

Link the pages the way a buyer moves

A set of answer pages works as a path. Someone who has just understood their problem wants the options next, then the provider, then the cost. Link each page forward to the next question and back to the one before, in the body of the text where the question comes up.

There is a documented reason as well. Google lists making content easily findable through internal links among its fundamentals. It also says AI Overviews and AI Mode may run several related searches across subtopics to build one response. Our reading is that a connected set of pages, each settling one sub-question, serves that better than one long page that touches everything. That is an inference, not a Google instruction.

Keep one home per question as the set grows. When two pages start answering the same thing, the reader, the search engine and the assistant all have to guess which one you mean.

When to review, merge or retire a page

A calendar reminder alone is not a review policy. These four events are what should send you back to a page.

  • A fact changes

    A price, an opening hour, a service area, a staff member or a platform rule. Correct the page that day and update the date it shows.

  • The questions are asked again

    Each time you repeat your research, re-read every page tied to a question on the list and compare it with what the answers now say.

  • Two pages compete

    Merge them into the stronger one and redirect the other. Overlap grows quietly whenever several people publish.

  • A question goes quiet

    If no one asks it any more and the page supports no decision, retire it or fold what is still true into a page that does.

Questions about planning AEO content

How many pages do we need?+

As many as you have distinct questions worth answering, and no more. There is no useful quota. Start with the questions tied to the work you most want, and improve existing pages before adding new ones.

Can existing pages do the work?+

Often, yes. Improving a relevant page is usually more useful than adding a competing page with similar content.

Should every page include an FAQ section?+

Only when it helps the reader. A clear article or comparison can answer the questions better than a long list. Google says no special markup or structured data is needed for its AI features.

Can we use AI to write the pages?+

Use it to draft if you like. What a page needs is first-hand facts, evidence and a person who answers for its accuracy, and a tool cannot supply those. Google's guidance asks for helpful, reliable, people-first content, and publishing in bulk without that is not a strategy.

Who supplies the expertise?+

You do. We bring the question research, the structure and the editing. Claims about performance, customers and capabilities need evidence from the business before they are published.

How do we know whether a page worked?+

Repeat the same questions, read the new answers and compare them with the saved ones. Track inquiries on their own line. Found First reports what it observes and does not guarantee mentions, rankings or leads.
