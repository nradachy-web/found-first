Can AEO help a repair shop book pre-purchase inspections?

AEO can help a repair shop explain the handoff that makes a pre-purchase inspection possible. A prospective buyer may arrange the work while the seller still holds the keys. Give each person enough detail to get the vehicle and the right authorization to the appointment.

State where the inspection happens

Say whether the shop needs the vehicle in its bay or offers a visit to the seller. A dealership address entered on a request form can easily look like a request for a mobile inspection. If the seller or buyer must bring the car in, say who should confirm those arrangements before the shop reserves bay time.

Show a sample of what the buyer gets

An anonymized sample report helps a buyer distinguish observed wear from items the technician could not check. Use it to explain what the appointment covers, who receives the report and whether a follow-up discussion is available. Document any limits on the visit, such as a road test the seller declines.

The seller's terms change the booking

Suppose an independent shop's booking form requires a seller contact, a report recipient and a drop-off arrangement. An out-of-town buyer wants the report by email, and the dealer agrees to bring the car to the bay. The adviser can confirm that appointment with both parties. If the dealer allows inspections only on its own lot and the shop offers bay inspections exclusively, the page gives the buyer that answer before anyone reserves a time.
