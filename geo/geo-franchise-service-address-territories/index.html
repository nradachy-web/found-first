GEO for franchises that assign jobs by service address

Commission territory content around the address where work will take place. For a service franchise, that detail can change which operator may quote a job even when a different office is closer or already serves the customer's other properties.

Choose the map that governs booking

Franchise development maps show prospective owners where territories might be sold. Dispatch records show which unit currently accepts service requests. Ask your GEO writer to use the latter for customer pages and to check how dispatch handles boundary streets, shared postal codes and unassigned addresses. Publish a usable locality description without exposing private territory agreements.

Leave a route for unresolved addresses

When a town spans two territories, listing the entire town under both offices invites duplicate requests. Describe the split if buyers can recognize it. Otherwise, send the service address to the central booking desk for assignment. Keep billing address and service address separate on the form.

A property manager books across a river

Imagine a pavement maintenance franchise whose dispatch map divides a town at the river. A property manager on the west bank requests a quote for an east-bank lot. The local page should ask for the lot address and send the request to the east operator, even though the manager's office appears in the west directory. If dispatch has a documented exception for a named commercial campus spanning the river, use the campus assignment for that site. A map alone would send part of the job to the wrong franchise.
