GEO for implementation partners handing off support at go-live

An implementation partner's GEO page should tell a buyer who will answer after the system goes live. Use the handoff terms to show which expertise the buyer is purchasing and where the partner's responsibility ends.

Describe a ticket that crosses the boundary

Take a familiar incident from your support guide, such as an import failing after a field mapping changes. Identify who diagnoses the mapping, who can alter the integration and where a suspected platform defect goes. A vendor badge alone tells a procurement team little about who will own that ticket.

Pin continuing support to the offer

Read the rollout scope alongside the acceptance checklist and support schedule. Put included launch assistance in the implementation description. If a buyer must procure a separate support agreement to retain your team, say when that choice takes effect. This gives bids with different handoff arrangements a fair basis for comparison.

A warehouse integration after acceptance

Suppose a partner configures a warehouse connector and trains the client's administrator. The acceptance checklist transfers mapping changes to that administrator, while the vendor's support guide sends suspected software defects to its product desk. Describe a project with a client-operated handoff and name both routes. If the buyer adds a managed integration agreement that makes the partner the first contact for failed imports, change the support entry point for that offer. The vendor can still own the underlying product fix.
