Tracing syndicated research citations in a GEO review

Count a partner's republication as another route to your finding, while keeping track of how many independent studies support it. When commissioning a GEO review, ask to see both the page a generated answer cites and the research that supplied its evidence.

Follow the sentence back to the table

Save the wording of the answer and open the cited article. Match its claim to a release, figure or table by checking the question, population and result. A partner may round a percentage or shorten your label. A close resemblance merits a check, but it is insufficient on its own to assign your study as the source.

Give new analysis its own credit

A partner can calculate a regional breakdown or combine your dataset with other records. Record which piece came from your research and who produced the derived comparison. Crediting your organization with the partner's conclusion makes it harder for buyers to find the analyst who can explain it.

Three trade stories repeat a repair survey

Picture a maintenance research firm reviewing an answer that links to three trade stories about spare-part delays. Each story reprints a different passage from the same press release. The reviewer records three cited stories and one underlying survey, then checks whether any story names the firm. On closer reading, suppose one story also analyzed its own repair tickets. It supplies additional evidence for ticket turnaround only. It still relies on the firm's survey for the claim about managers' reasons for stocking parts.
