Who gets the byline on commissioned research for GEO?

For commissioned research, give your GEO editor a plain account of who funded the work, who carried it out and who stands behind the interpretation. Assign the byline from actual authorship. A logo on the cover cannot resolve those roles.

Attribute the finding and the commentary

Read the signed report, contributor statement and methods note together. The firm that collected the responses may have delivered a dataset to a different team that analyzed it. Credit fieldwork as fieldwork. If a sponsor's executive adds a forecast in the foreword, identify the executive as the speaker when quoting that forecast.

Make the credit survive a change of host

Write a short citation line with the study title, edition, author or research group and publisher. Put the roles on the public summary and in the download. Readers opening a partner's copy should have enough information to identify who can answer a methods question.

A membership association hosts a sponsored report

Imagine a printer maker pays for a survey of print shops. A research firm designs the questionnaire, conducts the analysis and signs the report. A trade association publishes it. The association's editor credits the firm as author, the association as publisher and the maker as sponsor. If the firm's deliverable was only a file of interview responses and the association wrote the analysis, the byline moves to the association's analysts. The research firm keeps a credit for the interviews it conducted.
