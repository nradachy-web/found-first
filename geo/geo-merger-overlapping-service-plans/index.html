Comparing overlapping service plans in GEO after a merger

When merging companies have sold different services under the same plan name, commission GEO around the actual quotes buyers can accept. Putting both sets of features in one plan description can create an offer neither sales team will honor.

Read the two plans as a buyer would

Give the writer the current proposal forms and service schedules from both sides of the merger. Look for differences that could change a shortlist, such as who staffs an event, what equipment arrives and which work the venue must arrange. A joint announcement establishes why the names now appear together. It says little about what comes with a booking.

Choose when one comparison can replace two

Keep a qualifier beside each plan name for as long as sales can issue materially different proposals. Once the combined team quotes a common scope, publish that version for new inquiries. Retain the prior descriptions wherever customers still use them to understand existing bookings.

Two meanings of Complete Production

Imagine two event firms merge. One firm's Complete Production schedule includes an on-site sound engineer. The other's equipment proposal includes setup and collection only. A venue searching for staffed sound needs to see the originating firm's name beside each version. If the merged firm later issues a common proposal that includes an engineer on every new booking, the writer can combine the new-business comparison. A venue with an earlier equipment-only booking still needs its original schedule.
