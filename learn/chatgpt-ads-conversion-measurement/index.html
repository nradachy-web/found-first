A recorded action needs a business meaning

A click shows that someone interacted with an ad. An inquiry indicates a different level of intent. A completed order or qualified sales opportunity answers another question again. If these actions are grouped together as success, the report can look decisive while the business remains unsure what it received.

Consider a hypothetical consultation business. A visitor selecting its contact button, submitting a request and attending a suitable appointment are three different events. The owner needs to know which one the campaign is being judged against. A useful measurement conversation starts with that meaning, before discussing the apparent cost of a conversion.

Platform attribution and a confirmed customer are different evidence

OpenAI's conversion measurement uses events received from a connected data source, campaign configuration and available attribution signals. Its documentation also describes modeled attribution where available. A platform conversion is therefore a reported attribution outcome, not an independent verification that a particular customer was a good fit or became revenue.

Your business records answer questions the ad report cannot settle on its own. Did the inquiry reach the right team? Was it relevant? Did the person purchase, cancel or remain undecided? Keeping those questions visible gives campaign decisions a commercial basis. It also prevents a marketing label from becoming an unsupported claim about sales.

Different totals are a reason to investigate, not choose a favorite

OpenAI says its reporting and third-party analytics can differ because of attribution methods, reporting windows, timestamps, consent and browser conditions, deduplication and configuration. Its current reporting can include eligible view-through conversions as well as click-through conversions, depending on the selected windows.

Those differences change what a comparison means. A visit count and an attributed event count may both be valid while describing different things. The business needs an explanation that relates each number to the decision being made. Agreement between dashboards is not the goal by itself; a defensible understanding of what happened is.

Allow the evidence to mature without hiding missing outcomes

OpenAI notes that attributed conversions can take 24 to 48 hours to appear and recent totals may change. Business outcomes can take longer still when a purchase involves a quote, consultation or internal approval. An early campaign view should not be treated as a final account of that customer's journey.

There is also a difference between a confirmed absence of outcomes and an outcome that has not been observed. When inquiries cannot yet be connected to business records, the uncertainty belongs in the interpretation. It should not become a claim that nothing happened, or a claim that unseen revenue must have occurred. Both would weaken the spending decision.

Before you decide.

Is a click on our phone or booking button a confirmed lead?+

A button click establishes interaction with that control. It does not by itself establish a connected call, submitted request or completed booking. Decide which of those outcomes the business needs, then interpret the recorded action at the level it actually proves.

Why can analytics show visits without matching paid clicks?+

A visit and a billable ad click are different measures. OpenAI also notes that ad-review crawlers can visit landing pages with tracking parameters, which third-party analytics may classify as paid traffic. Those crawler visits are not billable ad clicks.

How should we evaluate a sale that takes weeks?+

Separate the early inquiry from its later commercial progress. A qualified conversation can be useful evidence while the eventual purchase remains unresolved. The assessment should reflect your sales cycle and the available records without automatically assigning every later sale to the campaign.
