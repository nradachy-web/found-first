Which photo should lead ChatGPT ads for a commuter bag?

Choose the photograph that lets a shopper check the main claim in your ChatGPT ad. If your commuter bag earns its place on a suitcase by sliding over the handle, show the sleeve in use with enough of the case visible to explain the connection.

Keep the point of contact in the frame

A tight crop of a leather band can look like a grab handle. A wide shot of someone crossing a station can hide the band altogether. Review the image at a small size and ask a colleague to point to what holds the bag in place. Their answer tells you whether the photograph explains the feature on its own.

Let measurements settle what a photo leaves open

Showing a sleeve on one suitcase demonstrates that particular pairing. For shoppers with another case, publish the usable sleeve width beside the bag dimensions on the product page. Keep any compatibility wording within what the product team has measured.

Two photographs from the same trip

Suppose a bag brand has an airport portrait, a rear view of the bag threaded onto a case, and a construction drawing that identifies the sleeve opening. It chooses the rear view for a suitcase-sleeve ad and carries the opening measurement onto the product page. If the brief changes to easy access on a crowded train, neither travel shot shows where the keys go. An overhead photograph with the outer pocket open would give that second ad a defensible lead image.
