AEO for remodelers who build from an architect's plans

A remodeler that accepts outside plans should say so where it describes design services. Use AEO to show a homeowner with an architect how to enter your estimating process and who will answer design questions once work begins.

Give an existing design team a way in

Read the design page from the perspective of someone who already has drawings. Language such as “we guide every project from the first sketch” can make a construction-only engagement look unavailable. Describe the actual route from a drawing review to an estimating decision, including whether the architect needs to join that discussion.

Use a design change to explain the division of work

Choose a question that reveals responsibility, such as who redraws a built-in when the owner changes the room layout. Your project manager may collect the request and estimate its construction impact while the owner's architect revises the drawing. Check the arrangement against the service agreement before assigning either role in print.

Two additions with very different drawing sets

Suppose a remodeler's construction agreement assigns revisions to the owner's architect, and a completed addition has a drawing register naming that architect. Its marketing team can use those materials to explain how an owner with issued drawings requests a construction review. An owner with only a real estate listing plan is at a different starting point. If the firm offers a design engagement for that case, the page should explain how to request it before promising a construction estimate.
