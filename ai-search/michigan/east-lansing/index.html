Proximity explains the setting, not the whole customer base

East Lansing describes downtown as directly across Grand River Avenue from Michigan State University and within walking distance of several neighborhoods. Choose the audience that fits the offer

For an East Lansing creative agency, work for a student organization and work for an established regional employer may involve different responsibilities and commercial expectations. Both can be legitimate assignments. The agency should decide which offer it wants to grow and make that distinction understandable.

AEO is relevant when the company is known for its location but not fully understood for its capabilities. The objective is to connect the right buyer with a coherent account of the work, rather than trying to attract every person who has a reason to visit the area.

What brings the buyer into your market?

A temporary reason to be nearby

The customer may need a clear, bounded purchase during a visit or event. The offer should be understandable without assuming familiarity with the area or a continuing local relationship.

An ongoing personal or organizational need

The customer needs to understand the business beyond its proximity to campus. Expertise, continuity and the intended service relationship may matter more than the immediate setting.

Questions worth asking.

Should an East Lansing business lead with Michigan State University?+

Only when the connection is accurate and relevant to the offer. Proximity can orient a customer, but the company should establish its own identity and avoid implying an institutional relationship unless one actually exists.

Can we serve customers who have no campus connection?+

Yes. The company’s actual service model and expertise determine its audience. A nearby university can provide location context without defining who may become a customer or limiting the business to a particular type of purchase.

How should a business think about event-driven inquiries?+

Consider whether the offer fits a temporary occasion or a continuing need. The two can coexist, but the customer’s expectations may differ. A clear identity helps the buyer recognize which relationship the company is prepared to support.
