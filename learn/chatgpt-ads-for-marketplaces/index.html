Start with the side that is limiting useful activity

A marketplace can have many registered users and still lack the right counterpart for a particular request. More buyers may be unhelpful where suitable supply is absent. More suppliers may become disappointed if the marketplace cannot offer a plausible source of demand.

Found First helps clarify which audience the advertising is meant to serve and what action has commercial value. For a specialist services marketplace, attracting providers in an already crowded category is a different decision from attracting buyers in a category with available capacity. The campaign should follow a specific business need rather than a general ambition to grow membership.

Describe the role of the marketplace accurately

A customer should understand whether they are buying from the marketplace, being introduced to a seller or requesting a match. A supplier should understand whether registration creates a listing, an application or an opportunity subject to further review. These distinctions affect trust and the appropriate next step.

Our advertising and destination work helps make that relationship clear. If a marketplace does not perform the service itself, the copy should not imply that it does. If access depends on approval, the ad should not promise immediate participation. A clear role can make the offer more useful without exposing the internal matching process.

Availability matters at the category level

A broad marketplace promise can hide gaps in the specific category or geography a prospect needs. A visitor may respond to an appealing offer and discover that no suitable choice is available. That is a commercial limitation, even if the platform has substantial supply elsewhere.

Found First can assess whether the advertised proposition has enough operational support behind it. For a marketplace with an emerging category, the right next step might be an accurate availability inquiry rather than a promise of immediate purchase. Marketing should reflect the experience you can provide now, while leaving room for the business to expand honestly.

Follow the outcome past registration

A buyer registration is not a completed match, and a supplier registration is not usable supply. The definition of acquisition quality depends on what happens next in your model. An advertising report should therefore be interpreted alongside the marketplace's own evidence about meaningful activity.

A founder conversation can clarify the audience, category and desired action before Found First proposes managed ChatGPT advertising. We can discuss whether the offer and destination are ready or whether clarity work should come first. The service is paid-channel management grounded in the marketplace's actual transaction model, not a promise that conversational traffic will automatically balance supply and demand.

Before you decide.

Can one campaign acquire buyers and suppliers?+

Those audiences usually need different propositions and destinations. Even if both are commercial priorities, their outcomes should remain distinguishable. A combined signup total can conceal whether the marketplace is gaining the participants it actually needs.

What if we cannot guarantee a match?+

The public offer should say what the marketplace can provide without implying certainty. An inquiry, application or search can be a valid next step when described accurately. Found First will not turn an uncertain matching process into a guaranteed transaction.

Is marketplace traffic judged like ecommerce traffic?+

Only where the buying model is comparable. A direct product purchase, a request to several providers and a supplier application have different business meanings. Found First helps define the relevant outcome before comparing channel performance.
