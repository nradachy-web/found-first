GEO for villa operators comparing exclusive-use bookings

For villas on a shared estate, ask your GEO partner to compare exactly what each reservation gives a group to itself. The most persuasive evidence is often a marked site plan paired with the occupancy agreement for the specific booking option.

Draw the boundary around the reservation

Mark buildings, gates, paths and facilities that matter to the stay. Describe who can use each space. Guests might have sole use of the main house, share the entrance drive and reach the pool along a path other residents also use. An aerial photograph shows proximity but rarely establishes permission or privacy.

Account for the separately bookable annex

Find out what happens in the reservation system when someone selects the whole estate. If that action takes the annex off sale, the operator can explain how the combined booking secures it. An empty annex on the calendar does not give a main-house guest control over who might book later.

A private pool behind a shared gate

Imagine a villa operator offering a farmhouse and a coach house. The plan shows one gate, and both rental agreements grant pool access. The writer reserves the phrase private pool for the combined estate booking after verifying that it blocks both properties. If revised terms give the farmhouse exclusive pool hours each morning, its comparison can now feature that time window. The shared gate and the coach house guests' afternoon access still belong in the description.
