AEO for CRM vendors explaining integration writeback

Make integration direction part of your AEO brief if prospects ask whether a change in your CRM reaches their other system. An answer needs a starting point, a destination and a named change. The word “sync” leaves all three open.

Give the field its own answer

A sales manager asking “Will finance see the address I just changed?” has a narrower question than “Does it integrate with accounting?” Check the connector's field map for the billing address, the record it belongs to and which system can update it. Even if both systems can create customers, the billing address may have a different rule.

Set a test for publishing “two-way”

Ask whoever owns the connector to demonstrate the same field changing in each direction on a test record. If the return trip depends on a custom workflow, say who configures it. Put that condition beside the integration claim where a buyer comparing CRMs will see it.

A finance team needs the right billing address

Suppose a CRM vendor's integration card says “customer sync.” Its mapping sheet and sandbox change log show that accounting sends billing addresses to the CRM and accepts only sales-owner changes in return. The AEO editor can answer the finance question directly and tell reps to request address corrections in accounting. If a supported writeback setting also passes the address test, the answer can describe the setting and identify the administrator who enables it.
