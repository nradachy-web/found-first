GEO for brokerages that inherit another office’s directory listing

Ask who owns the directory entry before commissioning a GEO provider to update it. Changing the phone number on a previous occupant's profile can leave your brokerage appearing to claim its memberships, specialties and client history.

Determine what the publisher lists

Inspect the entry heading, member record and destination of the contact button. Some directories describe the occupant of a retail suite. Others profile an enrolled business. For a tenant record, a change of occupant can justify new contact details. A member profile follows the member even when someone else takes the lease.

Make the correction specific enough to use

Give the publisher the disputed fields, the current office contact page and evidence identifying the member associated with each claim. Ask the provider to show you the public entry after the publisher responds. A submitted correction tells you little about which business a buyer will actually reach.

The chamber profile that came with the phone number

Suppose a brokerage takes a former broker's suite and desk line. A chamber profile now rings the new receptionist but still carries the former broker's name and testimonials. The chamber confirms the membership belongs to the former firm. Ask it to correct the contact route and use a separate entry if the new brokerage joins. If the record instead belongs to the building's tenant map, request a new occupant label and link there, leaving member history with the chamber.
