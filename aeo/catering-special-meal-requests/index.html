Catering AEO for special meal requests at events

For an event caterer, an AEO answer about special meals should help the host work out what can be agreed before they sign off on the menu. Cover how a request is reviewed and how the confirmed dish gets to the guest during the service the host has chosen.

Be precise about what the menu establishes

A vegetarian symbol helps a host count menu choices. It cannot settle a guest’s question about a particular ingredient or preparation practice. Explain who can check the current recipe, what details they need and when the host will receive a response. Use menu descriptions the kitchen can support for that event.

Carry the agreement through to service

For a seated dinner, describe how the organizer records an agreed choice on the seating plan and how late seat changes reach the service captain. A buffet may require a different arrangement, such as collecting a named dish from a member of staff. Publish the handoff your team actually offers.

The host changes from plated service to a buffet

Suppose a banquet order includes a separately prepared dish, and the place-card proof identifies the guest who requested it. With assigned seating, the captain can use the approved seating chart to direct delivery. If the host switches to an open buffet, that chart no longer identifies where to deliver the dish. The coordinator needs to agree on a pickup or handoff with the host and amend the banquet order before confirming the revised service.
