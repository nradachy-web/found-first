Measuring AI marketing results

Measure AI marketing as five separate things: mentions in AI answers, citations of your pages, visits to your site, inquiries and revenue. Mentions and citations are visible only in saved answers, visits and inquiries in analytics and ad reports, and revenue in your own sales records. Report each on its own line, and do not claim that one caused another without evidence.

Five measures, and what each one can tell you

An assistant can name you without anyone clicking. A visitor can read about you in an answer and type your address days later. A form can come from someone who will never buy. These are different events, seen in different places, and adding them together produces a number that means nothing.

Set up measurement in this order

The order matters because the first two steps cannot be done after the fact. You cannot save a baseline once the work has started, and a lead definition agreed after the results are in is not a definition.

  1. Define a qualified inquiry

    Write down what counts, who decides and where it is recorded. A booked job, a quote request in your service area and a wrong number are not the same thing.

  2. Save a baseline of answers

    Before anything changes, put a fixed set of customer questions to the AI systems you care about and keep every answer with its date. AI visibility audits has the checklist.

  3. Tag what you control

    Add UTM parameters to every ad landing page URL, set up conversion measurement in Ads Manager, and ask every new customer how they found you.

  4. Keep one record of inquiries

    Log each one with its source marked as observed, reported by the customer, or unknown. Unknown is an honest entry.

  5. Repeat the same research

    Ask the same questions in the same way, then set the two rounds side by side with inquiries and revenue on their own lines.

Why the ad report and your analytics will not match

If you run ChatGPT ads you will have at least two sets of numbers, and they will differ. OpenAI says so itself: Ads Manager, your analytics provider and other ad platforms may report different conversion totals, and a difference does not necessarily indicate an error.

Choose one source of truth for inquiries, which should be your own record, and use the platform reports to explain it, not to add to it. These are the reasons OpenAI documents for the gap.

  • A click is not a session. Ads Manager counts interactions with the ad. Analytics counts page loads, which depend on redirects, consent choices and browser blocking.
  • The attribution window is a setting. Ads Manager can report conversions over a 7, 14 or 30 day window after a click, with or without a 1 day window after a view. Change the setting and the total changes.
  • Totals arrive late and can be modeled. OpenAI says attributed conversions can take 24 to 48 hours to appear, spend about 7 to 8 hours, and that totals may include modeled conversions where available.
  • Some tagged visits are not people. OpenAI's ad review crawlers may open landing pages with your tracking parameters, and analytics may file those visits as paid traffic. They are not billed as clicks.

How to report without overclaiming

Hold any AI marketing report to these, including ours.

  • Every count has a date and a denominatorNamed in so many of so many answers, collected on a stated day.
  • Each measure has its own lineMentions, paid results, inquiries and confirmed revenue are never added into one figure.
  • Sources are labeledObserved, reported by the customer, or unknown.
  • After is not becauseA change that follows the work is reported as a change. Calling it a result needs more than timing.
  • Unavailable is not zeroIf an AI system could not be collected, the report says so and leaves it out of the totals.
  • The method stays the sameSame questions, asked the same way. Any change in method is flagged beside the numbers it affects.
  • Costs sit beside outcomesMedia and fees appear in the same report as the inquiries they are being judged against.
  • A flat month looks like a good oneSame format, same prominence, same level of detail.

Questions about measuring results

Is an AI mention a lead?+

No. It is an observation that an assistant named your business in a saved answer. A lead needs a separate action, such as a call or a form, and should be qualified against your own criteria.

Can you attribute every sale to AI?+

No. People read an answer and call later, links lose their referrer, and most purchases involve more than one touch. We show what the evidence supports and label the rest as reported by the customer or unknown.

Can Google Search Console show my traffic from AI Overviews?+

Google says sites appearing in AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in overall search traffic, in the Performance report within the Web search type. Treat those visits as part of your search traffic.

How do I see which visits came from ChatGPT ads?+

Add UTM parameters to the landing page URLs in your ads. OpenAI says those parameters persist on ad clicks, so your existing analytics can separate the traffic. Expect the click count in Ads Manager and the session count in analytics to differ.

So what is the return on AI marketing?+

It is the confirmed revenue you can trace to the work, set against media and fees, with a plain note about what cannot be traced. A single return figure for AI visibility that comes without that note is a guess.

How often should we review results?+

Often enough to catch a problem, and no more often than the data can support. Small samples swing widely, so avoid changing several things at once or drawing conclusions from a handful of visits.
