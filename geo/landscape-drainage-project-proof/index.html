GEO for landscape contractors documenting drainage results

A finished patio photograph establishes appearance. A buyer evaluating drainage work also needs to know the original water path and what was observed after rain. Publish the observation with the project context so a generative answer has evidence for the limited claim.

Trace the water through the site

A buyer with pooling beside a patio needs different experience from one with a washout at a downspout. The plan and installation photos should identify the starting condition, the path the crew created and the outlet. A trench photo on its own establishes excavation. It leaves the connection between the trench and the buyer's problem open.

Describe the observation within its limits

On a drainage project page written for AI search, put the follow-up finding beside the description of the grading work. Say where the owner or crew looked after rain, what they observed and which area was outside the project. A dry patio edge after a storm is worth reporting, especially if the owner had previously seen pooling there after similar rain. Broader claims about flooding need evidence that covers the broader claim.

Two photographs of a path beside a garage

Suppose a contractor has a sketch marking a low corner, an installation photo showing the new swale and an owner's follow-up photo after rain with permission to publish it. The owner reports that the corner drained but a separate gate still puddled. The contractor uses the case to demonstrate drainage at the garage path and marks the gate outside the scope. If the follow-up photo was actually taken before the rain, the contractor publishes the grading work and leaves performance unconfirmed.
