What does claiming your business change?

OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT in beta on November 10, 2026. Business Hub is its home for business claims, information and engagement insights. A verified claim creates an official route for maintaining information about your organization.

That makes ownership a practical business decision. A company name, website or product description can be technically correct yet leave customers unsure which offering suits them. Your responsibility is to provide an accurate account of the business you operate today. A claim is useful when someone can keep that account current as the business changes.

Business information, independent answers and ads serve different jobs

OpenAI says claiming a business does not guarantee higher placement or sales, and ChatGPT continues to make independent recommendations. Treat a claimed presence as an information responsibility rather than a purchased endorsement.

Consider a hypothetical company that has stopped selling one service and expanded another. Correcting its business information addresses what it offers. Improving the public explanation of its expertise addresses how prospective customers understand it. Paying to reach eligible audiences addresses distribution. These needs can coexist, but progress on one should not be presented as completion of the others. Found First helps identify which commercial problem deserves attention first.

Who will own the facts after the initial claim?

OpenAI requires the person managing a profile to own the business or be authorized to represent it. Internally, that authority needs a clear counterpart: someone who can confirm the offerings, public descriptions and customer promises being represented.

For a founder, this may be straightforward. For a company with several brands or a changing catalog, marketing and commercial leadership may need to settle which name and offering belong together. An agency can help frame the work, but it should not guess at facts the business itself has not resolved. Clear ownership reduces the chance that an outdated description outlives the service it describes.

Eligibility should shape the opportunity you pursue

OpenAI currently allows eligible businesses with a website in supported markets to claim a business. Industry, location and verification checks affect eligibility. Business Agent access is a separate question; verification does not guarantee permission to publish an agent.

This distinction matters when comparing proposals. A business that needs accurate public information should not be sold a customer conversation feature before its access and use case are established. Equally, an unavailable feature need not hold up useful website or content work. The right scope follows the business need and the access actually available to your organization.

Before you decide.

Does claiming my business start an advertising campaign?+

No. A business claim concerns your identity and information. Advertising is a separate commercial choice. Decide whether you need to correct what customers learn, improve organic discovery or fund paid distribution before treating them as a single project.

Can Found First help if another person manages our marketing?+

Yes. The fit conversation can include the person responsible for your website or marketing. Business ownership, authority to act and approval of public facts should be clear before agreeing work. Existing partners can remain part of that decision.

Does a verified business automatically qualify for a Business Agent?+

No. OpenAI evaluates agent eligibility separately. The initial Business Agent rollout covers eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses. Found First can discuss the customer use case, but access must be established before an agent launch is proposed.
