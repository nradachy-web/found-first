Begin with the actual concern

Disappointing results can mean several things: little delivery, visits without action, inquiries that do not fit or purchases that are not commercially useful. Those problems require different judgments. A review that starts with generic optimization advice can miss the issue the business actually needs resolved.

Found First helps frame the concern in terms your team can verify. If the campaign is quiet, the review should establish what is known about delivery. If leads arrive but sales rejects them, the question is about fit and expectation. The account's activity matters, but it should be connected to what happens after the advertisement.

Put the offer beside the account evidence

An advertising account cannot explain every commercial weakness by itself. The proposition may be unclear, the destination may describe a different offer or the business may be unable to fulfill the work it promotes. Those conditions can make an apparently reasonable campaign unproductive.

Our review considers the relationship between the ad and the actual buying experience. For a service company receiving broad inquiries for a narrow specialty, changing distribution alone may leave the underlying mismatch intact. Found First can discuss whether the offer needs clarification, the page needs improvement or the account warrants a more focused management engagement.

Read reported conversions with their limits

OpenAI explains that conversion totals depend on received events, campaign configuration and attribution conditions. A difference between systems is not automatically an error. At the same time, a reported conversion does not prove that the business received a qualified lead or completed a worthwhile sale.

Found First helps separate measurement confidence from commercial performance. A review should identify which evidence can support a decision now and which uncertainty remains. That makes it possible to discuss investment honestly without treating every dashboard total as definitive or dismissing useful evidence because two systems do not display identical numbers.

A recommendation should change a business decision

A long list of potential improvements is not the same as a priority. The review should help you choose whether to correct an identified issue, refine the proposition, continue with a justified objective or redirect attention. Each option carries a different reason and expected business role.

Found First offers account review alongside managed ChatGPT advertising and supporting website work. A consultation can establish the concern, the current offer and the decision you need to make. We do not promise that every account can be made profitable, and we do not need to find a fault with the existing manager to provide useful judgment.

Before you decide.

Is an account review a commitment to ongoing management?+

The scope should be agreed before work begins. A review addresses the decision about what to do next; management is a separate responsibility. Found First can discuss both without assuming that every review must result in a full channel engagement.

Can you tell whether the previous agency did a bad job?+

Only evidence can support a specific conclusion. Low results alone do not establish poor management. The review should distinguish account decisions, measurement limits, offer fit and business conditions rather than turn uncertainty into blame.

What if the account has very little data?+

The conclusion may need to be narrow. A review can still identify clear readiness or destination issues, but it should not invent a performance verdict. Found First will distinguish a supported finding from a question that remains unresolved.
