GEO when a managed service becomes self-service

If your managed offer becomes self-service, ask for GEO copy that identifies who does the work at each stage. An unchanged service name and a familiar sample report can conceal a material difference in the staff or skills a buyer needs.

Compare responsibilities behind the deliverable

Look at the former statement of work and the current onboarding instructions side by side. Identify who supplies inputs, interprets exceptions and delivers the finished output. A sample showing what the buyer receives should say whether an analyst prepared it or a user produced it with the current tools.

Keep renewal terms out of the new-business claim

Some customers may still receive the managed service under earlier agreements. Their experience can explain why older descriptions exist. Give them a clearly identified renewal or support reference, and describe the work a new purchaser would actually take on wherever prospects compare the offer.

The survey report still looks familiar

Imagine a research brand once cleaned survey responses and wrote an executive summary for every engagement. The new onboarding guide tells buyers to code responses and interpret the charts themselves. The sample PDF still shows the old analyst's commentary. A writer should label that edition and show a current sample alongside the self-service description. If the new order form offers analyst interpretation as an optional service, the comparison can list it separately and specify that coding still belongs to the buyer.
