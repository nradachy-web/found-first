GEO for co-managed IT bids with shared support duties

If your managed IT firm wants to make a co-managed shortlist, use GEO to show where your team’s authority starts and ends. A buyer comparing providers needs to know who can take action when routine maintenance goes wrong.

Follow one task across the boundary

Take a recurring job such as server patching. Trace it from the change request through testing, approval, installation and recovery. Use the service schedule to identify the party responsible at each step. Where the client retains approval, explain what happens to the maintenance window if the approver is unavailable.

Give each reviewer evidence they can use

A procurement lead can use an exclusions list to identify work that needs a separate agreement. The internal IT manager can use a sample change record to see which decisions stay with their team. Before publishing either, check that the service description and the change record assign the same person the authority to approve a restart.

A failed patch after hours

Suppose your service schedule covers patch installation and the client’s runbook reserves recovery approval for its infrastructure lead. A sample ticket shows your technician escalating a failed update. Describe that offer as provider-led installation with client-authorized recovery. If the client delegates rollback authority in the agreed runbook, describe the conditions under which your technician can restore service directly. A buyer with no overnight approver can now distinguish the two arrangements.
