AEO for manufacturers quoting blanket orders with scheduled releases

Manufacturers considering AEO for repeat-order work should explain how they assess the quantity on a blanket order. A buyer asking whether its annual volume qualifies may be offering a firm commitment, a forecast, or a series of individual releases.

Look for three different quantities

Pull a representative purchasing schedule and read it alongside the estimator's intake sheet. The projected annual demand, the quantity the buyer is committing to and the amount due in each shipment may all differ. If your form has a single quantity field, a purchasing manager has to guess which figure you mean.

Explain why the release pattern matters

Use the answer to describe the arrangement your team is willing to review. Some requests call for a single production lot held for scheduled shipments. Others ask the supplier to accept each release as it arrives. Identify who would hold the balance and what release dates the estimator needs to evaluate the request.

The same forecast can describe two different orders

Consider a buyer who attaches a demand forecast and asks for monthly releases of a housing. Its draft order commits only to the first shipment. An estimator following that document would assess the first lot and record the balance as forecast demand. If purchasing instead commits to the full quantity and supplies a release calendar, the estimator can assess one committed order, including the proposed storage and shipment schedule.
