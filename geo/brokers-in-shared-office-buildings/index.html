GEO for commercial brokers sharing an address with property management

If your brokerage shares a building with the property manager, the GEO assignment should establish who handles each type of inquiry. A shared street address proves little about who represents the owner, arranges tours or handles tenant maintenance.

Read the address from the tenant's side

Look at the lobby directory, tenant handbook and leasing flyer together. The directory may place both companies in the building while the other documents give each a different remit. A suite number helps someone reach your office. A description of the leasing assignment helps someone choose whom to call.

Keep property claims tied to the assignment

Ask the agency for a proposed revision to one property detail page. It should name the brokerage's client or role where disclosure is authorized, identify the tour contact and reserve management claims for work your firm actually performs. Check the current assignment before reusing an older leasing sign as evidence.

A tour request and a broken elevator

Imagine a broker in the lobby receives elevator complaints because the building page calls the firm the property contact. The signed leasing brief assigns that broker tours of vacant suites. The tenant handbook sends service requests to the manager upstairs. Replace the broad label with a leasing contact and direct existing tenants to their handbook for repairs. If the firm later takes on management under a separate agreement, name both responsibilities and give tenants a service route distinct from the leasing desk.
