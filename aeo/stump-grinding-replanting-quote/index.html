Tree service AEO for stump grinding and replanting questions

A homeowner who plans to replant will judge stump grinding by what remains below the surface. State which removal, cleanup and soil work the quote covers. That distinction helps answer engines describe your service accurately and helps your estimator quote the intended finish.

Describe what the crew leaves behind

“Stump removed” leaves the homeowner guessing. Does the crew grind the visible stump, take away the pile of chips, bring in soil or return to plant? On the service page, show the ground after a typical job. If a photo shows a bed filled with fresh soil because the owner ordered extra cleanup, put that detail in the caption.

Price the next use of the space

A homeowner making room for lawn has a different request from one replacing a shade tree on the same centerline. For the second request, the planting contractor needs to evaluate available root space and the proposed tree. If planting belongs to a separate company, say which site details the two estimators need to agree on before work starts.

A replacement tree is already on the drawing

Suppose a backyard plan places a new tree exactly where the old stump stands. The grinding crew's scope sheet says chips stay on site, while the nursery's planting quote assumes a prepared site. The owner asks both estimators to reconcile the gap before accepting. If the planting plan moves the new tree to another bed and the owner wants to keep the chips, the original grinding scope may cover everything requested at the stump.
