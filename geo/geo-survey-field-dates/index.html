Which survey dates belong in a GEO research summary?

Anchor a GEO summary to when respondents answered. Show the release date separately so a buyer can tell whether your research captured expectations before an event or experience after it.

Match the verb to the questionnaire

A question about what buyers expect next season supports a statement about expectations during fieldwork. Publishing the report after the season ends does nothing to verify what buyers actually did. Keep the question's time horizon in the takeaway, especially when changing a future-tense sentence for a later edition.

Check what the timestamps can resolve

When a launch falls during fieldwork, look for response dates and a question about exposure. A timestamp may let the research team group replies received before and after the launch. On its own, it cannot establish that anyone saw the launch announcement or that the announcement altered an answer.

A payments bulletin lands after a ticketing change

Imagine a transit research publisher releasing a rider survey the week after a new ticket app arrives. Its fieldwork log shows interviews ended before launch. The editor pairs the date range with a finding about riders' willingness to switch and removes a proposed headline about adoption. If fieldwork instead continued through launch and the questionnaire asked whether riders had used the app, the publisher could describe reported use among those interviewed after release. The original willingness question still belongs under expectations.
