GEO for metal distributors offering traceable cut stock

Before a GEO writer calls your cut stock traceable, walk one shipped blank back to its mill record. Procurement teams comparing suppliers need to know how they can identify the material they receive and what paperwork they can obtain for that order.

Follow a piece beyond the saw

Use a receiving record, saw ticket and packing slip to see where the heat identifier travels. It may appear on each piece, on a bundle tag or in a job record that the buyer can retrieve using a delivery reference. Those arrangements matter to a buyer who will split a bundle across jobs. Describe the arrangement your warehouse actually offers.

Name the document and when to request it

Show whether a mill test report accompanies the shipment, must be specified on the purchase order or can be retrieved later. A redacted report illustrates the fields a buyer can expect. Label it as an example and explain which identifier connects the delivered material to its own report.

A tagged bundle goes to two work cells

Suppose a service center cuts plate into blanks and carries the parent heat number from its saw ticket to a sealed bundle tag. The sales page can offer bundle-level identification and a matching mill report. A customer plans to separate the blanks immediately. If the shop can mark each blank under a documented order option, publish that option and tell buyers to specify it before cutting. Otherwise, keep the bundle limitation visible when describing the service.
