Showing room fit in ChatGPT ads for extendable dining tables

If an extendable table is being advertised for a small dining room, show what happens to the room when the leaf goes in. A photograph of the closed table supports an everyday footprint claim, while a hosting claim also depends on space for chairs and access around the open table.

Measure the path of the extension

Start with the product drawing. Some tops expand at both ends, while others extend toward one wall. Give the shopper the open and closed dimensions and show which end moves. Someone with a fixed banquette needs that information even if the catalog lists an appealing seat count.

Account for the chairs in the scene

A styled room can make a tight arrangement look generous by leaving out the end chairs. Keep the chairs used in the photograph identifiable and show how they sit against the apron and legs. If a generous walkway is part of the message, ground it in a measured layout for that particular room.

The leaf opens toward a sideboard

Imagine a furniture dealer matching a table drawing to a floor plan of its display room. The closed top leaves room to pass the sideboard. Extended, it fits inside the room, but an end chair blocks the route to the kitchen. The dealer uses the closed configuration in its compact dining ad and shows the open dimensions alongside it. If moving the sideboard leaves a usable path with occupied chairs, the dealer can photograph that arrangement and feature the table as a hosting option in that room.
