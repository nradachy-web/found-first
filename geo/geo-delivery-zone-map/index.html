GEO for delivery zones shown only on a map

If your delivery footprint lives in a shaded image, use GEO work to put its usable boundaries into words beside the map. Describe only the places dispatch can identify consistently from its service-area records.

Choose the right geographic unit

A city name works when service covers the whole city. It becomes a poor substitute for a boundary that follows one side of a road. Use a street, district or postal area only when it matches the operating rule. Let the written description explain any exceptions that the map’s scale makes hard to see.

Keep the map for orientation

The visual still helps people recognize a neighborhood. Use a short image description to identify the map and give the detailed boundary rules in ordinary page text. When an address check determines acceptance, explain what the check resolves so someone outside a clearly named area knows how to proceed.

A shaded district divided by a rail line

Suppose a linen service shades the River District on its website, but its dispatch sheet accepts stops only west of the freight line. A hotel comparing suppliers could easily read the shading as districtwide coverage. Publish the west-side rule with the map and direct hotels on the boundary to address confirmation. If dispatch instead assigns routes building by building on both sides, remove the west-side shortcut. List confirmed service locations where appropriate and make the address check the answer for the rest.
