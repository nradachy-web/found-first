Resolving duplicate glossary definitions for AEO

Before consolidating duplicate definitions for AEO, establish whether different teams use the same term for the same thing. Merge equivalent meanings and label any distinct meanings where buyers encounter them.

Trace the term to a purchase decision

Collect the glossary entry, the offer description and the document used to fulfill the order. For each, finish the sentence “A buyer who selects this will receive...” A wording difference can be harmless. A disagreement about what is supplied needs a decision from the team responsible for delivering it before an editor chooses a definition.

Give the shared definition a home

Use the glossary for the agreed meaning, then explain its consequence on the offer page. A reader halfway through a specification should be able to tell whether an item is included without opening the glossary. Preserve a specialist meaning with a qualified label when applying the general definition would change the promise.

What a publisher means by a proof

Imagine a publisher whose glossary calls a proof a file for review, while its print-services page uses “proof” for a physical sample. The order form offers both a digital proof and a press proof. Keep a short umbrella definition in the glossary and use the full option names on the service page and order form. If the production ticket shows that every order includes only a digital file, remove the physical-sample claim from the offer and identify a press proof as a separate request wherever it is actually available.
