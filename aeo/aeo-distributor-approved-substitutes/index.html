Approved substitutes in AEO content for industrial distributors

A distributor publishing part cross-references should answer the question purchasing has to act on. Can the buyer request this alternate under its existing specification, or does engineering need to approve a change? In AEO copy, identify who gave any approval and the application it covers.

Keep the two records distinct

A manufacturer's comparison table may identify parts worth considering. A customer's approved parts list answers a separate purchasing question about a particular assembly. Label the source of a match wherever it appears. A product card headed “approved equivalent” leaves the buyer guessing whose decision it records.

Let the inquiry carry the original requirement

Keep the specified part number in the quote request even if the buyer selects an alternate. Give purchasing a place to record the proposed substitute and supply its approval reference privately. That preserves the comparison for the sales team and avoids turning a public cross-reference into a customer-specific approval claim.

Two switches share a catalog cross-reference

Imagine a distributor's line card links a discontinued switch to a current model. The buyer's bill of materials names the old part and requires written approval for substitutions. The distributor can offer the current model for review and provide its data sheet. If the buyer supplies a current approved parts list naming that model for the assembly, sales can quote the alternate against that record and identify it by its own part number.
