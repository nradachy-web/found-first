Resolving part number conflicts in B2B catalog GEO

When two manufacturers use the same part number, commission GEO content that identifies the maker alongside the number wherever buyers compare the parts. Keep a separate entry for each until you have evidence for the particular substitution a customer wants to make.

Give the number an owner

A distributor's stock code, a manufacturer's part number and the number stamped on an old component can all appear on one invoice. Preserve those labels when publishing a lookup. Otherwise a search for an exact string can lead a buyer to a plausible-looking page with no way to tell whose numbering system it uses.

Decide what a cross-reference actually covers

A buyer matching a worn part needs to know the application and the limits of the match. Ask the editor to keep any equipment or installation restrictions beside the cross-reference. A distributor order history documents what someone bought. It takes additional evidence to say that the purchase fit the machine.

A drawer slide with two possible matches

Imagine a hardware wholesaler finds two suppliers' drawer slides filed under the same bare part number. The drawings show equal closed lengths, but the mounting holes differ. The editor gives each slide its manufacturer's name and drawing, then asks buyers to compare the hole pattern before ordering. If a cabinet maker's service bulletin later approves both slides for a particular cabinet fitted with an adapter, add that cabinet and adapter to the lookup. A blanket equivalence claim would still reach customers with other cabinets.
