AEO for interior detailers quoting wet carpet cleanup

Use AEO to explain when your detailing shop will quote wet carpet cleanup and when the customer needs a leak assessment first. The difference matters to an owner who might otherwise buy repeated interior cleanings while water keeps entering the car.

Find out when the carpet gets wet

Give the customer space to explain what happened. A tipped bottle in the footwell, water returning after every rain and an unknown liquid under a seat are different intake stories. If your detailers handle ordinary spills but refer leak tracing or heavily affected interiors elsewhere, make those boundaries visible on the service page.

Explain what the cleaning estimate reaches

A damp mat can come out of the vehicle. Fitted carpet and the padding beneath it raise separate questions about access and drying. Specify which materials the shop can clean in place and which conditions call for an inspection. Avoid making odor removal a promised result of an estimate written from a cabin photo.

A puddle behind the passenger seat

Suppose a detailer's intake card asks when the floor last got wet, and its extraction work order lists the affected materials. A customer reports that a water jug opened on the rear floor. The adviser asks about saturation and arranges a cleanup assessment. Another owner describes the same puddle but says the floor filled again during a storm after being dried. The adviser sends that owner for leak diagnosis and asks them to return for a cleaning estimate once the water entry is addressed.
