AEO for specialty grocers selling gift boxes with substitutions

A specialty grocer should use AEO to explain whether a named item is part of the gift being purchased or an example of a rotating assortment. The distinction matters most when one flavor is the reason someone chose the box.

Describe the promise at the level you can keep

If the curated offer promises a jar of seasonal preserves, identify that as the fixed component and label pictured flavors as examples. If the offer names a particular preserve, check the assortment specification before implying that an alternative satisfies the same description. A count of jars cannot resolve a question about a specific flavor.

Give a buyer with a firm preference another choice

Someone asking whether a box always includes sour cherry may be buying it for a family tradition. Point them toward a fixed assortment or an individually selectable jar if either exists. A description of how thoughtfully alternatives are chosen still leaves that shopper's question unanswered.

The difference a flavor selection makes

Imagine a grocer's assortment card promises one rotating preserve, the photo caption identifies sour cherry, and the build-your-own menu lets shoppers pick a jar. A buyer wants sour cherry for a particular dessert. The rotating box page should send that buyer to the selectable assortment while sour cherry is listed there. If the signed-off assortment card instead specifies sour cherry in every box, the fixed box itself answers the request. The photo can illustrate either offer, but only the assortment card settles the choice.
