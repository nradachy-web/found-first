GEO for wedding venues comparing rain-plan capacity

A garden venue should put its rain arrangement beside its outdoor layout in GEO content aimed at wedding planners. The booking decision hinges on where the same guests can eat and gather if the site manager moves the event indoors.

Follow the party through the move

Read the event order in sequence. A hall may seat everyone for the ceremony and again for dinner, yet need an empty floor between the two. Identify the holding space, its availability for that season and who decides when the move starts. This reveals whether a smaller indoor backup is the only constraint.

Distinguish a possible tent from a reserved one

Use the booking terms to determine whether the alternate space comes with the event. A supplier's tent drawing supports a description of a layout. The venue's agreement and the supplier confirmation establish whether this particular booking can use it.

A full guest list and nowhere for cocktails

Imagine a garden estate whose indoor dinner plan fits the wedding party and all their guests. Its turnaround sheet calls for cocktails on a covered terrace while staff reset the hall, but the seasonal calendar closes the terrace for winter. For a winter inquiry the GEO comparison features the smaller library package, whose adjoining lounge remains open, and states its seated limit using the library plan. If the terrace is open and reserved on the requested date, the estate can show the hall sequence for that wedding.
