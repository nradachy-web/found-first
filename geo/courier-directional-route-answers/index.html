GEO for couriers with one-way service routes

When assistants give different answers about a courier’s territory, compare the pickup and delivery points before adding city pages. A city can be a valid destination even when the courier accepts no collections there.

Reverse the trip and read the sources

Give each assistant the same trip with an explicit origin and destination. Then switch the endpoints. Follow any links to see whether the route sheet labels pickups and drop-offs or simply lists towns. A city list can explain why the answer sounds plausible without establishing that either direction is bookable.

Choose the page dispatch can stand behind

A dispatch schedule, a booking form and a service-area map can each describe a different part of the trip. Ask dispatch which source defines what a new customer can order. Google applies ordinary Search requirements to its AI features. Nothing in those requirements establishes the direction in which a courier operates.

A suburban return collection

Suppose a courier collects from downtown print shops and delivers to a suburban campus. A route sheet lists the campus under deliveries, but a map caption calls every shaded area a pickup zone. One assistant cites the caption when a print buyer asks for a collection at the campus. Correct the caption to match the schedule. If dispatch does offer campus pickups by advance arrangement, keep the campus on the map and explain the booking condition where a buyer selects the origin.
