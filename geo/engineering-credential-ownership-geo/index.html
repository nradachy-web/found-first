Whose engineering credential should your GEO page name?

Attribute an engineering qualification to the person or organization the issuer identifies. For GEO content, pair the issuer's record with a separate explanation of how that holder participates in the service your firm markets.

Read the record as a prospective client would

Use the issuer's exact designation and identify the holder in the same passage. Where the issuer provides a public directory, direct readers to the relevant entry. Check that the recorded status still supports the wording. A course attendance certificate and a professional designation may both belong on a biography, but each calls for its own description.

Connect the holder to the proposed work

A buyer comparing regional offices may want a qualified person involved in the assignment itself. Explain whether the credential holder leads the work locally, reviews it from another office or joins by arrangement. Staffing records or a description confirmed by the practice lead can support that part of the page. The issuer's record answers only the qualification question.

One specialist serving several offices

Imagine an engineering firm has an acoustics specialist in its central office. The issuing body's directory confirms her designation, and a regional proposal assigns her the final technical review. Credit her by name and describe that review on the regional service page. If future proposals offer her input only on request, adjust the service description to explain how clients arrange it. Her qualification can remain on her biography through either staffing arrangement while the regional promise changes.
