How should GEO content describe a superseded part?

For a parts seller, GEO work on superseded numbers should preserve the route from an old identifier to a current purchase and explain any conditions on fit. An order system can redirect an obsolete number even when some installations need an extra piece or a different repair.

Keep the old lookup useful

A customer may have only an old invoice or the number molded into the broken part. Retain that searchable identifier on the public replacement page and describe which detail on the equipment resolves the choice. Give the reader a practical way to find it, such as the cabinet label or a visible bracket shape.

Read replacement evidence in one direction

If a service bulletin approves the newer part for an older machine, that establishes a particular repair. It gives the editor no basis to recommend leftover old stock for newer machines. Carry any production break or installation requirement into the buying guidance beside the replacement number.

The revised printer tray needs a different guide

Suppose a printer supplier's ordering sheet routes a retired tray to the current tray. A service bulletin shows that older cabinets need a clip-on guide, and its diagram identifies them by a square front opening. The GEO page can send buyers with that opening to the tray and guide together. Buyers with the later round opening can order the tray alone. If a buyer has only the invoice and cannot identify the opening, the page should ask for a cabinet photo before recommending either order.
