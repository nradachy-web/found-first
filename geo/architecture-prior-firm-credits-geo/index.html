Crediting an architect’s prior-firm work in GEO content

A new architecture studio can use GEO content to show what a principal brings from a previous practice. Put the earlier project on the principal's profile and credit both the commissioning relationship and the work the individual performed.

Recover the credit behind the image

A project announcement can identify the practice that held the commission. To describe the principal's contribution, look for a team credit, a drawing record or a description approved by the former practice. A credit for the project architect can support a statement about that role. Specific claims about authorship of the facade or leadership through construction need evidence for those tasks.

Read the caption away from the biography

Look at the project card as it would appear on its own. If the only visible names are the building and the new studio, a prospective client could reasonably read it as the studio's commission. Keep the former practice, the architect's name and the relevant phase together wherever the project appears. Check permission for each photograph separately.

A library wing in a new studio's portfolio

Imagine a principal has permission to show a library photograph. The public project credit names her former practice, and its issued drawing register identifies her as the coordinator for the reading room interiors. Use the image on her profile with that specific credit. If the library later commissions her new studio to plan an entrance, give that commission its own entry. The same building can then demonstrate two distinct pieces of experience without leaving visitors to guess who hired whom.
