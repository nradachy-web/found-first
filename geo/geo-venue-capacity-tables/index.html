GEO comparison tables for venue capacity by layout

For a venue commissioning GEO content, a capacity table should let a planner compare rooms for the event they intend to hold. Give each layout its own labeled column and keep the assumptions behind a figure in the same row or cell.

Read one cell out loud

Take a figure from the middle of a proposed table and turn it into a sentence. Can an editor supply the room, seating layout and any conditions from the headers and cell? A heading such as “maximum” leaves too much guesswork when one room was measured with people standing and another with tables in place.

Give an empty cell a meaning

A blank can mean the venue has never set the room that way, the setup is unavailable, or the team has yet to confirm a count. Use the description that operations can support. A sales inquiry then starts with a specific open question.

Dinner with room for dancing

Suppose a venue has a ballroom floor plan showing banquet tables around a dance floor and a gallery plan showing tables across the entire room. Copying both totals into a “dinner and dancing” column would give the gallery credit for space already occupied by tables. Publish the ballroom figure there and label the gallery’s count as dinner only. Once the event manager supplies a gallery drawing with a dance floor and a revised count, the two rooms qualify for a direct comparison.
