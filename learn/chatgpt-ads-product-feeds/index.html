A catalog is not a single advertising proposition

Products in the same store can have very different buying requirements. One may be a straightforward replacement item; another may need comparison, sizing or substantial explanation. Treating the entire assortment as one offer can hide the differences that determine whether an ad visit is useful.

Found First helps connect paid catalog distribution with merchandising judgment. For a retailer carrying both everyday essentials and specialist equipment, the strongest candidate assortment may depend on the destination, margin considerations and how much context buyers need. We do not assume that more advertised items automatically mean a better acquisition program.

Keep the paid-feed promise narrow and accurate

OpenAI describes product-feed campaigns as a retail advertising capability, particularly relevant to broad or frequently changing catalogs. Its current beta guidance says products submitted for this advertising use do not thereby appear in organic ChatGPT conversations. That distinction should be visible in any proposal or internal business case.

Our managed ChatGPT advertising service addresses the paid opportunity. If your goal also includes organic product discovery, that is a separate workstream with its own expectations. A successful feed import establishes that product information was processed; it does not establish commercial performance, organic recommendation or suitability for every buyer conversation.

Merchandising conditions belong in the decision

A product can be eligible to advertise while being a poor acquisition choice for your business. Limited stock, unsuitable variants or a destination that obscures purchase conditions may reduce the value of additional attention. These are reasons to review the offer, not simply to ask the channel for more traffic.

Your team should be able to explain which products it wants to sell and what a customer must know before ordering. Found First brings those commercial facts into campaign planning and destination review. The advertising should represent an offer the store can fulfill, with enough clarity that the click does not begin a search for the real product.

Evaluate purchases beyond the first reported number

A retail campaign can generate interest in products that customers later return, replace or abandon. Those business outcomes matter when deciding what to keep promoting. Advertising reporting and store records answer related questions, but they should not be collapsed into one unexamined total.

Found First can discuss how paid catalog work fits the way you already judge acquisition quality. That includes the difference between product engagement, reported purchases and orders your business considers successful. A founder consultation can clarify whether feed campaigns, a more focused product offer or destination improvements are the better next investment for your assortment.

Before you decide.

Do we need a large catalog for this to be useful?+

Catalog size alone is not the deciding factor. The question is whether feed-based distribution serves a meaningful assortment and whether the buying information is dependable. A small set of distinct offers may need a different advertising approach.

Will product-feed ads improve our organic ChatGPT rankings?+

Paid product advertising should not be purchased on that promise. OpenAI's current feed-campaign guidance separates the beta advertising use from organic conversations. Any organic visibility engagement should be assessed independently.

What should merchandising contribute before a campaign discussion?+

A clear view of the products worth promoting, their purchase conditions and the limitations advertising must respect. Found First does not need a public operating playbook to explain the service, but the private scope must reflect the store's actual commercial priorities.
