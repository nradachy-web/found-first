AEO for contract security firms staffing visitor desks

A contract security firm selling lobby coverage should use AEO to explain the authority that comes with a staffed desk. Buyers can compare proposals more accurately when they know who checks in a visitor, who approves the visit and who can change the admission instructions.

Write the answer around the decision maker

Use a public description of the division of responsibilities in your approved service scope. For example, an officer may verify that a host has approved a visitor and issue a temporary badge under the client’s policy. The person handing over the badge may have no authority to approve the visit.

List the duties a facilities team needs to specify

A facilities brief that says lobby coverage may also assume reception work, delivery intake or badge administration. Tell buyers which duties to list separately when requesting service. Publish enough to distinguish those assignments while keeping property-specific admission procedures in the site instructions.

A shared office lobby with several tenants

Consider a guard company preparing an answer from a sample desk assignment and a building manager’s tenant responsibility chart. Tenant hosts approve their guests and the officer handles check-in. The page can illustrate that arrangement, giving the manager a concrete basis for requesting desk staffing. If the buyer also expects the officer to approve unlisted guests, the proposal needs an explicit decision about delegated authority and an agreed scope before the firm accepts that duty. Extending the coverage hours would leave the approval question unresolved.
