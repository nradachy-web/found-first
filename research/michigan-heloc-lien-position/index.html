Does a HELOC always come second? Michigan’s mortgage records give an answer.

Of 46,741 qualifying Michigan home equity lines originated in 2025, 12,539 were recorded in first lien position. See the counts by loan purpose.

Found First Research · November 25, 2026

About one in four occupied first lien position.

Ask whether a home equity line of credit is a second mortgage and the first question back should be whether there is already a mortgage on the home. Across the 46,741 Michigan records we counted, 34,202 lines had a subordinate lien. The other 12,539, or 26.8%, held the first lien.

A lien position describes the order of claims against a property. A line of credit describes how the borrower can draw funds. Those are two different facts about a loan. The CFPB explains how adding a home equity line alongside an existing first mortgage creates a second mortgage. CFPB explanation of home equity borrowing.

What a mortgage broker can answer

A borrower asking “Can I keep my current mortgage?” and someone asking “Can I open a line on a paid-off home?” deserve separate answers. A broker can explain which situations it handles, who supplies current terms and how to request a quote. The statewide share gives that second question a reason to be on the page. It cannot establish an individual borrower’s eligibility.

AEO and GEO for mortgage brokers. For a commercial example selected by Found First, see DynaGen Lending’s HELOC product page. All figures in this study come from federal records.

Which records counted?

We downloaded the 2025 Home Mortgage Disclosure Act snapshot for Michigan properties, filtered to originated loans. The file contained 229,505 originations. We retained records explicitly coded as open-end credit, principal residence, one to four units, non-reverse and primarily noncommercial. Records with exempt or missing values for any required classification fell outside the count. We grouped the remaining records by reported loan purpose and lien position.

Each record represents an origination reported by a covered institution. HMDA reporting coverage and exemptions limit the population. This table measures recorded lines, not individual borrowers, draw activity or broker market share. The figures describe 2025 and do not quote current terms.
