AEO for shirt brands offering pocket monograms

When a shirt brand offers monograms, give its AEO writer the combinations the workroom can actually make. Someone asking whether initials will sit on the pocket needs more than a preview of letters floating over the chest.

Describe the spot on the finished shirt

Use the workroom's placement name and explain it in terms a shopper can picture. Initials on the pocket face, above the pocket and on the cuff describe different finished shirts. Identify the shirt configuration in a sample photo, especially when the same photo appears beside both pocketed and pocketless styles.

Follow the choice through the selector

Check what happens to an embroidery choice when the buyer changes the base shirt. If the site keeps a pocket-face option selected after the pocket disappears, rewriting the caption will leave an impossible order available. Make the permitted replacement locations visible while the shopper can still change their selection.

Initials on an Oxford shirt

Suppose the sewing diagram for an Oxford shows initials centered on the pocket before the pocket is attached. Its option sheet also permits cuff embroidery on both shirt versions. For the customer who wants initials on the pocket itself, the page can specify the pocketed shirt and the pocket-face selection. If the customer switches to the pocketless front, remove the pocket placement and offer the documented cuff option. Moving the initials to the chest without asking would change the design they chose.
