AEO content that earns its place on your website.

An AEO content strategy maps real buying questions to useful pages. Each page should answer a distinct question, show why the answer is credible, and help the visitor decide what to do next.

By Found First · September 16, 2026 · AEO & AI marketing

Discuss this with Nick

The decision behind the search.

More pages do not necessarily make your business easier to understand. If five pages repeat the same offer with slightly different titles, the buyer still lacks the details needed to choose. Start with the gaps your sales conversations reveal: compatibility, price drivers, implementation, limitations, and proof.

A practical way to approach it.

01 / Collect actual questions

Review sales notes, customer support themes, search terms, and existing site queries. Group similar questions into a coherent page rather than creating a URL for every phrasing.

02 / Build around evidence

Use specifications, process details, demonstrations, attributable case studies, and named expertise. Make comparisons fair and keep time-sensitive facts dated.

03 / Maintain and connect the library

Link to relevant follow-up answers, update changed claims, and consolidate pages that no longer serve a distinct purpose. A maintained library is more useful than a publishing quota.

Platform guidance: Google Search Central and OpenAI: Ads in ChatGPT. Paid campaigns must satisfy current eligibility and policy requirements.

Questions to bring to the brief.

Use these as starting points and replace the bracketed terms with the specifics of your business.

  • What makes this product a good or poor fit?
  • What affects cost, timing, and implementation?
  • What evidence would help a skeptical buyer decide?

What the work can include.

We agree the scope around your starting point and priorities.

  • A prioritized question-to-page map.
  • Content briefs with evidence requirements.
  • Answer pages connected to the next buying step.
  • A maintenance and measurement plan.

Before you get started.

Can existing pages do the work?

Often, yes. Improving a relevant page can be more useful than adding a competing page with similar content.

Should every page include an FAQ?

Only when it helps readers. A clear article or comparison may answer the questions better than a long FAQ section.

Who supplies expertise?

We combine research with your first-hand product or service knowledge. Claims about performance, customers, and capabilities need supporting evidence.
