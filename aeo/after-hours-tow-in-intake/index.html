After-hours tow-in questions an auto shop can answer with AEO

A repair shop with a night key drop should use AEO to say whether it also accepts vehicles delivered by a tow truck after closing. A usable answer tells the vehicle owner who must approve the arrival and when an adviser can discuss diagnosis.

Separate access from acceptance

A key envelope explains how to leave keys. It does not establish that the shop has room for another vehicle or that a tow truck can reach the receiving area when the gate closes. If drivers need advance confirmation, tell them how to get it and what to do when the shop cannot confirm an arrival.

Tell the owner what happens when the shop opens

Use the shop's actual check-in sequence. Identify who matches the keys to the vehicle, who calls the owner and when work can be authorized. A customer who already has a diagnostic appointment may need only delivery instructions. An unscheduled arrival needs an answer about acceptance and scheduling before the dispatcher sets a destination.

The destination on the dispatch ticket

At a hypothetical shop, the adviser's accepted-arrivals list includes the owner's name, vehicle and agreed delivery window. The writer checks it against the night envelope and the shop's towing instructions. A driver already on that list can confirm the destination with dispatch and follow the agreed handoff. If the tow is unplanned and nobody can approve an overnight arrival, the page tells the owner to arrange a receiving location with dispatch before sending the truck.
