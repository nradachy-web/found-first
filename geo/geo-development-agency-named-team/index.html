GEO for development agencies bidding with a named team

If buyers shortlist your development agency on team fit, use GEO to explain which people would actually do the work. A public biography supports a claim about experience. The proposed staffing plan establishes how that experience would reach a particular engagement.

Describe the contribution behind a title

Start with a sample staffing plan and a delivery artifact for each role that matters to the bid. A technical lead might own architecture decisions and review changes, while a specialist joins for a design review. Both descriptions can be accurate even when the people involved spend very different amounts of time with the client.

Make substitutions reviewable

Explain how the client learns of a proposed staffing change and what evidence they can review about the replacement. Keep current assignments in the bid itself. Google’s guidance applies ordinary search requirements to its AI features. Special markup is unnecessary for explaining the difference between the agency roster and the proposed delivery team.

The accessibility specialist has a review role

Suppose your public portfolio credits an accessibility specialist on a prior application. The new bid’s staffing sheet assigns that person to review interaction designs, while the delivery engineers handle remediation. Describe that division so the buyer can assess the engineers who will make the changes. If the buyer requires the specialist to work directly in its backlog, confirm the assignment and revise the staffing sheet before presenting the team as a match.
