Technical knowledge should lead to an understandable offer

A company may publish excellent documentation yet leave its commercial role unclear. A reader can learn how a technology works without knowing whether the company sells a product, provides consulting or supports an existing system. That gap matters when research turns into vendor consideration.

For an infrastructure provider, a public guide might explain a deployment problem while never stating which part the provider actually handles. Found First helps make that business relationship legible. The aim is to connect accurate expertise with the real offer, so a researching prospect can decide whether the company belongs in a conversation about their requirements.

Search access is not the same decision as training access

Anthropic distinguishes its model-training crawler, search crawler and user-directed retrieval agent. A blanket policy described internally as blocking AI may therefore cover several different uses. Your marketing and technical teams should understand what the current policy permits before assuming the site can participate in search.

Found First can discuss discovery readiness with the people responsible for your website. That discussion respects the company's content and security decisions. Allowing search access is not a promise of inclusion, and a marketing objective does not require treating every crawler purpose as interchangeable. The relevant question is whether an intentional policy is being confused with an accidental barrier.

Publish enough specificity to support a buyer's judgment

Technical buyers may need to distinguish supported environments, deployment responsibilities or the boundary between a product and professional services. Those are business facts that deserve clear explanations. Unsupported compatibility claims and vague statements about enterprise readiness are poor substitutes for approved details.

Our content work helps translate those distinctions into useful public information without exposing proprietary implementation. A business can explain what it offers and where a prospect should ask for confirmation while keeping internal architecture private. The result should be a stronger evaluation experience on your own site, regardless of whether a particular research answer chooses to cite it.

Choose a scope that serves technical sales

Claude-specific work is most useful when it addresses a meaningful research situation. If a prospect has shared an inaccurate description, or a key capability is hard to understand publicly, there is a concrete issue to examine. General curiosity about a new assistant is a weaker basis for a large engagement.

Found First brings organic discovery and commercial website judgment together. A consultation can clarify whether the priority is technical access, an unclear product explanation or broader representation across AI search. We keep the final objective visible: a prospect who understands enough to make an appropriate inquiry, not a collection of screenshots detached from the sales process.

Before you decide.

Does blocking model training automatically block Claude search?+

Anthropic documents distinct agents for training, search and user-directed retrieval. The actual effect depends on your site's controls. A review should examine those purposes separately rather than infer the answer from a general statement that AI bots are blocked.

Can confidential technical documents be used for visibility?+

Public discovery work should rely on information your business is authorized to publish. Found First can help clarify the public offer without exposing customer environments, credentials or proprietary specifications. Private sales material can remain part of a later controlled conversation.

Is this an alternative to product documentation?+

No. Documentation serves customers and technical evaluators. Visibility work can help connect it with product identity and commercial fit, but it should preserve the accuracy and utility that make the documentation valuable in the first place.
