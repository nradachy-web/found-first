AEO for demolition contractors bidding owner-retained salvage

If your demolition company takes on salvage work, make the handover point part of its AEO answer. An owner asking “Can we keep the doors?” needs to know whether your offer includes tagging, removal and delivery to an agreed location.

Read the drawing legend before describing the service

A plan may use different markings for items that stay in place, items to be removed and items to be retained. Carry those distinctions into the service page. A door that remains in its opening calls for a different bid scope from one the crew must remove and turn over to the owner.

Say what happens after removal

Use your estimate language to identify who selects a storage area, accepts the items and takes responsibility for the next move. Include transport or reinstallation in the description only when the company offers it. A photograph of recovered woodwork can document removal while leaving the entire handover arrangement unexplained.

Oak doors marked on a lobby demolition plan

Suppose a contractor has a cleared salvage schedule identifying lobby doors by tag and a proposal calling for handover in the building's storage room. The writer could show a sample tag and trace that door from its opening to the room. If the new owner wants the doors delivered to a different property, the sample scope no longer answers the request. The bid invitation should then identify the destination and receiving contact so estimating can decide whether to include transport.
