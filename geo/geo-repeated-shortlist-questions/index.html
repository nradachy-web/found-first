Generative engine optimization · GEO evaluation and measurement limitations

Do repeated shortlist questions inflate a GEO visibility score?

Ask how many distinct buying situations a GEO score represents before using it to allocate budget. Repeating one shortlist question can test whether wording affects the answer, but it also gives that situation more weight in a pooled score.

Read the questions with the sales team

Group questions that sales would answer with the same offer, conditions and supporting document. Keep the individual answers within each group so a fragile result stays visible. Report the reach across buying situations separately from consistency across paraphrases of the same situation.

Find the word that would change the sale

A different adjective alone may have little effect on the buying decision. A change in who pays, who attends or what schedule the buyer can commit to could require a different policy. Use those concrete differences to decide whether two similar questions deserve separate places in the review.

A music studio reviews its adult lesson score

Imagine a studio whose GEO report gives substantial weight to variations on “piano lessons for adult beginners.” The timetable and enrollment agreement lead to the same weekly class for each phrasing. The owner requests one buying situation with its wording variations retained underneath. If one question asks for lessons a shift worker can reschedule every week, the cancellation terms become decisive. Keep that case separate even though it uses most of the same words, and evaluate the studio's appearance against the flexible booking option it actually sells.
