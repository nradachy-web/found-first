AEO for professional services

A professional firm is chosen on judgment a client cannot inspect in advance, so answer engine optimization for a firm is mostly evidence: named people with verifiable credentials, plain statements of who the firm does and does not act for, and explanations a careful reader could check. No one can guarantee that an assistant names a firm.

What is different about selling judgment

  • The client is checking a person

    People ask about a named lawyer, accountant, doctor or consultant, not only about the firm. Each professional needs a page of their own: license or registration, the body that issued it, what they actually work on and what they have written.

  • The question starts as a problem

    A prospective client asks whether they need a professional at all, what it will cost and what happens first. The useful page answers honestly, including when the answer is that they may not need you.

  • A poor fit costs more than a missed inquiry

    Say who you act for and who you do not: matter types, client size, jurisdictions, minimum engagement. A clear no saves an intake call and gives an answer something accurate to say about you.

  • Some ordinary marketing may be off limits

    Results, testimonials, comparisons and words such as specialist may be restricted by your profession's rules, and confidentiality limits the examples you can use. Plan the evidence around what your rules permit.

Turn each claim into something a client can check

Most of what a firm says about itself cannot be verified by a stranger. For each claim, publish the thing that lets someone confirm it.

Questions prospective clients put to an assistant

Test these with your own profession, matters and jurisdiction in the brackets. They are example shapes, not measured search data.

  • Do I need a [type of professional] for [situation], or can I handle it myself?
  • How do [professionals] usually charge for [matter], and what affects the cost?
  • Who handles [specific matter] in [jurisdiction] for [type of client]?
  • What should I ask a [professional] before I hire one for [matter]?
  • Is [named professional] licensed in [jurisdiction], and what do they focus on?
  • What should I bring to a first meeting about [matter]?

Check your regulator's rules before you publish

This is a list of questions to take to your own rules. It is not legal or compliance advice. Rules differ by profession and by jurisdiction, and the current text from your regulator is the authority.

Found First drafts with these limits in mind and flags what needs a decision. A responsible professional at the firm approves every page before it is published.

  • Past resultsDo your rules allow you to publish outcomes, and what must accompany them?
  • Testimonials and reviewsAre client testimonials permitted, and may you ask for reviews or reply to them?
  • Words about expertiseWhich terms, such as specialist or expert, need a certification or are restricted?
  • ComparisonsMay you compare your firm with others, and what substantiation is required?
  • Required wordingDo advertisements need a label, a disclaimer, a named responsible person or a retained copy?
  • ConfidentialityHas every client whose matter could be recognized, even without a name, given written permission?
  • Information, not adviceDoes educational content say it is general information and explain how to get advice on a specific situation?
  • Client and patient dataDo your privacy obligations allow the forms, analytics and advertising tools on pages where people describe their situation?

Where a rule is unclear, ask your regulator or professional body. We do not interpret conduct rules for clients.

What a firm can measure

Count qualified consultations, not contacts. A firm that declines most of its inquiries gains nothing from more of the ones it declines, so intake should record whether each inquiry fit the scope you published and how the person says they found you.

Research adds two things intake cannot see: whether assistants describe the firm and its people accurately, and for which client questions the firm is named. Where work arrives by referral, a referred client who checks the firm with an assistant before calling leaves no trace in analytics.

Report mentions, accuracy and consultations as separate lines. How to measure AI marketing covers the method, and how to choose an AEO agency covers what to ask any agency before you hire one.

Questions from professional firms

Can we publish client results or testimonials?+

Only as your professional rules and each client's permission allow. That is a question for your regulator's current text, not for a marketing agency. Process detail, credentials and published work can carry a page without any client outcome.

Who checks the content against our conduct rules?+

The firm does. We flag anything that reads as a result, a comparison or a claim of specialization, and a responsible professional at the firm approves each page before it goes live. We do not give legal or compliance advice.

Should each professional have their own page and business listing?+

A page for each professional, yes: credentials, focus and published work in one place. Google's guidelines let a public-facing practitioner who can be contacted directly at the location hold a Business Profile separate from the firm's, and say a practitioner should not hold several profiles to cover different specializations.

Are ChatGPT ads available to professional firms?+

It depends on the profession. As checked on November 11, 2026, OpenAI's ad policies permit legal services ads in the US only where the advertiser is licensed in the jurisdiction where the ad is shown, and treat financial and health services as restricted categories approved case by case, where proof of licensure may be required. OpenAI also says ads are not eligible to appear near sensitive topics such as personal health, and your own regulator's advertising rules apply to any ad you run.

Will you promise outcomes for a regulated service?+

No. We do not guarantee rankings, mentions or new clients, and we do not write outcome promises into a firm's content.

What if an assistant says something wrong about our firm?+

Start with the facts you publish: firm and practitioner pages, business profiles and directory entries. Correct what is wrong at its source, then test the same question again and record what changed. We report what we observe and cannot promise a correction.
