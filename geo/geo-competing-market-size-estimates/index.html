Comparing rival market-size estimates in GEO content

Publish competing estimates side by side only after you can name the market each measures. For GEO work, approve a comparison that helps a buyer choose the appropriate figure for a decision. The midpoint between differently defined markets has little meaning.

Write out the unit in each column

Pull the unit, boundary, geography and reference period from each source's methods. Annual deliveries measure a flow over time. An installed base counts equipment in use at a point in time. Both can illuminate demand, but choosing the smaller figure as the low end of a range suggests the sources estimated the same thing.

Keep disagreement visible when it is real

Two teams may estimate the same quantity differently because one uses a partial registry and another extrapolates from interviews. Give each estimate its source and collection method. Describe a difference as unexplained if the available notes cannot account for it.

Counting presses for a parts distributor

Suppose your publishing team has a shipment survey for industrial presses and a field census of presses still operating. A distributor evaluating replacement-parts demand needs the active fleet, so give the census the lead and use shipments to discuss newly delivered equipment. Now suppose the shipment survey includes used presses resold between plants. Even its value as an indicator of new equipment has changed. The editor should label it as deliveries including resales or extract the documented new-unit subset before making that comparison.
