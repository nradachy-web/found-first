Can a publisher credit a GEO citation gain to a site edit?

Treat a new citation after a site edit as an observed change. Before crediting the edit, check which fact the answer used, where that fact was available and what else appeared between the two reviews.

Look for the signature of the edit

Compare the archived page with the revision. A new footnote defining an exclusion gives you something specific to trace through the next answer. A headline rewrite followed by a citation to a long-standing table is a weaker basis for saying the rewrite helped.

Keep competing explanations in the decision

Check the sources named in the later answer for independent changes. Even a close textual match can establish use of a detail more readily than it can establish why the page was selected. For Google AI features, ordinary search requirements apply and meeting them offers no assurance of inclusion.

A benchmark gains a footnote and a trade association summary

Suppose a research publisher clarifies that its occupancy benchmark excludes seasonal closures. A later answer cites the report, but a trade association has also issued a summary explaining that exclusion. If the answer cites both, approve the note as a correction readers needed and leave its effect on citation frequency unresolved. If the answer quotes a distinctive worked calculation available only in the revised report and links to that calculation, record evidence that the new material informed this answer. That supports keeping the calculation available.
