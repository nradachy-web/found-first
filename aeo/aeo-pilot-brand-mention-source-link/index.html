What counts in an AEO pilot, a brand mention or a source link?

If you bought the pilot to help buyers identify possible suppliers, a named mention can count even when the link goes elsewhere. If you bought it to help buyers verify a condition of purchase, follow the link and check what it actually supports.

Read the sentence that names you

A list of possible vendors, an explanation of a competitor's policy and a warning about a discontinued service can all contain your company name. Save enough of the answer to show which role you played. For a shortlist question, credit the mention only if the answer presents your business as a plausible fit for the stated need.

Ask what the reader can verify

Open the cited page and find the passage a buyer would rely on. Your homepage might confirm who you are while saying nothing about the offer in question. Equally, your technical note could support an answer that never identifies you as a supplier. Count those observations separately when deciding whether the pilot met its objective.

A supplier makes the shortlist on someone else's evidence

Suppose a records storage company wants buyers to find a provider that retrieves individual cartons. An answer names the company but links to a trade directory that lists only its address. The team has an example of shortlist visibility. It still needs evidence that buyers can verify carton retrieval. If the link instead opens the company's service sheet and the sheet explains how to request a carton, the same answer satisfies both tests.
