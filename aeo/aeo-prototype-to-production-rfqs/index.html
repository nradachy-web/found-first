AEO for contract manufacturers quoting prototypes and production

If your team quotes both prototype and production work, use AEO to explain what transfers between the two. An accepted sample gives the buyer a reference point. Your answer should identify the evidence that lets an estimator consider a repeat order.

Distinguish the two buying decisions

A development team may need one part to check fit while the purchasing team needs a supplier willing to support repeat releases. Give each a recognizable route. Describe the conditions under which you accept development jobs, then identify the inputs you need to assess production, such as a released drawing, expected lot size and acceptance requirements.

Find the qualifications in the original order

Ask sales to look at a closed prototype quote. A temporary material substitution, limited inspection or pending design change may be recorded only in its exceptions. Explain on the site how those qualifications are reviewed when a customer asks to move the job into production.

The enclosure that passed its fit check

Suppose a buyer approved a sample enclosure and sends its sample acceptance email with a request for regular shipments. The prototype quote permitted a substitute finish for the fit check. If the released drawing still requires the original finish, route the files for a production quote with that requirement highlighted. If the buyer has formally adopted the sample finish, use the revised drawing as the production reference and assess the requested lots against it.
