Let the offer determine the useful geography

For a Rochester Hills design studio, customers may travel for a distinctive style and a specific kind of project. For a mobile repair business, the relevant geography is where service can be delivered. Applying the same local pitch to both would hide what makes each convenient or desirable.

An engagement is relevant when your business has a clear specialty but its location shorthand is doing too much of the explaining. Decide whether customers need to understand a destination, a service area or expertise that can travel. Then judge discovery against that relationship.

Questions worth asking.

Should we use Rochester and Rochester Hills interchangeably?+

They are distinct cities. A regional service relationship can include both, but the business should identify its actual location accurately. Familiar shorthand should not create an incorrect expectation about an address or a local presence.

Can a destination business compete beyond its immediate neighborhood?+

Yes, if the offer gives suitable customers a reason to travel. The relevant question is what makes the experience worth the trip. Local geography provides context; the company’s real specialty provides the commercial reason.

What if customers choose us for a specific project style?+

That distinction may be more important than broad category recognition. A buyer should be able to understand whether the company fits the project they have in mind, rather than treating every provider in the category as equally suitable.
