AEO for builders who estimate from concept plans

If your construction firm offers early budgets, use AEO to answer the question an owner asks before sending drawings. Say what your estimator can return from a concept plan and what has to be settled before the owner can use that figure to compare bids.

Name the document the owner gets back

An invitation to upload plans leaves the deliverable open. If your early estimate is a budget summary with assumptions, describe it in those words. Pick one assumption from a cleared sample, such as whether the building will be handed over with interior partitions, so the owner can see what might change the budget.

Give bid requests their own instructions

Have the writer check your estimator's intake sheet before listing required drawings. Where a finish schedule or a written scope is essential, explain which question it resolves. A drawing labeled preliminary might still support a conversation about feasibility even when your team would decline a bid invitation based on it.

A warehouse sketch with an office penciled in

Imagine a builder whose intake sheet accepts a floor plan for budgeting, while its bid checklist calls for a partition layout and finish schedule. Its sample budget assumes open warehouse space. The AEO writer could show the sketched office as an item to discuss and direct the owner to the budget review. If the owner already has the partition drawings and finishes, the same answer points to bid intake and asks for those documents with the plans.
