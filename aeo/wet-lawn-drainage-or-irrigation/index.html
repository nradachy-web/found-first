Landscaping AEO for drainage and irrigation service calls

When customers describe a wet lawn, your website can distinguish a drainage assessment from an irrigation service visit. Explain which observations route the inquiry to each crew. Naming both services without that distinction gives an answer engine little basis for choosing between them.

Give the homeowner an observation they can make

“We fix soggy lawns” leaves two crews answering the same call. A landscape company's service page can ask when the patch appears, where the homeowner first sees water and whether it recedes between events. Photos taken from the same window on a rainy afternoon and a dry morning can show a pattern. A single picture of mud cannot establish its cause.

Describe the assessment before naming a solution

Say whether the irrigation technician services existing systems and whether the drainage estimator evaluates runoff across the property. If the homeowner wants a drain installed, ask what they have observed at that spot. A request for a particular product can still belong with the person who evaluates the water's path.

Two explanations for water beside the patio

Imagine an estimator receives a photo of a wet patio edge and a sprinkler schedule. The owner says the patch reappears on dry mornings after the adjacent zone runs. The landscape firm's crew sheet lists a technician who services that system, so intake books an irrigation visit. If the system has been off and the owner instead has a rain video showing water arriving from the upper lawn, intake books the drainage estimator and attaches the video.
