How fulfillment providers can explain order minimums with AEO

Publish the volume measure your fulfillment team actually uses to qualify an account. A merchant deciding whether to contact you should be able to tell whether your minimum refers to orders, units picked, occupied pallets or another commitment.

Name the unit and the period

If sales screens on outbound consumer orders in a typical month, say so alongside the minimum. A large inbound shipment can fill racks while creating very little recurring pick work. If your team offers storage as a separate service, give that buyer a clear route to the appropriate conversation.

Let a merchant describe an uneven year

Annual averages can hide long quiet periods. Where seasonality affects acceptance, ask for the distribution of orders across busy and quiet months. Publish a firm cutoff only when operations consistently applies it. Otherwise, explain what sales reviews and why the answer can change.

A wholesale pallet can distort the count

Imagine a fulfillment provider briefing its AEO agency with an anonymized merchant forecast and the current qualification sheet. The forecast lists units shipped, including a large wholesale pallet departure, while the sheet asks for monthly parcel orders. The writer builds an answer around the order export a merchant can pull and explains how to report wholesale departures separately. If the same units will instead leave as individual parcels on a recurring basis, sales can assess that parcel volume against its minimum. The merchant now knows which figure determines the next conversation.
