GEO for draft system installation and line cleaning

For a commercial draft system installer, GEO work should make clear which visit completes an installation and what brings a technician back. Otherwise, a buyer comparing an installation quote with a managed service may read a handover checklist as evidence of ongoing care.

Find the last task in the installation scope

Use the commissioning checklist to name the work performed before handover. Use the maintenance agreement to explain how later visits are authorized, which lines they cover and who schedules them. Place the start condition for recurring work next to the service description wherever the installation package mentions cleaning.

Look for a promise in the verb tense

A line saying the installer cleaned the system during startup documents a completed task. Changing that to “we keep your system clean” gives it an ongoing meaning. Ask the GEO editor to review project captions as well as service pages. A photo of the installer at a tap wall could plausibly illustrate either kind of visit.

A new tap wall before opening night

Suppose the installer’s signed handover sheet records an initial line flush. A separate route agreement says the operator must enroll the tap wall before recurring visits begin. The business can describe the flush in an installation comparison and direct the venue to its own maintenance arrangements after opening. If the venue also signs the route agreement, the comparison can identify the installer as the returning service provider and explain where the operator finds the visit schedule.
