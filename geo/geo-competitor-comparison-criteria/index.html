Reading a competitor's first-place finish in a GEO comparison

Ask what made the competitor the preferred choice before commissioning a rebuttal. A GEO comparison can contain accurate product facts and still favor a buying priority your customer never specified.

Separate requirements from preferences

Underline requirements in the buyer's question. Then identify the deciding reason given in the answer and trace its factual part to the cited material. Evidence that a vendor offers a feature establishes availability. The value a particular buyer assigns to that feature requires a separate judgment.

Choose a fair comparison to commission

If the deciding preference was left open, ask for a comparison that keeps the original requirements and makes the tradeoff explicit. If a competitor wins on a stated requirement that you also satisfy, check whether the report overlooked a public term or relied on a vague marketing phrase. Only the latter reveals an obvious writing assignment.

Ticketing for a theater with its own box office

Imagine a ticketing provider evaluating an answer about reserved seating for small theaters. Both vendors' seat-map guides show assigned seats. The answer favors the rival for having a staffed evening hotline. A theater with its own evening staff may prefer either vendor, so the provider asks for the hotline preference to be labeled in the comparison. If the buyer specifically requires evening phone support and only the rival's contract includes it, accept the result for that buying situation.
