Evaluating GEO bids for a map publisher’s Google AI eligibility

A map publisher can rule out one proposed GEO deliverable up front. Google says its AI features in Search use ordinary search requirements and require no special markup. Ask the agency to show which public map or index a buyer should be able to find and what currently stands in the way.

Name the page the buyer would use

A catalog entry can establish the territory covered by a walking map. A sample sheet can show whether individual footpaths are labeled. Either page might be relevant to an answer about the map, but a buyer deciding whether the map has enough detail needs to inspect the sample.

Keep the acceptance criterion within reach

Write a deliverable the cartography team can verify. For example, a visitor reading a county entry can identify its map series and open the matching sample without guessing the edition. A promise that Google will cite the sample cannot serve the same purpose. Google’s guidance concerns its Search experiences, so it also cannot establish eligibility in another assistant.

An index that opens the wrong sample

Suppose an AI Overview links to the publisher’s county index when asked whether a trail map shows footbridges. A GEO bid proposes adding AI-specific markup. The editor tries the existing sample link and finds it opens the road atlas. Correcting the link gives buyers a way to inspect the trail symbols themselves. If it already opens the correct sheet and footbridges do not appear in its legend, the editor should clarify the map’s level of detail before describing it as suitable for that trip.
