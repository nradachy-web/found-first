AEO for dealer trade-ins quoted on manufacturer websites

If a manufacturer’s website starts the trade-in journey, use AEO to identify who stands behind the result. The page should let a shopper tell whether they have a reference estimate, a request for a dealer appraisal or an offer a named dealership has agreed to honor under stated conditions.

Read what arrives after the valuation

Follow the estimate through to the email or printout the shopper brings to the sales desk. Look for an issuing business, vehicle details, inspection conditions and participating stores. When the document names no buying dealer, tell readers who can inspect the vehicle and issue an offer before suggesting they travel.

Handle the store-switch question explicitly

A shopper may complete the form for one dealer and find the replacement vehicle at another. Say whether the appraisal follows them, based on the actual program terms. If the second store must appraise the vehicle, give its used-vehicle contact and say which information from the first request the buyer can reuse.

The preferred van is at a different dealership

Suppose a manufacturer’s estimator produces a worksheet labelled with mileage and trim. A participating dealer inspects the trade-in and issues its own signed offer. The shopper then finds the van they want at another store. The program sheet says each dealership purchases its own trade-ins, so the second store page invites a fresh appraisal using the worksheet details. If the buyer stays with the issuing dealer and the recorded conditions still hold, the answer sends them back with the signed offer reference.
