ChatGPT advertising · Creative and message testing

Curiosity headlines in ChatGPT ads for paid workshops

Test a curiosity headline only if the reader can still identify the paid offer before clicking. A teaser that sounds like the title of a free lesson may attract readers who have little reason to buy the workshop.

Leave the answer open and the offer clear

Choose a question the class genuinely helps participants answer. Put the workshop format and any defining requirement in both treatments. The direct version can state the skill participants will practice, while the question version lets the reader recognize a problem they have encountered.

Decide how to read an ambiguous result

Use completed bookings linked to the campaign where your checkout records allow it. OpenAI’s reported conversions depend on event setup and attribution. If the tracked event is a syllabus download, stronger download activity supports a conclusion about interest in the syllabus. It leaves the team’s choice of booking message unresolved.

A question about a hollow-sounding recording

Imagine an audio school with a mixing syllabus, a checkout receipt and a class exercise on phase cancellation. Its question-led ad asks why a mix sounds hollow in mono and identifies the paid mixing workshop. The direct version offers practice diagnosing phase problems in that same workshop. If the question draws syllabus readers who never reach a linked booking, keep the direct message for enrollment while the team investigates. If completed bookings favor the question across comparable runs, retain it with the workshop label intact.
