How should GEO content describe a manufacturer's certified sites?

Put the certification claim where a sourcing team chooses the facility and the work it wants performed. For a manufacturer buying GEO services, a revealing editorial test is whether a writer can take the certificate and identify which operations it actually covers.

Read the scope and any site schedule together

Match the certificate holder to the legal entity, check the facility address and read the activities within scope. If an attached schedule assigns different activities to different sites, carry those distinctions onto the capability pages. A certificate for a facility's quality management system supports a claim about that system. A buyer asking for approval of a particular product will need different evidence.

Keep the evidence useful when a buyer forwards the page

Identify the issuer, document reference and applicable site in the text near the claim. Provide the current certificate or a verification route the buyer can use. Ask who will update that page when a site moves, the scope changes or the supporting document reaches the end of its validity.

Machining at one plant, assembly at another

Imagine a company quoting machined and assembled housings. Its certificate lists the machining plant and describes machining in scope. The editor can show that evidence on the machining page and identify where final assembly takes place on the quote page. If a revised certificate and schedule later include assembly at the second address, extend the claim there using the newly listed activity. A shared parent company or logo alone supplies no basis for that edit.
