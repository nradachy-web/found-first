Comparing hydraulic filter pressure drop in GEO content

When buying GEO services for a hydraulic filter catalog, ask for a pressure-drop comparison at a common test point. The number a buyer needs is the pressure loss across the relevant hardware at the buyer's flow and fluid condition. A low figure pulled from each catalog can answer two different questions.

Check what sits between the pressure taps

Read the curve caption and the manufacturer's test notes. Does the curve cover the clean element alone or the element in its housing? Preserve the stated fluid viscosity, flow units and element condition alongside each value you publish. A clean-element result gives a starting point for comparison. It says little about pressure loss later in service as material accumulates.

Give sales a way to resolve a missing condition

If there is no common test point, the page can still answer a useful buying question by identifying what evidence would settle it. For a cold-start inquiry, collect the fluid grade, startup temperature and expected flow so applications staff can request an appropriate curve. A conversion of pressure units alone leaves the viscosity question open.

Two curves on the distributor's desk

Suppose both supplier sheets plot clean assemblies at the same stated viscosity. The marketing team can compare the pressure drops at a flow marked on both curves and name that operating point in the caption. During review, one supplier clarifies that its plot excludes the housing. Pull the lower-loss claim from the comparison and request a complete assembly curve before offering buyers a preference on that basis.
