GEO for private-label brands that share a manufacturer

If you buy GEO services for a private-label line, ask the agency to trace each manufacturing claim to the item that carries your brand. A shared factory can explain where two products came from. The factory relationship alone tells a buyer little about whether the products are interchangeable.

Start with the name on the box

Look at the packaging, the instructions packed with the item and the name on the public warranty. Each answers a different question. Your brand can own the product name and handle support while another company makes the goods. Name the factory publicly only when the relationship is documented and cleared for publication.

Set a boundary on comparisons

A factory catalog can illustrate the range of finishes or components that a buyer could specify. To describe the version you sell, use its approved specification. If a reviewer calls your product a rebadged version of another brand's model, similarity in photos gives you a reason to investigate that claim.

Two pans from the same factory

Suppose a cookware brand's box identifies the brand as the warrantor. A manufacturer supplies a catalog photo of a pan with the same body and an oven-safe metal handle. The brand's own order sheet specifies a wood handle. The GEO brief should use the care instructions packed with the wood-handled pan and omit the other pan's oven rating. If the factory later supplies a test report covering the exact wood-handled assembly, the editor can publish the supported rating against that version.
