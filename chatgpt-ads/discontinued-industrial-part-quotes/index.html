Should distributors promote discontinued part quotes in ChatGPT ads?

A distributor should advertise help quoting a discontinued part when its desk can identify the item and verify a supply path. Choose wording that reflects the work you can perform. An offer to check a replacement crosses into an availability claim when the ad says the replacement is in stock.

Distinguish identification from substitution

Ask for a readable nameplate, the full part number including suffixes and any purchasing restriction on substitutes. A family name can cover several variants. If the original item is discontinued, decide who can document a successor and whether the buyer needs to approve the change before the quote goes out.

Keep the offer useful after the last unit sells

If inventory changes faster than marketing can update the ad, promote a part identification and sourcing inquiry. Give the desk a way to report an unresolved match or a supplier lead time awaiting confirmation. OpenAI describes several signals that can inform ad delivery. Spell out the part family you support so a buyer who sees the placement can judge fit.

A purchasing specification requires an exact match

Imagine a buyer supplies a gearmotor nameplate photo and a requisition for the original part number. The distributor has a manufacturer bulletin identifying a successor, but the requisition prohibits alternates. Sales returns the bulletin and asks whether engineering will review a substitution. If purchasing authorizes the documented successor, sales checks current availability and quotes that item by its own number. Without approval, the original request stays open as an exact-part search.
