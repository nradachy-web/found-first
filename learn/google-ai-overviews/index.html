Google AI Overviews and AI Mode

Google says there are no additional requirements and no special optimizations for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode. To be shown as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet, and Google adds that indexing and serving are not guaranteed. The work is ordinary search fundamentals done properly, not a new file or a new kind of markup.

[ definition ]

AI Overviews and AI Mode are features of Google Search that answer a query with an AI-generated response and links to the web pages that support it.

An AI Overview sits on the results page. Google says one is shown only when its systems determine it adds to classic Search, so many searches show none. AI Mode is a more interactive response that people can follow with further questions.

How the two features differ

The requirements for a page are the same for both. What differs is what each feature is for and how a visit from it is recorded.

What Google says a page needs

These three are the requirement, and Google says there are no additional technical requirements. The fundamentals it lists as still worthwhile, from crawl access to structured data that matches the page, are set out on Answer engine optimization services.

  • The page is indexedA supporting link in an AI Overview or AI Mode has to be in Google's index. The URL Inspection tool in Search Console tests a specific page.
  • The page can show a snippetIt must be eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet. Google names nosnippet, data-nosnippet and max-snippet as the controls that limit snippets, so check that none is set by accident.
  • The page worksGoogle indexes only pages served with an HTTP 200 status that have indexable content.

How it shows in Search Console

Google says sites appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode are included in the overall search traffic in Search Console. They are reported in the Performance report, within the Web search type. The guidance describes them as part of the totals, not as a separate line.

Clicking a link to an external page from either feature counts as a click, and standard impression rules apply. Position works differently in each: every link inside one AI Overview shares the overview's single position, while AI Mode follows the same method as a results page. Search Console does not include data from Search Labs experiments.

Google also says that clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are higher quality, meaning those visitors are more likely to spend more time on the site. That is Google's statement about its own data. Check it against your own analytics before you rely on it, and see measuring AI marketing results for how we keep visits, mentions and inquiries apart.

What controls your content in these features

Google says AI is built into Search, so the control for crawling is the same one as for Search: robots.txt rules for Googlebot. To limit what is shown from a page, Google names the nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex controls. They apply to ordinary results as well, so limiting a page for AI features limits it everywhere in Search.

Google-Extended is a different control. Google describes it as a way to manage whether content may be used to train Gemini models and for grounding in Gemini Apps and Vertex AI, and says it does not affect a site's inclusion in Google Search and is not a ranking signal. Blocking it is a decision about those other systems, not about AI Overviews.

Changes take time. Google says recrawling can take anywhere from several days to several months, depending on how often its systems decide a page needs refreshing.

What our own research does not cover

Our published research does not cover Google's AI features. Our first study planned two Google model routes, both were unavailable, and they are excluded from the study's counts, not scored as zero.

Questions about AI Overviews and AI Mode

Do I need an llms.txt file or special schema to appear in AI Overviews?+

No. Google says you do not need new machine readable files, AI text files or markup to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that there is no special schema.org structured data to add. Structured data you already use should match the visible text.

Will meeting every requirement get my page into an AI Overview?+

Not necessarily. Google says a page that meets all requirements and best practices may still not be crawled, indexed or served. Found First does not guarantee inclusion either.

Can I pay to appear in an AI Overview?+

Google describes no paid route to being a supporting link, and says it costs nothing to get a page into search results. Advertising is separate: Google Ads Help says ads can appear within or alongside AI Overviews and AI Mode, labeled Sponsored, in English in the United States and select English-speaking markets.

Can I see AI Overview clicks separately in Search Console?+

Google's guidance says sites appearing in AI features are included in overall search traffic, in the Performance report within the Web search type. We explain that data as it is and do not label a share of organic clicks as AI clicks.

Why does my page appear in AI Mode and not in an AI Overview?+

Google says the two may use different models and techniques, so the responses and links they show will vary. An AI Overview also appears only when Google's systems judge it adds to classic Search.

How do I keep my content out of AI Overviews?+

Google names nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex as the controls for limiting what Search shows from a page. They also limit ordinary results. Google-Extended does not do this: Google says it does not affect inclusion in Search.
