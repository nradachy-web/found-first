How broadly can GEO content describe a reader survey?

If your publication polled its readers, commission GEO copy that names the people who answered. A trade publication can have a national readership and still have little basis for saying what businesses nationwide prefer.

Read the invitation before approving the headline

Ask to see the invitation, distribution list description and eligibility question beside the proposed finding. Each answers a different question. A mailing list tells you who could receive the link. A screener tells you which replies qualified. Neither tells you that people who skipped the poll share the respondents' views.

Give an editor a sentence worth quoting

Keep the subject and the finding together in the sentence you want a trade editor or a generative answer to use. A methods appendix can explain recruitment in depth. It does little for a headline that has already turned a reader preference into an industry buying trend.

A fleet newsletter asks about vehicle replacement

Suppose a publisher sends a replacement-planning poll to newsletter subscribers. The invitation welcomes drivers, dealers and fleet managers. The draft says fleet managers favor extending leases. If a role question identifies managers who answered the lease question, the editor can report that preference among those responding managers, after checking their replies. If the survey collected no roles, the editor should describe the respondents as readers and drop the claim about managers. The newsletter's editorial focus cannot supply the missing job titles.
