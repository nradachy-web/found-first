AEO for dealerships that sell vehicles another dealer repairs

Build your dealership’s AEO answers for warranty work around what the service desk can accept. A manufacturer may authorize your store to sell a vehicle line and assign certain repairs to a different workshop. Tell owners which service desk can book the work their vehicle needs.

Check the workshop listing at model level

Compare the manufacturer’s service directory with the models and job types in the store’s booking menu. A listing for routine service is weak evidence for a claim about specialist repairs. Where the records differ, have the service manager confirm what the store will take before adding that job to the dealership page.

Separate the booking decision from the coverage decision

Name the workshop that can inspect the issue and the details it asks the owner to bring. If coverage is decided after inspection, say so where the reader chooses an appointment. Staff can then book the inspection while leaving the question of who pays for the repair open.

An electric van sold at a mixed-fleet dealership

Imagine a store whose sales roster includes an electric van. Its workshop schedule accepts tire and brake work, while the manufacturer’s repair directory identifies another dealer for traction battery diagnosis. For a battery warning, the store page gives that dealer’s service contact and asks the owner to have the vehicle identification number ready. If the same van needs a brake appointment, the page offers the selling store’s scheduler and lets the owner choose a time.
