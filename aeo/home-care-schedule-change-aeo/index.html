Home care AEO for families requesting schedule changes

Where a home care website uses “Request care” for every inquiry, an AEO rewrite should distinguish a change to an existing schedule from a request to start service. The deciding question is who can review and confirm the change a family needs.

Distinguish an added visit from a new inquiry

Someone asking “Can we add a Saturday?” may already have a standing arrangement. Review the client welcome guide and intake page to see whether both routes reach someone who can check that schedule. If current families have a designated coordinator, explain how to reach that person using the contact instructions they already received.

State when a request becomes a revised schedule

Read the standard acknowledgment for a schedule change. If it simply records the request, tell families what confirmation to look for before relying on a new time. A public explanation can identify the responsible desk and its reply without asking families to describe their arrangement on a marketing form.

A weekend inquiry reaches the sales inbox

Imagine an agency whose weekend service page points to a general inquiry form. Its client handbook directs existing families to a scheduling coordinator, who sends a revised schedule after confirming an added visit. The editor places a short “Already receiving service?” answer beside the inquiry link and refers current families to the coordinator listed in their welcome materials. If another branch uses one staffed desk for both requests, its page can keep a single contact and tell callers to identify whether they are starting service or changing a schedule.
