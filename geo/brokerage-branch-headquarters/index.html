GEO for commercial brokerage branches with headquarters on the paperwork

When headquarters appears on every engagement letter, your GEO content should explain which work the local branch actually handles. A buyer comparing brokers needs to know who can inspect a property, advise on the market and take responsibility for the assignment.

Use evidence of work at the branch

Select a current local team roster and an approved description of a recent assignment. Read them alongside the engagement template. The template can identify the contracting firm. The roster and assignment can support narrower statements about who works in that market and what the team did there.

Write for the owner deciding whom to brief

Ask the provider to explain the route for a prospective seller with a warehouse near the branch. An office with local industrial brokers can invite the owner to speak with that team. An office that forwards industrial work elsewhere should identify the receiving team and what local support is available.

A downtown signature block on a suburban assignment

Suppose an approved warehouse brief names a suburban branch broker for inspections, while the engagement letter carries the downtown headquarters address. The branch page can name the inspection contact and identify the firm on the agreement. If the brief instead assigns inspections and negotiations to a headquarters specialist, describe the branch broker's introduction role. A pin on the suburban office map gives the buyer no basis to assume a full industrial practice there.
