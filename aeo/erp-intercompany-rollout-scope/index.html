AEO for ERP vendors scoping an intercompany rollout

When a prospect asks whether a new subsidiary fits its ERP plan, your AEO page should answer the transaction the finance team needs to run. Adding another ledger and processing a sale between two group companies are distinct buying decisions.

Follow the transaction into both sets of books

A finance director asking about intercompany trading may expect the selling entity's invoice to create a corresponding payable in the buyer's ledger. Use the product coverage table to say which documents the software creates, which entries require staff action and whether group reporting reads those entries. Identify any module entitlement that changes the answer.

Define what the rollout quote includes

The product may support a workflow that the implementation agreement leaves for the customer to configure. Look for responsibility for entity setup, trading-partner mappings and paired transaction testing. A buyer with a launch deadline needs to know who will run the first test with both finance teams.

A second ledger opens before the first shipment

Suppose a vendor's plan summary allows another entity, but the quoted rollout workbook contains only ledger setup and user access. A sample intercompany invoice requires a trading-partner mapping that nobody has agreed to configure. For a subsidiary trading with its parent on launch day, the AEO page should identify that extra implementation work. If the subsidiary initially sells only to outside customers, the buyer can evaluate ledger setup now and scope intercompany testing before internal trading begins.
