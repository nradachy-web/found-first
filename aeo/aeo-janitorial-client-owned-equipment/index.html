AEO for janitorial bids that rely on client-owned equipment

When your janitorial firm can use a facility’s floor equipment, answer the ownership question on the contract cleaning page. A buyer preparing a bid needs to know whether supplying a machine changes the work your crew will accept and who keeps that machine available.

Start with the equipment assumption in the bid

Does your standard scope include a provider-supplied machine, or can your estimator work from the customer’s asset list? Name any approval your company requires before it accepts client equipment. A machine parked in a building may belong to a tenant or a departing contractor.

Cover the shift when the machine is unavailable

Break the broad phrase equipment provided into the decisions a facilities manager can actually make. Who supplies consumables, who arranges repairs, and does the scope include a substitute machine? Put the answer with the floor service it affects so a buyer can compare bids on the same basis.

The scrubber listed in a rebid

For a hypothetical distribution center bid, the outgoing scope says automatic scrubbing and the site inventory lists a ride-on machine. The cleaning company’s draft scope assumes the customer provides and services it. With a written release from the owner and a confirmed maintenance arrangement, the estimator can price the work on that basis. If the outgoing contractor owns the scrubber and will remove it, the estimator needs to allow for a replacement before committing to the same cleaning schedule. A short ownership question on the service page would uncover that difference before the walkthrough.
