Which placements support a recruiter’s executive search GEO page?

Select placements for your executive search GEO page by the responsibility the employer hired your team to fill. Titles vary across employers, and an impressive title can obscure the actual scope of both the position and the search.

Read the mandate from the employer's seat

Look for the reporting line, the decisions assigned to the role and the functions the hire would oversee. A plant leadership search might hinge on one site's production and workforce. A group operations appointment might include choosing site leaders and allocating resources across facilities. Those differences help an employer decide whether to discuss its own vacancy with you.

Separate the appointment from a later promotion

Check the original search brief and the accepted role against the candidate's current biography. A promotion can be welcome news, yet it cannot establish that the employer engaged your firm to recruit for the more senior job. Anonymous summaries can preserve the original remit where publishing the candidate's identity would be inappropriate.

A vice president in the placement archive

Take a placement entry labeled VP of Engineering. The candidate now holds that title, but the original brief and offer describe a team lead reporting to the engineering director. Feature the search as a technical leadership placement. If the archive instead contains a mandate from the chief executive to appoint the head of engineering with responsibility for department staffing and strategy, use those duties in the executive search case. The employer comparing firms can see the level at which your recruiters worked.
