AEO for car washes answering roof rack questions

If drivers call your wash asking whether their rack can go through, make equipment compatibility the subject of the AEO work. Publish the entry rules for the actual wash lane and distinguish bare roof rails from the carrier a driver attaches to them.

Start with what the driver can identify

Use labeled pictures of factory rails, crossbars, roof boxes and mounted bike trays where those distinctions change the operator's answer. A driver may call all of them a roof rack. A clearance sign answers a height question but tells the driver little about a projecting attachment that the operator excludes even on a low vehicle.

Put the decision before payment

Someone buying a wash online should be able to check the entry policy before selecting a package. Give an unfamiliar setup a practical next step, such as showing a photo to an attendant before joining the queue. Mention an alternative bay only if it is available and the operator has confirmed that the setup fits there.

The same wagon arrives for two visits

Take a hypothetical tunnel whose posted rules allow the wagon's factory rails but exclude rooftop bike trays. The operator gives the writer the current entry sign and photographs of each setup. The page lets a driver with bare rails continue to the remaining entry checks. The same driver returning with an empty bike tray gets the removal instruction before buying a wash. Taking the bike off still leaves the excluded tray attached.
