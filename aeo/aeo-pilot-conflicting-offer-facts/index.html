How to score an AEO pilot when your offer pages disagree

Set the accuracy judgment aside until someone who owns the offer can establish which terms applied. When two public pages disagree, an answer can faithfully repeat one page and still give the buyer bad instructions.

Find the terms for this purchase

Identify the offer, customer type and effective period in the buyer's question. Bring the relevant public passages to the person who approves that offer. A recently revised overview might simplify an exception that still applies under the order terms. The latest edit date alone cannot settle the dispute.

Separate a publishing fix from an accuracy finding

If the offer owner confirms an error on a public page, agree who will correct it and retain both earlier passages with the pilot record. If the pages describe different agreements, revise the test question to specify the agreement or accept an answer that explains the distinction. Either choice gives you a defensible target for the next review.

Two minimum terms for the same rental

Suppose a projection equipment supplier's event page offers single-day hire while its order form states a weekend minimum. Sales confirms that the minimum applies only when a technician attends. For a question about equipment-only hire, accept a qualified single-day answer and ask for that distinction on the order form. If operations instead confirms that every weekend order now carries the minimum, the event page is stale. Correct it before using weekend answers as evidence for extending the AEO work.
