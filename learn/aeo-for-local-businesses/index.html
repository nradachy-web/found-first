AEO for local businesses

For a local business, answer engine optimization means making sure AI systems can find and correctly state what you do, where you do it and when you are available. That rests on a consistent business profile, a plain page for each job you want and real reviews. In our published study of one plumbing business, how often it was named changed sharply with the problem described.

What changes when the question includes a place

  • One question carries a job, a place and a deadline

    A local customer asks about a specific problem, in a specific town, often tonight. A page that names the service but not the area, or the area but not the hours, answers a third of the question.

  • Your facts are published in places you rarely look

    Profiles, directories and old listings all state your name, phone number and hours. Our first study turned up variants of one business's published details, and confirming them became the first task.

  • Being available is part of being named

    An answer can mention a business and add a caution about whether it can come. If your hours, dispatch area or after-hours line are unclear, the caution is what the customer reads.

  • Similar names get mixed up

    In our study some answers confused the business with a similarly named company. Google asks that a profile show the name on your signage and stationery. Using that one name everywhere gives every system the same thing to match.

What our plumbing study found

Found First's first published study is of a local business: five homeowner plumbing scenarios, each asked three times across a panel of AI models. Inside that one business, the count of answers that mentioned it moved with the problem described.

What that finding shows, and what it does not

It shows that one check of a business name is not a measurement. The same company was mentioned often for one problem and almost never for another, so a local business has to test each job it wants to win as its own question. It does not show why, it says nothing about another trade or another city, and it is a baseline: no improvement from Found First's work is claimed.

What it gives a local owner is a method. List the jobs you want, write the question a customer would ask for each, collect the answers more than once, and read them before you count them. An AI visibility audit is that method applied to your business.

The scenarios in our plumbing study were of this kind: urgent plumbing, recurring drain problems, water heaters, hidden leaks and a second opinion on sewer work.

Local facts to confirm before you write anything new

Most of this is a morning with your own listings. Where Google has a written rule, we cite it.

  • The name matches the signGoogle asks that a profile show the business as it appears on signage, stationery and other branding. Use that exact name on the site and every listing.
  • There is one profile per businessGoogle's guidelines say there should be only one, because duplicates cause display problems on Maps and Search. Find and remove old ones.
  • Address and service area are set the right wayIf customers never come to your address, Google says to remove it from the profile and list the areas you serve instead.
  • The service area is honestGoogle asks for a specific, accurate area that reaches no more than about two hours' drive from your base. List the same towns in text on your site.
  • The phone number reaches youGoogle asks for a number under the business's direct control that connects to the location, and a local number over a call center line where possible.
  • Hours are true this weekInclude holiday and after-hours arrangements. If emergency calls go to voicemail, do not call it 24-hour service.
  • Each job you want has its own pageScope, price drivers, what to expect and how to book, in text. Google asks that important content be available in textual form.
  • City pages say something only true of that cityPublish a town page only where you work there and have something local to say. Otherwise it is the doorway page Google's spam policies describe.

What a local business can measure

A local sale often starts with a phone call, and a call does not say which answer the customer read first. So measure in layers and keep them apart: a rise in one does not prove a rise in the next.

How to measure AI marketing covers the reporting in full, and the AEO service page explains how the work runs and what it costs. For a local business the layers are these:

  • Answers: for each job and town you test, how often the business is named, how it is described and whether the details are right, with the date and the sample.
  • Google: visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted in Search Console inside overall Web search traffic. Google does not report them separately.
  • Inquiries: calls, quote requests and bookings, counted by your own phone and booking systems. Ask new customers how they found you and write down what they say.
  • Jobs: booked and completed work, which only you can confirm.

Questions local owners ask

Should we create a page for every nearby city?+

Only where you actually work there and the page says something specific to that place, such as the jobs you do there or local conditions that affect the work. Google's spam policies describe pages aimed at specific cities that funnel visitors to one page as doorway abuse.

Do reviews change what AI says about a local business?+

We cannot tell you how any model weighs reviews, and we do not claim to know. Reviews are public text about your business that customers and AI systems can both read, so ask every customer for an honest one and reply to the critical ones. Google prohibits offering discounts or free services in exchange for reviews.

We work from a truck, not a storefront. Does that hurt us?+

It changes what you publish, not whether you can be found. Google has a service-area business remove its address from the profile and list the cities or postal codes it serves, and your site should name the same areas in plain text.

Is this different from local SEO?+

The foundations are the same: accurate listings, useful service pages and real reviews. What AEO adds is testing how AI systems answer your customers' questions and checking that what they say about you is correct.

Can ChatGPT ads be limited to our service area?+

OpenAI says campaigns that do not use a product feed can target by state or region, city, market and postal code where those options are available, in addition to country. The ad is a paid placement below the answer and does not change what the answer says.

Can you guarantee we appear in local AI answers?+

No. We improve the accuracy and availability of your information and report what we observe. Whether a business is named is each platform's decision and can change with the wording of the question.
