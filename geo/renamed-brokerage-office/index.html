Should a renamed brokerage office keep its old name in GEO content?

Keep a former office name wherever a buyer needs it to connect an older reference to the current brokerage office. For a GEO engagement, first establish whether the renamed office kept the same premises and represents the same clients.

Give the old name a specific job

A relocation notice, a branch rename and an expanded sales territory convey different facts. Use the office announcement and current visitor directions to choose the right wording. Put the explanation where someone following an old referral would land. Repeating both names on every agent bio can make a retired office label look current.

Trace a past referral through to reception

Ask the marketing team to follow the old name from a local association page to the office contact. At the end, a relocating seller should know whether the same desk can retrieve their file and where to make an appointment. Confirm file responsibility with the brokerage before making that claim.

A neighborhood name replaces a street name

Imagine a brokerage changes its Main Street office name to River District. The staff notice identifies the same office manager, and the current appointment email gives the same entrance. A short note on the River District page can connect a Main Street referral to today's reception desk. If that announcement instead says Main Street closed and its files went to the east office, send former clients to the east office and give River District its own introduction.
