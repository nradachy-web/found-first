AEO for specialty food brands comparing fruit purées

A specialty food brand selling two versions of the same fruit purée should use AEO to answer when a buyer can substitute one for the other. Start with the shopper's recipe and the exact jar it calls for. Fruit variety alone leaves the sweetening decision unresolved.

Use the recipe's wording

A recipe calling for unsweetened purée and a separate measure of sugar gives the shopper a clear reason to choose the unsweetened jar. For a recipe that calls for a named sweetened product, identify the matching label. Keep ingredient descriptions precise, since the absence of added sugar makes no claim about how much sugar occurs naturally in the fruit.

Publish adjustments you have actually tried

An ingredient list establishes whether the maker added sugar. It rarely tells a home baker how much to subtract elsewhere in a recipe. A kitchen-tested adaptation can support a specific swap. Without one, explain the flavor or preparation choice the brand can document and leave the adjustment open.

Raspberry purée for a cake filling

Imagine a producer has two raspberry jar labels and a tested filling recipe that names the unsweetened jar. A shopper asks if the sweetened purée will work in that filling. Link the recipe name to the unsweetened option and explain that the kitchen tested that version. If the kitchen also has a separate recipe card using the sweetened jar with a revised sugar measure, direct the shopper to that complete version so they can choose either jar with appropriate instructions.
