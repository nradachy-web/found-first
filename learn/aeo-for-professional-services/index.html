A specialty is valuable only when a prospect understands it

People who already work with your firm may know exactly why they chose it. A new prospective client has to form that view from public information. If the website reduces specific expertise to broad professional language, the distinction that matters in a referral can disappear during independent research.

Found First helps translate the firm's approved service scope into a useful buying explanation. The goal is not to make the firm sound like every other provider in its category. It is to make the relevant specialty, client context and working relationship clear enough that an unfamiliar buyer can see a reason to begin a conversation.

Explain the engagement without turning content into advice

A prospective client needs to know what kind of help the firm provides and which questions belong in an initial discussion. That can be explained without delivering individualized professional advice through a general page. A clear service description respects the difference between evaluating a provider and receiving a recommendation about a particular matter.

The same precision applies to qualifications and experience. A credential should be attributed to the person or entity that holds it, and a narrow specialty should not become a universal claim. Our industry pages explore the buying contexts in more detail; the shared principle is an accurate account of the firm and the client relationship it actually offers.

A mortgage broker can explain the difference between line of credit and lien position without quoting a borrower a rate. In Michigan, 12,539 qualifying home equity lines held first lien position in 2025. Read the HELOC study and its inclusion criteria.

Start with the client relationship you want to develop

A professional firm may prefer a narrower set of better-qualified inquiries over a larger volume of general interest. The relevant priority could be an overlooked specialty, a changed service offering or fewer mismatched requests. That commercial choice should shape the work before broad visibility becomes the objective.

Questions before you choose a partner

Can this support a highly specialized firm?+

Yes. A defined specialty can provide a strong reason for the right client to consider the firm. The service should make that reason understandable using approved facts, rather than broaden the offering until it loses the distinction that makes the business valuable.

Who approves professional claims?+

The firm remains responsible for its claims and the requirements relevant to its profession. Found First helps improve clarity and presentation using approved information. The marketing relationship does not turn the agency into a source of legal, medical, financial or other individualized professional advice.

Will you guarantee new clients or professional outcomes?+

No. Visibility, inquiries and completed engagements are different things, and professional outcomes are outside a marketing promise. The business case should focus on accurate representation and relevant client consideration, with expectations tied to the work being proposed rather than control over an AI answer.
