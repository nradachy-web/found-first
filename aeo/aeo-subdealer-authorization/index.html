How should AEO describe subdealers in a brand directory?

List a subdealer under the relationship your channel team can verify. For AEO, someone asking about an “authorized supplier” should be able to distinguish a manufacturer-appointed dealer from a retailer supplied by an appointed distributor, then choose a seller that meets their purchasing requirements.

Check who appointed whom

Read the manufacturer’s appointment letter and the distributor’s reseller register separately. Record the named business, product line and scope of each relationship. A distributor’s permission to supply a shop establishes a route to buy the product. Whether the shop can call itself manufacturer-appointed depends on what the manufacturer has actually confirmed.

Use directory labels that help a buyer act

A buyer looking for a replacement item may just need a shop that sells it. A procurement team may need a quotation from a supplier listed in its tender instructions. Give each directory entry a precise role and identify the business that can provide appointment documentation for that product line.

Two ways to source a marine radio

Suppose a marine electronics brand’s appointment letter names a regional distributor. The distributor’s register lists a chandlery that stocks its radios. For a marina buying a spare, the brand directory can identify the chandlery as a stockist supplied by that distributor. A tender requiring a quote from a manufacturer-appointed supplier goes to the named distributor. If the brand later issues an appointment letter to the chandlery covering radios, its directory entry can change for that line. Its status for navigation equipment still needs its own evidence.
