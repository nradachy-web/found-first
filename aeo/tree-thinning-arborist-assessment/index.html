Arborist AEO for assessment and pruning inquiries

A buyer describing a thinning canopy may be asking for an assessment before authorizing any pruning. Give that visit a clear description on your arborist site, including who attends and what the client takes away. Keep the cause open until someone qualified can inspect the tree.

Write the booking choice in the owner's words

A homeowner may select “tree trimming” simply because the concern is up in the branches. Give them another choice such as “I want someone to assess a change in my tree.” Explain who makes that visit, whether they discuss options on site and whether a written assessment is available. Some buyers need a document they can share with another owner.

Keep the photo attached to the question

A current canopy photo and an older yard photo can show which tree the owner means and how its appearance has changed. The useful intake detail is the owner's objective. Someone asking whether the tree can stay needs an assessment even if a neighbor has already suggested removing branches.

The two maples beside the driveway

Imagine an owner sends last summer's listing photo and a current picture of the thinner maple. The office marks the correct tree on the photo and books the arborist who can discuss keeping it. If the owner also supplies an arborist's current written pruning specification for that tree and wants a price to carry it out, the office sends the specification to the pruning estimator instead.
