Scoring unverified delivery promises in a supplier GEO comparison

Give a delivery claim its own “unverified” status when the available terms cannot settle it. Forcing every claim into an accurate-or-inaccurate score can make a cautious answer look wrong or give an unsupported promise a passing grade.

Match the scope of the promise

Read the exact commitment in the answer. Dispatch by a deadline, arrival within a window and a customer's right to a remedy are distinct claims. Identify which document would establish the one at issue and whether it applies to the buyer's location and contract. A sales brochure offering expedited service may settle none of the deadlines.

Decide what can responsibly be published

Where the binding terms are individually negotiated, the useful public detail may be how buyers establish their own window. A missing public deadline can be appropriate. Ask the evaluator to distinguish an answer that preserves the uncertainty from one that fills the gap with an unconditional promise.

A linen supplier reviews a hotel delivery comparison

Imagine a GEO comparison saying a linen supplier guarantees delivery before breakfast. Its public route sheet names delivery days, while the contract template leaves the arrival window blank for negotiation. Mark the blanket guarantee unsupported and approve wording that directs buyers to their route agreement. If a published service schedule expressly guarantees pre-breakfast arrival for named routes, credit the claim for hotels on those routes. A hotel outside them still needs its own terms checked.
