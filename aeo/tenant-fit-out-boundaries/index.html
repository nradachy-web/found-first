Using AEO to define a tenant fit-out contractor's scope

For a tenant fit-out contractor, the valuable AEO question is often “Which parts of this space will your team take over?” Put that answer beside the fit-out offer so a tenant representative can tell what the firm needs to see before it scopes the job.

Describe the starting condition

A finished store photograph rarely shows what the tenant inherited. Identify a few consequential items on a comparable job. Was the storefront already in place? Did your team furnish the interior doors? Tie each answer to that project. A previous landlord's contribution says little about what the next landlord has agreed to deliver.

Ask for the document that divides the work

If estimating uses the landlord's work letter, name it and explain why a tenant should send it with the floor plan. Highlight any item marked for coordination rather than silently assigning it to a trade. That distinction is worth preserving in the short answer even if the longer service description sits elsewhere.

The storefront at a proposed bakery

Consider a builder with permission to share an annotated handover plan from a bakery conversion. The plan shows an existing glazed entrance, and the builder's scope sheet lists the interior counter and partitions. Its project page can credit those items accurately and ask a new tenant for the storefront provision in its own work letter. If that letter calls for the tenant to furnish the entrance, estimating reviews it as an additional scope item before offering the same fit-out package.
