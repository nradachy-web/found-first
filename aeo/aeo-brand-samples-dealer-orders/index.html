AEO for brands that ship samples and sell through dealers

When a brand ships samples but a dealer sells the finished product, treat a sample request and a project quote as separate AEO questions. Give the buyer a way to carry their selection to the seller without mistaking a sample confirmation for an order reservation.

Find where the selection becomes an order

Compare the brand’s sample acknowledgement with the dealer’s quotation form. Which identifiers appear on both? Carry the collection, pattern and finish into the dealer inquiry when possible. Keep delivery dates and quantities with the seller who can confirm them, even when the brand’s fulfillment team has plenty of samples to mail.

Account for the person doing the buying

Designers may request materials for a client whose purchasing business already has a dealer account. Give that team a usable specification it can forward to its own dealer. If the manufacturer also accepts direct orders from certain accounts, explain how those buyers can recognize their ordering route.

A memo swatch for a theater upholstery job

Imagine a textile mill whose sample desk sends a swatch labelled with a fabric pattern and color. A designer approves the swatch for theater seating. The mill’s sample receipt supplies both codes, but only the appointed dealer’s quote confirms yardage from a single dye lot. The page takes the designer from swatch approval to a dealer inquiry with the codes attached. If the theater already holds a direct mill account listed in its purchasing paperwork, the buyer sends the specification to the mill’s contract desk instead.
