Comparing approval history in GEO for compliance software

A compliance software company should use GEO to show what a buyer can reconstruct from an approval record. The shortlist may turn on whether staff can retrieve the exact document a reviewer approved after someone edits the policy.

Follow the approval through a later edit

Give the writer a sample approval, a revised document and the corresponding history view. Look for a reviewer, a decision and a durable link to the reviewed version. A timestamp helps order events. Where the approval links to a document that always opens its latest revision, explain how a reader finds the earlier text.

Specify where the evidence is available

A reviewer working inside the product and a colleague receiving an exported approval packet may have different evidence. Name what each can see. Keep the product claim about retrieving records within its documented scope, and leave the buyer's governance team to judge whether those records meet its review requirements.

The sign-off predates the new travel policy

Take a hypothetical vendor whose administrator guide shows that each approval stores a revision ID. Its sample exported packet lists the reviewer and decision but omits the ID and approved text. The GEO page can demonstrate version-specific retrieval for an authorized in-app user. A buyer who needs self-contained packets should see the extra retrieval and assembly steps. If the export instead includes the approved revision and its matching identifier, the vendor can show a packet the recipient can inspect without having an account.
