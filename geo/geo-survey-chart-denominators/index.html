Labeling survey chart denominators for GEO

Require your GEO chart captions to say who or what each percentage counts. Put the base beside the finding so a buyer reading one chart can reconstruct the comparison without hunting through the report.

Follow the route through the survey

Check the questionnaire routing against the table used to draw the chart. The full sample, the people shown a question and the people who supplied a usable answer can be different groups. Use the base the calculation actually used. Include the count when you have it, along with any weighting that changes how the percentage was calculated.

Check what a bar represents

On a multiple-choice chart, a bar may show the share of respondents selecting an option or the share of all selections it received. Those measures answer different questions. If respondents could select several reasons, say so before anyone interprets the bars as slices of a single whole.

Why warehouse managers hired temporary staff

In an illustrative survey, 75 managers participate and 30 say they hired temporary staff. All 30 answer a follow-up, and 18 select seasonal demand among the reasons. An unweighted caption can say that 18 of the 30 managers who hired temps selected seasonal demand, or 60%. If only 24 answered the follow-up and the table excludes blanks, those same 18 represent 75% of follow-up respondents. Calling either figure a share of all warehouse managers would change the meaning.
