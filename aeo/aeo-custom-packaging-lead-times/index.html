Explaining custom packaging lead times with AEO

For a packaging supplier, a useful AEO answer about lead time names the event that starts the clock and the milestone at the other end. A brand planning a launch needs to know whether your published estimate gets cartons to the carrier or to its filling line.

Use the milestones in the order acknowledgment

Compare the website's delivery language with the acknowledgment your customer actually receives. If a production estimate runs from signed proof to dispatch, use those words. Explain where transit enters the discussion and when a buyer can get confirmation for a particular destination.

Work backward from what the buyer needs

An in-hand date and a proof approval deadline answer different questions. Give the request form a field for the required arrival date and ask whether the artwork is ready for review. Those details let sales discuss the remaining decisions before it offers a schedule.

The cartons have to reach a contract packer

Suppose a beverage brand submits its launch calendar and a carton proof with the ingredient panel still marked for correction. The supplier's acknowledgment starts production timing at signed artwork approval. Marketing's example can show why sales first asks when the corrected proof will be signed. If the brand supplies the signed proof with the inquiry, sales can move on to checking the production slot and transit to the packer's receiving address.
