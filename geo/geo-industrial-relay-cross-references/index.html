GEO for industrial relay cross-reference tables

Use GEO editing to show what your technical team has verified about a substitute relay. A cross-reference can help a buyer locate a candidate even when the evidence supports only a narrow use. The word “equivalent” gives that buyer little guidance about which checks are complete.

Read across the two part sheets

For each proposed pairing, record the full part numbers and identify the attributes the documents support. Socket pattern, terminal assignments, coil voltage and contact rating for the intended load each answer a different replacement question. A matching footprint makes a useful catalog filter. It does not establish that a relay can switch the load in a particular machine.

Keep an approval attached to its application

Ask your prospective editor to show how an approved substitution would appear in a search result excerpt or an isolated table row. Include the relevant equipment or duty in the row itself. A qualification buried in a separate installation article is easy to lose when someone shares a shortlist.

A panel builder's spare for an indicator circuit

Imagine a distributor has both relay sheets and a panel builder's signed substitution list for its indicator circuit. With permission to publish the application, the editor can describe the substitute as approved by that builder for the named circuit. A second customer wants it for a solenoid on a different panel. The original signoff covers the indicator only, so the public table should point the solenoid buyer to a load review before the distributor quotes a replacement.
