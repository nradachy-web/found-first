A migration changes more than the design

A website can look similar after a move while its addresses, content relationships or access behavior change. Those changes matter to customers following older links and to the public references that point toward your business. A migration therefore deserves a different review from a visual refresh.

For a company consolidating product sites under one brand, the issue is not simply where the new homepage lives. Buyers still need to find the product they were researching and understand its relationship to the company. Found First helps keep those commercial requirements visible while the technical team handles the move.

Preserve the information that made the destination useful

A migration can accidentally remove context that buyers relied on: service limitations, product comparisons or explanations of which team handles an inquiry. Moving the address does not preserve that value if the content disappears. The new destination should continue to answer the relevant question.

Found First's website and content work can help identify the business meaning that needs to survive. For a product being renamed, the site may need to explain the relationship between the old and current names. For a service being retired, the destination should communicate the change accurately instead of pretending the old offer remains available.

Treat external references as a separate observation

Google's site-move guidance addresses the handling of URL changes and explains that processing a move takes time. An AI answer citing an old address is another piece of evidence to inspect. A technically completed migration does not prove every external system has adopted the new destination.

We keep those boundaries explicit. Found First can help assess the public customer experience and the information available after the move. We cannot guarantee that a particular assistant will preserve or replace a citation on schedule. The business should know what has been changed, what remains reachable and which external representations have actually been observed.

Coordinate the move with the commercial change

Sometimes a migration accompanies a merger, a new market or a different product offer. Those changes need clear business decisions before the website can represent them accurately. A technical team should not have to infer whether an old service still exists from a new navigation label.

A founder consultation can clarify the reason for the move, the important customer paths and the facts that must remain understandable. Found First can discuss supporting website work and organic visibility evaluation alongside your existing engineering responsibilities. The aim is a coherent public transition that helps buyers find the right current information, with risks described honestly rather than hidden behind a promise of seamless visibility.

Before you decide.

Can you guarantee we keep our AI citations after moving?+

No. A citation is controlled by the external experience that produces it. Found First can help preserve useful content and assess the customer destination, but a future citation must be observed separately from the completed website move.

Is a platform change always a URL migration?+

Not necessarily. A platform can change while public addresses remain stable, or a redesign can change URLs without changing the platform. The actual scope should be established before deciding which discovery and customer-path risks need attention.

Should we combine a migration with a complete content rewrite?+

That depends on the business reason and the content's value. Changing everything at once can make it harder to distinguish what was preserved or removed. Found First can help decide which explanations need continuity and which genuinely need revision.
