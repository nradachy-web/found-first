Reconciling merchant dimensions in furniture catalog GEO

When merchants publish different dimensions for the same furniture item, ask your GEO provider to identify the object and state each source measured. The figures may all be accurate for different purposes, such as ordering freight, placing the assembled piece or opening a drawer.

Restore the missing measurement labels

Find the drawing for the exact variant, then map each figure to width, height or depth and record any projection such as handles. Read the carton diagram separately. Three values copied without axis labels can change order between merchants, and rotating a flat-packed carton tells a buyer little about the finished footprint.

Choose the dimension that answers the question

A comparison for narrow hallways needs the assembled piece's projection from the wall. A delivery guide may need packaged dimensions and information about the route into the building. Ask the editor to keep both sets of figures where they help and use the label a buyer needs for the specific decision.

The console depth includes the knobs

Imagine a furniture brand finds that a reseller's console depth exceeds the cabinet depth on its assembly drawing. The drawing gives one side view for the bare cabinet and another with projecting knobs. The reseller's number agrees with the latter. Publish the overall depth for hallway fit and retain the cabinet-only figure with its label. If the reseller's value instead matches the carton sticker, move it to the delivery details. Until an overall assembled depth is documented, leave the console out of a tight-clearance comparison.
