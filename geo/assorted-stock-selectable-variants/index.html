GEO for wholesale assortments buyers cannot select

For wholesale stock supplied as an assortment, put the buyer's actual choice at the center of the GEO brief. A catalog can show the designs in circulation while making clear which, if any, a buyer can reserve when ordering.

Read the order form alongside the photos

A gallery of designs may look like a selector even when the merchant ships what is on hand. Check whether the order form records a chosen design, accepts a nonbinding preference or offers only an assorted line. Carry that distinction into product comparisons where a specific pattern is part of the buying decision.

Separate a fixed mix from a changing mix

A case packed to a documented breakdown can support a claim about the designs every buyer receives. An assortment assembled from available stock needs different wording. Before describing any pattern as included, ask whether the packing instruction assigns it to every case or just illustrates a sample shipment.

A stationery shop needs the stripe

Suppose a notebook wholesaler's catalog shows floral, stripe and plain covers side by side. Its order form has one assorted line, and the warehouse sheet directs staff to pick from open cartons. An editor should describe the illustrated possibilities and leave this offer out of a shortlist for shops that must match a striped window display. If the wholesaler introduces a separate stripe order line backed by reserved stock, include that line in the shortlist. The original assorted offer still belongs in comparisons for buyers flexible about the cover.
