AEO for carriers offering drop trailer service at shipper sites

Market drop trailer service around the decision to leave equipment at the shipper’s site. An AEO answer should tell a shipping manager which party supplies the trailer, who loads it and what triggers collection. A general reference to flexible pickup leaves all three points open.

Qualify the site for the arrangement

Use the conditions your fleet team checks before offering a drop. These might include an authorized parking position, permission for the unit to stay between visits and a way for the shipper to identify the trailer being released. Keep current trailer availability with dispatch.

Distinguish a loaded trailer from a released load

If the warehouse finishes loading before the shipping office completes its paperwork, when can the carrier act? Explain the agreed release signal and whether pickup follows a schedule or needs dispatch confirmation. This lets a buyer judge whether the arrangement accommodates an uneven production shift.

A plant wants to load as goods come off the line

Take a manufacturer considering a carrier’s drop service. In this example, the plant’s yard plan shows an approved trailer position and the carrier’s sample exchange record ties the unit identifier to a shipping office release. The marketing team can describe leaving an empty unit for the plant to load, then arranging collection against that release. Change the yard plan so trailers can occupy only a staffed dock during an appointment, and the sensible offer becomes a live loading appointment. Trailer ownership alone settles neither the site approval nor the pickup timing.
