How AEO can distinguish a consulting discovery call from a diagnostic

Use the discovery call page to answer “What will we leave the meeting with?” For consulting partners buying AEO work, the answer should describe the decision a sponsor can make after the call and identify any analysis that requires its own engagement.

Trace the promise in the calendar invitation

Start with the invitation a sponsor receives. If it asks for a description of the problem and the name of its owner, explain how those details help both sides decide whether there is a project to scope. Phrases such as “discover the root cause” create a very different expectation about what the partner will deliver during the conversation.

Name the point where analysis starts

Have the writer compare the call agenda with a diagnostic proposal. Find the first task that requires access to records, interviews or time to interpret evidence. Describe that task where the diagnostic is offered so a sponsor knows when to involve the people who own those records.

A sales director wants an explanation for stalled renewals

Imagine the calendar invitation promises a fit discussion and the diagnostic proposal lists interviews and a written findings memo. The page could say the call will establish which part of the renewal process needs investigation and whether to scope a review. Ask the director for a brief description at booking. If the booked offer actually includes an analyst's advance review of a renewal report, give that appointment its own preparation instructions and say whether the analyst will provide written findings.
