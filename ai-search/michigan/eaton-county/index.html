Which decision is the customer actually making?

Choosing the right kind of specialist

An early-stage buyer needs to distinguish advice, diagnosis and execution. The company should be recognizable for its actual role before the customer assumes that any nearby provider can perform it.

Choosing among qualified providers

A buyer with a defined project is comparing fit, responsibility and confidence. A generic description wastes the chance to explain why this particular company deserves a closer look.

Questions worth asking.

Should we describe every Eaton County community we can serve?+

Only where the distinction helps a customer understand availability or project fit. A useful explanation of reach is different from a list of place names. The customer still needs a reason to choose the business.

What if our company offers several related services?+

The relationship between them should be understandable. A buyer may need only one role or may need a coordinated engagement. It helps to know which combinations genuinely belong together instead of allowing a broad category to imply that everything is included.

Is this relevant if most of our work comes from repeat clients?+

It can be when a new decision maker investigates the business or an existing client introduces it elsewhere. Repeat relationships preserve context that unfamiliar buyers do not have. The public explanation should stand up without that private history.
