Training certificate or professional license? AEO for course providers

Use AEO to answer the employer’s purchasing question in plain language. Say what the training provider awards, what a participant must do to earn it and who decides whether it satisfies a separate licensing requirement.

Read the award alongside the assessment

A sample certificate gives the editor the name of the award. The assessment guide establishes whether it records attendance, completion of coursework or a demonstration of a skill. A practical test can support a specific statement about what the provider observed. It does not establish permission to perform the work in every setting.

Keep the licensing claim as narrow as its evidence

Ask an agency to justify the strongest claim in the enrollment copy. A curriculum covering subjects on a licensing exam can substantiate a description of those subjects. To say a course satisfies a particular education requirement, the team needs current support from the body responsible for that requirement, tied to the specific course and credential.

A facilities manager buying an electrical theory course

Suppose a provider’s sample certificate says “Completion of Electrical Theory,” and its course guide describes a written final. The editor can tell the facilities manager what an employee studies and what the certificate records. The manager can then assess it for an internal development plan. If the relevant licensing body also lists that exact course as an accepted education component, the editor can add that limited use and identify the body. Completion alone still gives the employer no basis to present the employee as licensed.
