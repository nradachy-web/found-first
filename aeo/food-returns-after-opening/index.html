Scoping AEO for specialty food returns after opening

For a specialty food store, an AEO brief on returns should settle what the shop means by “opened.” A delivery carton, a gift sleeve and a sealed food pouch can each have a different bearing on whether the buyer can send an item back.

Identify the seal your policy refers to

Ask the content team to read the returns policy alongside a photo of the packaging buyers receive. If the rule refers to the pouch seal, name it and explain whether removing the outer wrap matters. A generic warning about opened packages can discourage a valid request before the buyer even checks the contents.

Give packing errors their own answer

A customer who received the wrong flavor has a different reason for contacting the store from someone who changed their mind. Use the shop's actual instructions for a packing error, including which label to photograph and whether the customer should wait for a reply before shipping food back.

The carton is open, the tea is still sealed

Imagine a tea merchant whose written policy accepts unopened pouches for preference returns. Its packing checklist records the blend by label. A shopper opens the mailer and decides the sealed smoky tea was a poor gift choice. The page can direct them to the preference return request. If the invoice shows mint and the pouch says smoky tea, the same shopper should send the invoice reference and a label photo to the packing team. Seal condition alone cannot decide that case.
