AEO for freight providers quoting deliveries without a loading dock

If your freight company offers liftgate delivery, use AEO to explain where a pallet will be left at a commercial site without a loading dock. Naming the equipment answers only part of the shipper’s question. The quote also depends on the load, the receiving surface and the requested handoff.

Follow the pallet to its agreed stopping point

Have the service page distinguish lowering freight from moving it beyond the truck. Describe the unloading and placement services your team actually offers. A liftgate listing alone gives a facilities buyer little basis for assuming a driver will move freight across a yard or through a doorway.

Ask for details that change dispatch

Choose intake details that your dispatcher uses to assess fit, such as packaged dimensions, weight, ground conditions and the available approach. Place them next to the answer about sites without a dock. That gives a shipper something concrete to confirm with the consignee before submitting the request.

A crate for a branch office

Suppose a carrier’s equipment sheet and delivery terms support unloading a suitable crate onto a paved receiving area. A branch office sketch shows that the final destination is a room beyond a gravel path. The AEO writer illustrates two handoffs. If the branch accepts the crate on the pavement and arranges onward handling, dispatch can assess the liftgate request. If the order requires placement in the room, sales needs to assess a separate placement service or decline that leg. The equipment sheet supports the first answer only after the crate details check out.
