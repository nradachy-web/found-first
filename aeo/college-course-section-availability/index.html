College catalog AEO for courses without scheduled sections

If students keep asking for a course that appears in your college catalog, scope the AEO work around the term schedule. A catalog entry can explain what the course covers. The registrar’s section listing should answer whether someone can take it this term, in the format they need.

What can a department page safely say?

Keep the course description and its usual place in the curriculum on the department page. Give the term, campus and delivery format with any claim about a particular section. If registration status changes throughout the day, send readers to the section record for that answer. Copying a seat count into an article creates another number someone has to maintain.

Test the individual course before expanding the work

Give a prospective agency a catalog entry and the corresponding schedule. Ask for a revised answer to “Can I take this online in the fall?” Someone reading it should be able to decide whether to open registration, check a different format or ask about future offerings. Google applies ordinary search requirements to its AI features. An agency proposing special markup for AI inclusion should still be able to answer that scheduling question from the college’s own records.

A lab in the catalog, an evening section on the schedule

Suppose a college catalog describes a water quality lab and an old department flyer promotes online study. The fall schedule lists an evening lab on campus. The editor can point to that section and identify its format. If the registrar removes the section before enrollment opens, the course description still belongs in the catalog. The fall answer changes to “No fall section is currently listed,” with future scheduling questions directed to the department.
