GEO for architects pursuing occupied renovation work

To market your architecture practice for occupied renovations, show the part of the design that made continued use possible. A GEO page aimed at owners keeping a building open needs evidence of an operating constraint and your team's response to it.

Look for a drawing that changes between phases

A finished photograph can show the new entrance but tell the reader little about getting into the building during construction. Retrieve an approved phasing plan, client brief or design-stage circulation diagram. Identify the space that had to remain accessible, what the client needed to keep doing there and what changed in the design as a result.

Credit the plan at the right level

Compare your drawings with the contractor's sequencing documents. A practice can explain how it designed around a temporary access route even when the contractor developed the detailed sequence. If the architect first learned about the route from site photographs, look for another example to establish design experience with occupied premises.

A library that kept lending during the work

Imagine a library board gives your practice permission to show a diagram. Its brief calls for continued lending, and your drawing locates a temporary returns desk beside an entrance retained during the lobby works. Annotate the diagram with that design choice and the board's requirement. If the building closed throughout construction and the desk was only an unused option, label the diagram as a phasing study. For a claim about continuous operation, choose a commission where the client can confirm what happened.
