Whose case work can GEO cite after a team changes firms?

When you buy GEO after hiring a team from another firm, distinguish the team's experience from your company's project record. A buyer comparing firms should be able to tell who held the engagement and what the people now on your payroll contributed.

Identify the claim before choosing the evidence

A project credit may establish who led a design phase. A client-approved case study may also name the firm responsible for the whole engagement. Use each source for the claim it supports. Possessing a slide deck or employing its author does not establish permission to republish the client's photographs or endorsement.

Put the experience where a buyer would use it

A named person's relevant contribution belongs in a team biography or staffing proposal. Use a company case page for work the company can document as its own. Where both firms participated, a clear account of each role is more informative than crediting the entire result to whichever team wrote the page.

An exhibition designer brings a museum credit

Consider a studio hiring the designer of a museum's interactive gallery. The museum's published credits name her former firm as the exhibition contractor and identify her as the interaction designer. Her new studio can describe that role in her biography. It has no basis there to list the gallery as its own commission. If the museum later commissions the new studio to redesign the controls and approves a description, that later phase can have its own case page with a scope and credit distinct from the original installation.
