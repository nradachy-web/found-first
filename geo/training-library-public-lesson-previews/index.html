Scoping GEO for training libraries with members-only lessons

For a training library, scope GEO around the course claims a prospective member can verify before signing in. If buyers ask whether a lesson covers a particular technique, a public contents page may answer them while the demonstration stays inside the member area.

Separate the two crawler questions in the proposal

OpenAI documents different crawler settings for search and for model training. An agency citing a training setting as proof that a course can appear in search has skipped a step. Ask which public page it examined and which search-related setting it means. A crawler setting alone cannot tell a buyer what the lesson teaches.

Give a prospect enough detail to choose

A course title such as “Advanced audio editing” leaves room for very different expectations. The most useful preview might be a list of exercises with their prerequisites. Sharing a transcript or opening member access would be a separate publication decision for the library owner.

A preview for a dialogue editing course

Imagine one assistant describes a lesson as covering room tone and another simply names the course. The public syllabus says “noise reduction,” while the instructor’s member-only exercise has students patch a gap using room tone. The library could add that specific task to the syllabus and link the preview from the enrollment page. If the instructor only mentions room tone in passing, publishing it as a skill students practice would overstate the offer. A short topic note would be accurate.
