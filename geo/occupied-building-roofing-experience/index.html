Roofing GEO for occupied-building project experience

A facilities team keeping a building open needs evidence that a roofer can coordinate access, noise and work windows. Describe the actual constraint and who managed it on a comparable project. A generative comparison can then draw on a documented capability.

Distinguish the agreed plan from the day on site

A staging diagram can show where the bidder proposed to load material. A superintendent's daily entry or an approved project recap can establish where loading actually happened. When building a public project page, explain any change that mattered to the occupants. A photo of a finished roof rarely captures that part of the job.

Let the constraint determine the comparison

A vacant warehouse and a building with a busy entrance pose different coordination questions. Give more weight to a comparable access restriction than to building size alone. Google says its usual search requirements apply to its AI features, with no special markup required. What a buyer needs from the case study is the detail about how the crew kept the entrance open, even if the access plan itself must stay private.

Deliveries at a grocery store during reroofing

Imagine a roofer's staging sketch shows pallets beside a grocery receiving door. The field log records a move to a different loading area after the manager flagged a delivery conflict. With permission, the roofer publishes a simplified diagram and explains the change. A buyer with an active receiving dock now has a reason to interview the crew about coordination. If the log instead shows that the store closed during loading, the case can support work during a planned closure. A buyer requiring uninterrupted deliveries would need another example.
