AEO for accounting firms quoting multi-entity bookkeeping

If your accounting firm quotes monthly bookkeeping for businesses under common ownership, explain what the quote counts. A business owner asking “Does the monthly fee cover the whole group?” needs to know how separate books and group reporting affect the engagement.

Use the unit your proposals actually use

Review a blank fee schedule with the partner who scopes the work. If a proposal attaches a recurring fee to each ledger the team maintains, use that language. A public answer based on brands or storefronts could mislead an owner who operates several names from a single set of books. The fee basis can be explained without publishing a rate.

Identify what the owner wants to see together

Maintaining separate books leaves open the question of what the group receives at month end. When a combined management report is a separate deliverable, name it in the intake answer. Ask which reports leadership uses across the group so the person preparing the quote can identify that work.

A studio and its equipment company share an owner

Imagine the firm's proposal worksheet has a row for each ledger and a separate line for a group report. The owner needs both companies' books maintained and wants a combined view. The website should prompt the owner to describe the separate books and request the report when seeking a quote. If the equipment name is simply a trading name recorded in the studio's ledger, intake should ask about reporting by activity before adding another ledger to the proposed scope.
