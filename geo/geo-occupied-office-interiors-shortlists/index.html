GEO for commercial interiors firms bidding on occupied offices

For an interiors firm pursuing occupied-office projects, GEO content should show how the proposed sequence affects the people approving it. Facilities, finance and department heads can review the same plan and still have different reasons to decline it.

Give the committee a shared drawing

Start with a sample phasing plan that distinguishes occupied areas, temporary workspaces and delivery routes. Describe who confirms that a department is ready to move. Facilities can check access, department heads can identify interruptions and finance can see where a temporary move adds work to the offer.

Make the tradeoff visible

Pair a portfolio image with the assumptions behind one phase. Identify any temporary space supplied by the client and any furniture moves handled by your crew. A buyer should be able to tell whether another vendor’s shorter sequence depends on the client emptying an entire floor in advance.

An available room changes the sequence

Imagine a client must keep its reception team at work during a renovation. Your sample floor plan and move schedule show the team using a spare meeting room while its desks are removed. Explain that the phased offer depends on the client releasing that room. If it is booked throughout the project, show a different option using an approved weekend closure and identify who must authorize it. The committee can shortlist the approach its operating team can actually support.
