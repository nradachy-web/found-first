Testing before-and-after photos in ChatGPT ads for restoration

Use a before-and-after photo in a sponsored restoration message when a buyer can tell what changed and which work produced the change. Choose a process photo if the finished image owes much of its appeal to work outside the advertised service.

Account for the visible differences

Read the work order beside the original photo files and confirm permission for advertising use. Check the finish, lighting, camera angle and any replacement components. A freshly fitted part can dominate a photo of an otherwise careful repair. A short caption may identify it, but the useful test is whether the pictured change is still legible at the size buyers will see.

Compare two ways to explain the repair

Pair the before-and-after treatment and the process treatment with the same description of the work. Look at the jobs prospects ask about afterward. An image that prompts inquiries about an upgrade the shop rarely performs may be showing the wrong part of a successful job.

What changed on the radio cabinet?

Suppose a vintage radio restorer has a paired photograph and an itemized job sheet. The cabinet received veneer repair, new grille cloth and polished knobs. For a veneer repair ad, crop the pair to the same damaged corner so buyers can see the repaired seam. If the original shot does not show that corner clearly, use a bench photo of the loose veneer being reattached. Reserve the finished cabinet image for an offer that includes the broader restoration.
