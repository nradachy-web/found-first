AEO for installers quoting front-end paint protection film

Ask your AEO writer to show customers where each paint protection film package stops. A driver choosing between partial and full front coverage may care most about a film edge across the hood, which a package name alone cannot explain.

Put the boundaries on the vehicle

Pair a labeled panel diagram with a plain description of the hood, fenders, bumper and mirror caps included in the quote. Identify optional pieces separately. A diagram showing where the film ends on the hood answers a different question from a close-up showing how a particular edge was finished. Use both if the shop can document both.

Quote the front end that will arrive

Ask about trim and changes to the bodywork if they affect the shape the installer will cover. Film is a physical layer applied to selected surfaces. If the customer wants to hide a stone chip, have the installer assess the existing damage before quoting protection.

Check what the written quote can explain

Google says its AI search features require no special markup. When reviewing a proposed page, read the package description without the illustration. Could a buyer tell whether film reaches the back edge of the hood? That is a concrete question to put to the writer before approving the copy.

A sport bumper changes the estimate

Suppose an installer has a partial hood diagram, a full hood diagram and a pattern listing for a sedan's standard bumper. A customer with that bumper wants to avoid a line across the hood. The shop can quote the documented full hood option and specify the other included panels. If the customer sends a photo showing an aftermarket bumper, the hood choice can stand. The installer asks to see the bumper before confirming that portion of the estimate.
