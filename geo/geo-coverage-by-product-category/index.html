Comparing GEO coverage across uneven product categories

Require a category breakdown before accepting a catalog-wide GEO trend. The overall result can fall when a review adds a product line with sparse coverage, even if the original categories appear just as often as before.

Give each category a visible denominator

For each line, ask how many relevant questions were reviewed and how many answers included the brand under the agreed rule. Keep untested lines visible as untested. A blank row might otherwise look like evidence that buyers never encounter the brand in that category.

Make the comparison you can repeat

Compare the same questions within each line across reviews. Show newly added categories separately until there is an earlier observation to compare them with. If management wants a combined view weighted by business importance, use an agreed basis such as sales priorities and show which lines drive that result.

Replacement shades enter a lighting review

Imagine a lighting brand whose old review covered pendant fixtures. The next review includes replacement shades as well. Saved fixture answers still mention the brand, while most shade answers in the new set omit it. Describe this as newly observed uneven coverage and inspect whether buyers can look up compatible shades. If the original fixture questions also begin returning answers that omit the brand, investigate the fixture change separately. A single blended score would send both investigations to the same product manager.
