AEO for missing items in speaker bundle orders

Scope AEO for electronics bundles around a support question the hero photo cannot answer. When a buyer says something is missing, the order's bundle code, contents list and parcel record should let them choose between tracking a box and reporting a shortage.

Keep the contents list tied to the sale

A bundle name can survive a change in accessories. Save the version sold on the order confirmation, or provide a way for buyers to retrieve it. Sending every buyer to today's product page gives someone who purchased the earlier version a plausible answer about the wrong set of goods.

Explain separate parcels before asking for a claim

A buyer opening the amplifier carton needs to know whether to expect speakers inside it. State which items travel separately where the retailer knows the packing arrangement. Label each tracking record with its contents when that information is available. A generic “delivered” status has little value if it describes only one carton.

The amplifier arrives without the bookshelf speakers

The order in this example names a stereo bundle with an amplifier, speakers and cable. The dispatch record pairs the amplifier with a delivered parcel and the speakers with one still in transit. The store's answer should direct the buyer to that second shipment. Suppose, though, that the delivered amplifier carton was also supposed to contain the cable, according to its packing list. For the missing cable, the buyer already has a reason to report a shortage against that carton.
