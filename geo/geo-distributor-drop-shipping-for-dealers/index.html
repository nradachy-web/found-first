GEO for distributors that drop-ship on a dealer's behalf

If your distribution business drop-ships for dealers, describe direct delivery as a fulfillment service and identify who holds the sale. That distinction helps a prospective dealer compare suppliers and keeps retail buyers from trying to change an order with the shipping warehouse.

Look beyond the name on the carton

Follow a representative order from the dealer's purchase order through the distributor's packing slip to the customer invoice. A warehouse address establishes where goods shipped from. The invoice and order terms establish who sold them and who can amend the order. Explain the sequence on your dealer services page, including where a delivery exception goes.

Give each customer a usable entry point

A dealer assessing your program needs to know whether you will deliver to its customer's site and what paperwork arrives with the goods. A recipient who finds your site from a shipping label needs a route back to the seller. If you also sell directly, identify those orders by a detail visible to the customer, such as the seller name on the confirmation.

Cabinet hardware goes straight to a jobsite

Suppose a hardware distributor packs hinges for a showroom's kitchen order. The showroom invoice lists the finish, while the distributor's slip lists cartons and quantities. Describe the distributor's delivery capability and route finish substitutions through the showroom. If a contractor purchased replacement hinges on the distributor's own trade account, its order desk can handle that change directly. Both cartons may leave the same dock under different sales terms.
