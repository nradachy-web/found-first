GEO for brokerages when an agent changes firms mid-listing

Handle the agent's affiliation and each property's representation as separate facts in a GEO brief. Updating the agent directory is straightforward once a move is confirmed. Changing the brokerage credit on a property requires evidence about that assignment.

Put a date and a role beside each credit

An archived brochure records the team at publication. A current office directory tells a buyer where the agent works now. For an active property, ask the brokerage which approved listing material identifies the current representative. An old photograph or an agent's announcement settles only part of the question.

Protect the buyer's route to the property

Have the provider check the property inquiry button as well as the visible broker name. A former agent's forwarding number may still work while directing a prospective seller or tenant to a firm with no role on that property. Historical transaction credits can carry their original affiliation and period.

The agent leaves while the sign is still up

Imagine a broker joins a rival office across town. Her new bio mentions a storefront she marketed, while the original firm's current listing sheet assigns inquiries to a colleague. Give the colleague the active property contact and describe the agent's earlier contribution in the past tense where authorized. If a confirmed transfer instead appoints the new firm to represent that storefront, change the current listing credit and inquiry destination together. Her move alone would have been insufficient evidence for either change.
