AEO for multi-location brands

A brand with many locations has two jobs in AI search: keep the shared facts identical everywhere they are published, and make the facts that differ by location easy to find. That takes one maintained record of location facts, pages that say something true only of each location, and reporting by location. No one can guarantee which location an assistant names, or that it names one at all.

The buyer already knows the brand

Someone asking about a brand with many locations is rarely asking who you are. They are asking which one, whether it is open and whether it does the thing they need. A wrong answer here does not lose a comparison. It sends a customer to a closed door or the wrong branch.

Questions to test, with your own brand and places in the brackets:

  • Which [brand] near [neighborhood] offers [service]?
  • Is the [brand] in [city] open on [holiday or late hour]?
  • Does the [brand] at [street or shopping center] take [payment type, plan or membership]?
  • How do I book [service] at the [location name] [brand]?
  • Is [brand] or [competitor] closer to [place], and which one has [feature]?

Sort every fact into brand or location

Inconsistency starts when nobody has decided who owns a fact. Decide once, in writing.

How a location fact stays right

Governance sounds heavy. In practice it is a record, an owner and a trigger, run in this order.

  1. Build the record

    One row per location and one column per fact: name, address, phone, hours, services, booking link, manager. Add who confirmed each fact and when.

  2. Compare it with what is published

    Check each location's page, its business profile and its main listings against the record. Note every mismatch and where the wrong version lives.

  3. Fix it at the source

    Correct the record first, then the website, then each profile and listing. A fix made only on the website leaves the old fact published everywhere else.

  4. Name the triggers

    An opening, a closing, a move, new hours, a new manager or a dropped service each start the same update. Without a trigger the record goes stale.

  5. Sample and check again

    Each cycle, test a set of locations against real customer questions and against the record. An error that repeats points to a broken step, not a careless location.

What a location page needs that the template cannot supply

Use one template so every page is consistent, then require content that is true only of that location. The test: if you swapped in another city's name and nothing on the page became wrong, the page has nothing of its own.

Google's spam policies name pages targeted at specific cities that funnel users to one page, and many pages generated without adding value for users.

  • How to get thereAddress, entrance, parking, transit and landmarks, written for someone arriving for the first time.
  • What this location doesThe services available here, and a plain note of any that are not.
  • When it is openRegular and special hours that match the business profile.
  • How to reach this locationIts own phone number and booking path, not the brand's general form.
  • Who works hereThe manager or lead practitioner, where they agree to be named.
  • What it looks likePhotos taken at this location, not stock or head office images.
  • What its customers sayReviews of this location, gathered without incentives, and the location's replies.
  • Markup that matchesLocalBusiness structured data for this location that says the same thing as the visible text.

Google asks that structured data match the visible page and that each location be defined as its own LocalBusiness. Checked November 11, 2026.

Measure by location, then add up

A network average can look healthy while one location publishes the wrong phone number. Report each measure by location first and total it second.

The reporting method is covered in how to measure AI marketing. What is specific to a network is the unit, which is the location. For each one:

  • Accuracy: for each sampled location, whether the answers gave the right address, hours and services. This is the measure a brand can act on fastest.
  • Mentions: which locations are named for which questions, with the date and the sample. A sample of locations describes that sample, not the whole network.
  • Search: Google counts visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode inside overall Web search traffic in Search Console, so a location page's numbers cannot be split into AI and ordinary search.
  • Inquiries: calls and bookings for each location, from the phone and booking systems that location already uses.

Questions from brands with many locations

Can every location use the same page template?+

Yes. A shared layout keeps the network consistent. The content inside it still has to reflect that location's own facts: its services, hours, access, people and reviews.

Where should a large network begin?+

With the record of location facts and a representative set of locations. Fix the errors that repeat across that set, correct the step that produced them, then extend the same checks to the rest of the network.

In a franchise, who should own location facts?+

Split them in writing. The brand sets the name, category, service definitions and policies. The location owner confirms the hours, phone number, services offered and staff, on a schedule the brand checks.

How many locations do you test?+

A sample agreed with you in advance, chosen to cover your different markets and formats. Results describe the locations and questions in that sample and should not be read as a score for every location.

What if an assistant gives the wrong hours for one of our locations?+

Find where the wrong fact is published before changing anything else. Check that location's page, its business profile and its main listings against your record, correct the source, and test the same question again later. The fix is in the published facts, and we cannot promise when or whether an answer changes.

Can you guarantee the right location is named?+

No. We improve the accuracy and consistency of what each location publishes and report what we observe. Which business or location an answer names is the platform's decision.
