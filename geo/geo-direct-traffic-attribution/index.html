Can a catalog brand credit direct traffic growth to GEO?

Keep direct traffic growth unassigned unless you have evidence linking a visit or inquiry to AI discovery. A rise after a favorable GEO comparison gives you a period to investigate, but the timing alone cannot tell you how visitors found the site.

Follow the destination as well as the date

Look at the pages where direct visits began. A trade login, a catalog landing page and a specification sheet suggest different reasons to visit. Check print distribution and customer communications for the same destinations. This can identify plausible explanations even when the source of an individual visit remains unknown.

Credit only the part you can trace

Keep a buyer's account with the corresponding inquiry. To attribute a particular visit, you also need evidence connecting that buyer to the visit. Transferring the explanation from one known inquiry to every unassigned visit turns a useful sales note into an unsupported traffic estimate.

The swatch book arrives the week the brand gets mentioned

Suppose a textile supplier appears in an AI answer about washable upholstery. Direct visits also rise that week. Its mailed swatch book prints the same short address where many visits began, so the owner leaves the increase unassigned. If an architect forwards the answer with a sample request and explains that they typed the address from it, record AI discovery for that inquiry. The evidence changes the sample request's history while the source of the wider increase stays open.
