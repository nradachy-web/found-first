Separating new subscriptions from renewals in ChatGPT ads sales

If you are buying ChatGPT ads to acquire subscribers, count the first paid subscription for each new customer. Track restarts and scheduled renewals separately, since each tells you something different about why the payment arrived.

Read the subscription history behind the invoice

Check the service start date, prior paid terms and billing trigger in your own records. A first invoice in a new billing system can belong to a longstanding subscriber. A replacement account after an email change can do the same. When earlier records are unavailable, mark customer history unknown until you can establish whether the buyer is new.

Define what earns win-back credit

A former subscriber who actively signs up again is a candidate for a win-back measure if the restart can be linked to the campaign. An automatic renewal was already scheduled. Its revenue can matter when evaluating the long-term value of customers you originally acquired, but counting every renewal as another newly acquired customer inflates acquisition volume.

The subscriber who clicked before billing day

Take a trade journal comparing a campaign report with its circulation ledger. A reader visited the offer page shortly before an annual payment. The old invoice and renewal schedule show that billing continued automatically, so the editor counts renewal revenue and leaves the acquisition tally alone. If the earlier subscription had expired and the reader used the campaign offer to place a fresh paid order, the editor credits a restart and evaluates it against the journal's win-back budget.
