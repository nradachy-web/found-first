AEO for reorders from last season's food catalog

When buyers reorder from a printed food catalog, retain an answer they can find by the old item name or code. Your AEO decision is where that lookup should take them when the gift on the current order form differs from the one they remember.

Distinguish a seasonal pause from a replacement

Check the retired order form against the buyer's assortment plan. If the same gift is due back and the team can substantiate that, explain the seasonal pause. If a new gift has inherited the name, describe the change before linking to it. A familiar photograph can obscure a different pack size or an ingredient the recipient would avoid.

Keep the old catalog entry useful

Google says ordinary search requirements apply to its AI features. For this job, commission a readable answer on the old gift entry that names the edition and its current order status. A redirect straight to the seasonal collection loses the detail a returning buyer used to identify the gift.

A pear gift box with a familiar cover photo

A hypothetical orchard's winter catalog pairs pears with plain preserves. The new packing list keeps the pears but replaces the preserves with a walnut spread. A lookup using the old gift code should explain that the original assortment has ended and identify the walnut spread before offering the successor. If the purchasing sheet instead confirms another run of the original box, the same lookup can lead to the item's seasonal availability notice.
