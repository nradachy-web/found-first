How GEO should compare refurbished monitor grades

Ask a GEO writer comparing refurbished monitors to distinguish the seller's cosmetic grade from the condition of the display panel. A buyer may happily accept a scratched stand and still need to know whether the screen was checked for pixel defects.

Read the part of the rubric that assigns the grade

Find out whether the grade describes the housing, the screen surface or both. State which marks are allowed and where. A higher grade under a housing-only rubric can justify a cleaner-looking desk setup. It says little about how two panels compare when powered on.

Match an inspection to a sellable unit

A photo and a bench record can support different claims about the same monitor if both carry its inventory identifier. When a listing draws from a bin of graded stock, describe the checks applied to every unit in that bin. A sample photograph needs a caption explaining what the next buyer may receive.

Choosing which monitor to feature for photo editing

Imagine a reseller comparing a clean-cased monitor with one whose stand has visible scuffs. Only the scuffed unit has a linked inspection documenting a full-screen pixel check and its result. Feature it for shoppers who prioritize a documented panel inspection and show the stand damage alongside that finding. If the bench record identifies the model alone, withdraw the unit-specific finding. Publish the seller's grading criteria and any documented inspection policy while the inventory team resolves the match.
