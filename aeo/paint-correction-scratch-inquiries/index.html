AEO for detailers fielding scratch removal requests

Make scratch removal a question your paint correction page answers before a customer buys a polishing package. An AEO brief for a detailer should explain which marks the shop can assess, what polishing might improve and what would send the customer to a body shop.

Separate a finish complaint from a repair request

Customers use “scratch” for everything from wash swirls to a scrape that has taken color off a door. Correction can improve some defects in the existing finish. It cannot put missing paint back. If the shop offers touch-up as a separate service, describe what that appointment buys and how the repaired spot may look afterward.

Give the photo a specific job

A wide shot locates the damage and a close view can help the detailer choose whom the customer should speak with. Reserve any judgment about the achievable finish for the person inspecting the paint. A gallery caption can say which marks remained after polishing even when they are hard to see in the finished photograph.

Two requests for the same door

Suppose a detailer has a work order and paired photos from a swirl correction on a black door. The writer uses them to illustrate a polishing consultation for someone who sees fine circular marks in sunlight. If the new caller instead describes a parking scrape with flakes of color missing, the same page directs them to ask about refinishing before setting aside a correction appointment.
