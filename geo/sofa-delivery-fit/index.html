GEO for sofa brands comparing doorway fit

If delivery access influences which sofa your furniture brand recommends, commission a GEO comparison of how each model enters the home. A shopper may have room for either sofa and still need a different model because of the turn off the landing.

Measure the piece that crosses the threshold

The dimension drawing usually shows the sofa as it will stand in the room. Find the packing sheet and delivery instructions as well. For a sectional, identify the largest rigid module. For a one-piece sofa, record which legs or arms the delivery crew can remove and the dimensions in that configuration.

Keep a straight opening separate from a turn

A carton width can settle a question about a straight passage when the other clearances are known. A narrow landing introduces a different question about turning space. Give readers the diagram or measurement request a delivery specialist would use before confirming that route.

Two sofas for the same upstairs room

Suppose a retailer has two sofas of equal assembled width. The split-frame model's packing sheet shows two short cartons. The other sofa travels as one long piece. Using a buyer's measured landing sketch, delivery staff confirm the short cartons can turn there, so the comparison can recommend the split frame for that route. If the buyer supplies only a doorway width, the writer can still explain the packaging difference and ask for the missing landing depth before drawing a fit conclusion.
