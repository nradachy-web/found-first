Which AEO answers belong in an accordion?

Keep the part that determines whether a buyer can use your offer visible before they open an accordion. Expandable answers work well for follow-up questions once the reader has enough information to decide whether to keep reading.

Read the page with every panel closed

Start with the promise in the heading and the action on the button. If a buyer could reasonably take that action and discover a disqualifying condition in a closed panel, revise the promise. A panel headed “What should I bring?” can still hold a detailed packing list. A heading like “Important information” gives a reader little basis for opening it.

Test the edit and the control separately

Ask a reviewer to describe the offer from the closed page, then have them open the answer using a keyboard. An accurate summary and a usable control solve different problems. Google describes ordinary Search requirements for its AI features. Accordion styling alone gives a team no basis for predicting inclusion.

An archive that requires an appointment

Suppose an archive has a “Visit the reading room” page, a visitor handbook and an appointment calendar. The handbook says all visitors need a confirmed booking. Put that sentence beside the calendar link and leave instructions for storing bags in the accordion. If only visitors requesting rare materials need appointments, change the visible line to name that collection. Someone consulting the general shelves can then follow the walk-in instructions.
