When a software demo needs more than a transcript for GEO

For a software team investing in GEO, transcribe the narration and then review what the presenter demonstrates silently. A transcript is enough when the speaker states the condition, action and result. Add a short written walkthrough where the interface supplies a missing part of the answer.

Listen once with the screen out of view

Mark any point where a phrase like “this option here” carries the explanation. Play that section again and record the control’s visible name, the state it starts in and what changes after the click. A time reference lets a prospect verify the passage without watching the entire demonstration.

Scope the evidence to the demonstrated account

A feature shown to an administrator may look different to a staff user. If the recording does not establish staff access, use the account role shown and check the permissions guide before extending the claim. This gives a reviewer a precise correction to make when the interface changes.

Changing one appointment in a series

Imagine a scheduling vendor whose presenter says “we’ll move this one” and silently selects “this and following” before changing the time. The transcript makes a single appointment sound affected. A written explanation beside the clip can name the selected scope and describe the later appointments that move on screen. If a newer recording actually selects “this occurrence” and the later times stay put, use that clip to document a single exception. The two recordings answer different procurement questions.
