AEO for physical therapy clinics offering video appointments

Before commissioning an AEO page about online physical therapy appointments, check what the word “online” describes. Booking from a phone, completing a form at home and attending a scheduled video visit are three different things a clinic website can offer.

Look for the meeting format in the confirmation

Start with the scheduler’s appointment names and the instructions a visitor receives after reserving a time. Directions to a building indicate that online booking may end in an office visit. A video appointment description should identify the appointment type the clinic offers remotely and how the office communicates the joining instructions.

Give the booking button a specific verb

A button labeled “Start online” could launch paperwork or request a meeting. Use the destination to choose wording such as “Complete intake forms” or “Request a video appointment.” If existing clients arrange remote visits through their usual desk, include that route so they can act without starting a new intake.

A virtual welcome packet precedes an office visit

Imagine a clinic with a PDF called “Virtual intake” and a first-visit confirmation that includes parking instructions. Its scheduler offers video only for a separately listed follow-up appointment. An editor reviewing these materials keeps the welcome packet under first-visit paperwork and limits the video answer to the follow-up option. If the clinic opens a remote first-visit appointment with its own booking entry, the editor can add that choice as soon as the scheduler and confirmation both reflect it.
