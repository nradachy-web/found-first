Quoting work with buyer-owned tooling in AEO content

A manufacturer seeking transfer work should use AEO to explain when it will quote production using a buyer's existing tooling. Answering “Can you use our mold?” starts with identifying the tool and ends with a clear statement of what your team still needs to verify.

Give a buyer inheriting the job a starting point

A new purchasing manager may have a drawing and a tool list from the previous supplier. Name the records that help your estimator recognize the asset, such as a tool ID, the related part revision and the last available condition report. Explain where the buyer can share those records privately.

Define the scope of the first response

A published answer can distinguish a records review from physical acceptance of the tool. If your production proposal depends on receiving and assessing the asset, say how that condition will appear on the quote. A buyer weighing a supplier transfer can then plan for the decision that follows delivery.

A familiar part number on an unfamiliar mold

In a hypothetical transfer, the buyer's tool register lists two molds for one cover. The condition report identifies the retired mold, while a shipping photo shows the ID of the other. The estimator requests records for the mold being shipped and offers to review that asset on receipt. If the report and photo identify the same active mold, the estimator can prepare a preliminary production quote with the outstanding physical review stated explicitly.
