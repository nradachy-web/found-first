AEO for grading contractors quoting off-site hauling

A grading contractor should answer “Does your estimate include hauling the surplus away?” with the endpoint of the quoted work. Ask an AEO writer to distinguish material moved to an on-site stockpile from material your company has agreed to deliver elsewhere.

Follow a load to its quoted destination

Look at the grading narrative and the estimate inclusions together. If the crew's quoted work ends at a designated stockpile, say who arranges collection from there. When your company also offers export, explain what must be known about the receiving location before the estimator can include that service. The presence of trucks in a project photo settles neither question.

Make missing information visible in the answer

A useful estimate example can show an open item such as “receiving location to be supplied by owner.” Preserve the effect of that note in public copy. Calling the job a complete site package while burying the destination assumption makes it hard for a developer to brief the next bidder.

One grading plan, two destinations

Imagine a site contractor reviewing a developer's plan with a stockpile area marked beside a future building pad. The proposal includes moving excavated material to that area. The service page could use a redrawn plan excerpt to show where the crew's assignment ends. If the developer removes the stockpile area and provides a receiving location, the estimator can review a haul scope. Until then, the answer should ask where the material is to go before describing off-site hauling as included.
