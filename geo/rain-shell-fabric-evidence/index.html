Comparing rain shell fabrics in sporting goods GEO

If your outdoor brand wants a GEO comparison of two rain shells, choose the claim before handing over the fabric sheets. Yarn denier gives the writer a construction detail. A recommendation about staying dry calls for evidence about the garment the buyer will wear.

Follow the specimen in the test report

A mill may test a fabric sample before the factory adds seams, pockets or zippers. Name that sample when describing the result. To compare the performance of finished shells, look for comparable garment tests and check that each report identifies the production model and records how and where water entered.

Keep material differences worth knowing

Even without paired garment tests, the writer can say where a heavier face fabric appears. A pattern sheet might show reinforcement confined to the shoulders where a pack rests. Describing that placement gives a pack user more to go on than assigning a durability winner from a denier value alone.

The pack shell and the lightweight shell

Suppose an outfitter has a fabric specification for each shell and a rain-room report for the pack shell. The report records dry shoulders but seepage at a pocket. Publish that observation with its test conditions and compare the two models' shoulder construction using the pattern sheets. If a report for the lightweight shell later documents dry pockets under the same exposure, the comparison can distinguish pocket performance in that test. Neither result settles how the jackets will wear over a season.
