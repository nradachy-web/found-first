AEO for nonprofits offering course scholarships

A nonprofit offering course scholarships should use AEO to explain the order of the scholarship and admission decisions. Put the answer where applicants first encounter the award, especially when a partner school controls enrollment.

Which application comes first?

Trace the required documents back to their issuers. If a scholarship application needs a school admission letter, give that requirement before sending readers into the award form. If the fund accepts applications from people still awaiting admission, spell out what they can submit now and what they must provide later. An invitation to apply says little about whether either decision is final.

Look for the handoff between organizations

A school can answer questions about an available place. The fund administrator can explain award review and the scope of any support offered. Ask the editor to identify where a reader can check each decision and which office handles a change of course or term. This is particularly important when both organizations use the word “accepted” in their notices.

A seat in the workshop, a separate scholarship review

Imagine an arts nonprofit whose award form requires an admission letter for a partner studio’s printmaking workshop. The studio’s letter confirms a place and says nothing about the award. The public answer should send an admitted learner to the nonprofit with the letter and direct funding questions to its program staff. If the next application cycle permits a portfolio before admission, the first step changes. A learner can submit the portfolio for consideration while pursuing a studio place separately.
